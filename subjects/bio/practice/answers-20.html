(1) [5]
A potato strip initially has mass 8.5 g. After immersion in 10% sucrose solution it has mass 7.0 g. Calculate percentage mass change and explain the direction of water movement through the cell membrane. Assume no tissue loss.
Worked solution / model answer
Mass change=-1.5 g; percentage=-17.65%. Water moves out by exosmosis into the more concentrated external solution; cells lose turgor and may plasmolyse. Not without qualification: boiling disrupts living membranes; the selective barrier assumed in the model may no longer function.
(2) [5]
Two similar leafy shoots lose 9.9 g and 4.95 g in 40 minutes. The second has petroleum jelly on both leaf surfaces. Explain the lower loss, identify two variables to control, and state one function of transpiration.
Worked solution / model answer
Jelly restricts stomatal/cuticular water loss; coating the more stomata-rich lower surface particularly reduces transpiration. Control leaf area, time, temperature, humidity and air movement (any two). Transpiration supports water/mineral transport and evaporative cooling.
(3) [5]
Assume independent assortment and complete dominance for two pea traits. Two double-heterozygous parents are crossed. Give the F₂ phenotype ratio and expected numbers in each category among 1120 plants. Do not calculate all dihybrid genotypes.
Worked solution / model answer

Write the parental genotypes, list gametes and combine one allele from each parent. Count genotype and phenotype classes separately.

Phenotypes are 9:3:3:1. Expected categories: both dominant=630; first dominant/second recessive=210; first recessive/second dominant=210; both recessive=70. This phenotype calculation assumes independent assortment.
(4) [5]
12 diploid body cells, each containing 12 chromosomes, undergo 1 complete rounds of mitosis. Find the number of daughter cells and chromosomes per daughter cell. Explain the role of mitosis in tissue growth.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify the stage from the chromosome behaviour. Count chromosomes by centromeres; after DNA replication a metaphase chromosome has two chromatids.

Cells=12×2^1=24. Every daughter cell has 12 chromosomes. Mitosis preserves chromosome number and produces genetically similar cells for growth/repair.
(5) [5]
A heart beats 90 times per minute with stroke volume 68 mL. A blood marker is at lungs. State the next major route it follows, the oxygenation change across the relevant capillary bed, and why valves are needed. Calculate cardiac output in L/min.
Worked solution / model answer
Route: pulmonary veins → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body. Pulmonary capillaries oxygenate blood; systemic capillaries supply tissues and collect CO₂. Valves maintain one-way flow by preventing backflow. Arteries are named by direction away from the heart, not oxygen content. Cardiac output=heart rate×stroke volume=90×68=6120 mL/min=6.12 L/min.

3 [10]
A potato strip increases from 24 g to 30 g in dilute solution. An identical strip loses mass in concentrated sugar solution. Both are blotted before weighing.
(1) [1]
Name the main process causing the changes.
Worked solution / model answer
Osmosis.
(2) [2]
Explain the first strip’s mass gain.
Worked solution / model answer
Water enters cells from the more dilute external solution across selectively permeable membranes, down the water-potential gradient.
(3) [2]
Describe water movement and the cell-state change in concentrated solution.
Worked solution / model answer
Water leaves the cells; turgor decreases. Sufficient water loss produces plasmolysis, with protoplast withdrawal from the wall.
(4) [2]
Calculate percentage mass gain and explain blotting.
Worked solution / model answer
Gain=(30−24)/24×100=25.00%. Blotting removes adhering solution that would alter measured mass.
(5) [3]
Give three controls for a fair comparison.
Worked solution / model answer
Comparable tissue and equal initial dimensions; same duration; same temperature. Accept equal solution volume and consistent blotting. Concentration is the variable changed.
4 [10]
A destarched plant has one leaf partly covered with opaque paper. After sunlight exposure, the leaf is tested for starch.
(1) [1]
Name the starch-testing reagent.
Worked solution / model answer
Iodine solution.
(2) [2]
Why must the plant be destarched first?
Worked solution / model answer
Previously stored starch must be removed so the result can be attributed to starch formed during the experiment.
(3) [2]
Why is alcohol heated in a water bath during the test?
Worked solution / model answer
Alcohol removes chlorophyll so the colour change can be seen; a water bath avoids direct flame near flammable alcohol.
(4) [2]
State the results expected in exposed and covered regions.
Worked solution / model answer
Exposed: blue-black. Covered: yellow-brown, provided destarching and light exclusion were effective.
(5) [3]
State the conclusion and explain why the experiment does not establish the need for carbon dioxide.
Worked solution / model answer
It supports a requirement for light in starch formation under these conditions. Carbon dioxide was not independently changed between the two regions; its role needs a separate controlled experiment.
5 [10]
A kidney model filters blood and returns useful substances to the circulation. Its ADH concentration then rises.
(1) [1]
Name the functional unit of the kidney.
Worked solution / model answer
Nephron.
(2) [2]
Explain ultrafiltration in the glomerulus.
Worked solution / model answer
High blood pressure drives water and small dissolved substances into Bowman’s capsule; blood cells and most large proteins remain in the circulation.
(3) [2]
Why is glucose in the filtrate normally absent from final urine?
Worked solution / model answer
It is small enough to be filtered, then normally selectively reabsorbed, chiefly in the proximal convoluted tubule.
(4) [2]
Predict two changes in final urine when ADH increases.
Worked solution / model answer
Water reabsorption increases; urine volume decreases and it becomes more concentrated.
(5) [3]
Distinguish excretion, egestion and secretion with one example each.
Worked solution / model answer
Excretion removes metabolic waste, e.g. urea; egestion removes undigested food from the alimentary canal; secretion releases a useful substance, e.g. a digestive enzyme or hormone.

7 [10]
Compare control of blood glucose after a carbohydrate-rich meal with the response to sudden danger.
(1) [1]
Name the endocrine region of the pancreas.
Worked solution / model answer
Islets of Langerhans.
(2) [2]
State the source and effect of insulin.
Worked solution / model answer
Beta cells secrete insulin; it promotes glucose utilisation/storage and lowers blood glucose.
(3) [2]
Why are glucagon and insulin described as antagonistic?
Worked solution / model answer
Glucagon raises blood glucose, including by glycogen breakdown; insulin lowers it. Their effects on this regulated quantity oppose each other.
(4) [2]
Give two physiological effects of adrenaline.
Worked solution / model answer
Increased heart rate; increased blood glucose. Accept appropriate increased breathing or redirected blood supply to skeletal muscles.
(5) [3]
Distinguish endocrine and exocrine glands and give an example of each.
Worked solution / model answer
Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into blood, e.g. thyroid. Exocrine glands release products through ducts, e.g. salivary glands. The pancreas has both functions.
8 [10]
Consider the normal human sequence from ovulation to implantation and placental exchange.
(1) [1]

Worked solution / model answer
Oviduct/fallopian tube.
(2) [2]
Distinguish ovulation from implantation.

(3) [2]
State two functions of amniotic fluid.
Worked solution / model answer

Worked solution / model answer
Oxygen passes from maternal towards fetal blood; carbon dioxide passes from fetal towards maternal blood across placental surfaces. Direct mixing is not required.

Testes produce sperm/testosterone; scrotum helps maintain a lower temperature for sperm production; sperm duct carries sperm towards the urethra.
