2 [25]
(1) [5]
A school generates 93 kg food waste, 28 kg clean paper and 18 used batteries. Propose separate management routes and explain why a single compost pit is unsuitable for the whole collection.
Worked solution / model answer
Food waste: controlled composting/biogas where suitable. Clean paper: reuse/recycling. Batteries: authorised hazardous/e-waste collection. Batteries can introduce toxic materials and paper is better recovered separately; mixed disposal reduces safe resource recovery.
(2) [5]
Two similar leafy shoots lose 3.8 g and 1.27 g in 10 minutes. The second has petroleum jelly on the lower leaf surface. Explain the lower loss, identify two variables to control, and state one function of transpiration.
Worked solution / model answer
Jelly restricts stomatal/cuticular water loss; coating the more stomata-rich lower surface particularly reduces transpiration. Control leaf area, time, temperature, humidity and air movement (any two). Transpiration supports water/mineral transport and evaporative cooling.
(3) [5]
For an X-linked recessive model of red-green colour blindness, a mother XⁿXⁿ and father XᴺY have children. Show the cross and state risks separately for sons and daughters. If 200 births followed the expected equal sex ratio, how many affected children are expected?
Worked solution / model answer

Write the parental genotypes, list gametes and combine one allele from each parent. Count genotype and phenotype classes separately.

Mother gametes=Xⁿ; father gametes Xᴺ, Y. all sons affected; all daughters carriers. Overall affected fraction=1/2; expected affected=100. The probability is per birth, not a fixed family outcome.

Check your work
A predicted ratio describes probabilities; it is not a guaranteed exact count in a small family.

(4) [5]
A model heart beats 56 times/min and pumps 47 mL/beat at rest. During exercise values become 133/min and 72 mL/beat. Using output=rate×stroke volume, calculate both outputs in L/min and explain the increase.
Worked solution / model answer
Rest=2.632 L/min; exercise=9.576 L/min. Active muscles need more oxygen/nutrients and faster removal of CO₂; increased heart output supports these demands.
(5) [5]
An organism has 20 chromosomes in a normal somatic cell. Compare chromosome number in its gamete, fertilised egg and daughter cells produced by mitosis. Why is reduction division necessary before sexual reproduction?
Worked solution / model answer

Identify the stage from the chromosome behaviour. Count chromosomes by centromeres; after DNA replication a metaphase chromosome has two chromatids.

Gamete=10; zygote=20; mitotic daughter cell=20. Meiosis halves chromosome number so fertilisation restores it rather than doubling it in each generation.

Check your work
Mitosis preserves chromosome number. Do not confuse DNA amount, chromatids and chromosomes.

3 [10]
A kidney model filters blood and returns useful substances to the circulation. Its ADH concentration then rises.
(1) [1]
Name the functional unit of the kidney.
Worked solution / model answer
Nephron.
(2) [2]
Explain ultrafiltration in the glomerulus.
Worked solution / model answer
High blood pressure drives water and small dissolved substances into Bowman’s capsule; blood cells and most large proteins remain in the circulation.
(3) [2]
Why is glucose in the filtrate normally absent from final urine?
Worked solution / model answer
It is small enough to be filtered, then normally selectively reabsorbed, chiefly in the proximal convoluted tubule.
(4) [2]
Predict two changes in final urine when ADH increases.
Worked solution / model answer
Water reabsorption increases; urine volume decreases and it becomes more concentrated.
(5) [3]
Distinguish excretion, egestion and secretion with one example each.
Worked solution / model answer
Excretion removes metabolic waste, e.g. urea; egestion removes undigested food from the alimentary canal; secretion releases a useful substance, e.g. a digestive enzyme or hormone.
4 [10]

(1) [1]
Name the junction between communicating neurons.
Worked solution / model answer

State the impulse directions in sensory and motor neurons.

Sensory: from receptor towards CNS. Motor: from CNS towards effector.
(3) [2]
Why can withdrawal begin before conscious awareness of pain?
Worked solution / model answer
A spinal reflex pathway can coordinate the response without waiting for conscious cerebral interpretation; impulses also travel to the brain.
(4) [2]
State two changes in the iris during pupil constriction.
Worked solution / model answer
Circular muscles contract; radial muscles relax.
(5) [3]
Trace the withdrawal reflex and distinguish receptor and effector.
Worked solution / model answer
Skin receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron in spinal cord → motor neuron → muscle. Receptor detects the stimulus; effector performs the response.
5 [10]
Compare control of blood glucose after a carbohydrate-rich meal with the response to sudden danger.
(1) [1]
Name the endocrine region of the pancreas.
Worked solution / model answer
Islets of Langerhans.
(2) [2]
State the source and effect of insulin.
Worked solution / model answer
Beta cells secrete insulin; it promotes glucose utilisation/storage and lowers blood glucose.
(3) [2]
Why are glucagon and insulin described as antagonistic?
Worked solution / model answer
Glucagon raises blood glucose, including by glycogen breakdown; insulin lowers it. Their effects on this regulated quantity oppose each other.
(4) [2]
Give two physiological effects of adrenaline.
Worked solution / model answer
Increased heart rate; increased blood glucose. Accept appropriate increased breathing or redirected blood supply to skeletal muscles.
(5) [3]
Distinguish endocrine and exocrine glands and give an example of each.
Worked solution / model answer
Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into blood, e.g. thyroid. Exocrine glands release products through ducts, e.g. salivary glands. The pancreas has both functions.

Worked solution / model answer
Oxygen passes from maternal towards fetal blood; carbon dioxide passes from fetal towards maternal blood across placental surfaces. Direct mixing is not required.

Testes produce sperm/testosterone; scrotum helps maintain a lower temperature for sperm production; sperm duct carries sperm towards the urethra.

8 [10]
Tallness T is dominant over dwarfness t in a pea-plant model. Two heterozygotes are crossed. Use Mendelian segregation and a large population.
(1) [1]
Name the alternative form of a gene.
Worked solution / model answer
Allele.
(2) [2]
Write parental genotypes and their gametes.
Worked solution / model answer
Tt×Tt; each parent produces T and t.
(3) [2]
Give genotypic and phenotypic ratios.
Worked solution / model answer
TT:Tt:tt=1:2:1; tall:dwarf=3:1.
(4) [2]
Calculate the expected dwarf number among 52 offspring. Explain whether this number is guaranteed.
Worked solution / model answer
52/4=13. It is an expectation; random segregation can give a different actual count.
(5) [3]
Explain a test cross distinguishing TT and Tt, including what an all-tall small sample can establish.
Worked solution / model answer
Cross with tt. TT gives all Tt; Tt gives expected 1:1 tall:dwarf. A dwarf demonstrates a t allele in the tall parent; an all-tall small sample does not conclusively establish TT.
