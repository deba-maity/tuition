(1) [5]
A tall pea plant is crossed with a dwarf plant. Among 3824 offspring, 1912 are tall and 1912 dwarf. T is dominant to t. Determine the tall parent’s genotype, show the cross and state the expected offspring if this tall parent were instead crossed with a pure tall plant.
Worked solution / model answer

Write the parental genotypes, list gametes and combine one allele from each parent. Count genotype and phenotype classes separately.

1. The dwarf parent is tt. Dwarf offspring must receive t from both parents, so the tall parent is Tt, not TT.
2. First cross: Tt×tt. Gametes are T,t and t. Offspring: ½Tt (tall), ½tt (dwarf), matching 1912 of each phenotype.
3. Alternative cross: Tt×TT. Gametes are T,t and T. Offspring: ½TT and ½Tt.
4. All offspring in the alternative cross are tall; the genotype ratio is 1:1 and the phenotype is 100% tall.

Check your work
A predicted ratio describes probabilities; it is not a guaranteed exact count in a small family.

(2) [5]
14 diploid body cells, each containing 46 chromosomes, undergo 2 complete rounds of mitosis. Find the number of daughter cells and chromosomes per daughter cell. Explain the role of mitosis in tissue growth.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify the stage from the chromosome behaviour. Count chromosomes by centromeres; after DNA replication a metaphase chromosome has two chromatids.

Cells=14×2^2=56. Every daughter cell has 46 chromosomes. Mitosis preserves chromosome number and produces genetically similar cells for growth/repair.

Check your work
Mitosis preserves chromosome number. Do not confuse DNA amount, chromatids and chromosomes.

(3) [5]
A potato strip initially has mass 8.4 g. After immersion in 4% sucrose solution it has mass 8.0 g. Calculate percentage mass change and explain the direction of water movement through the cell membrane. Assume no tissue loss.
Worked solution / model answer
Mass change=-0.4 g; percentage=-4.76%. Water moves out by exosmosis into the more concentrated external solution; cells lose turgor and may plasmolyse.
(4) [5]
A simplified kidney model forms 187 L filtrate per day and produces 1.5 L urine. Calculate the volume and percentage reabsorbed. Name the filtration site and explain why normal urine should not contain large plasma proteins.
Worked solution / model answer
Reabsorbed=185.5 L; percentage=99.20%. Ultrafiltration occurs at the glomerulus into Bowman’s capsule. The filtration barrier normally retains blood cells and large proteins.
(5) [5]
A model moth population changes from 57% dark forms among 164 moths to 82% after 24 generations on darkened tree trunks. Explain natural selection using variation, differential survival and inheritance. Why is “each moth changed colour because it needed to” an incorrect explanation?
Worked solution / model answer
Pre-existing heritable variation gives some moths better camouflage. Reduced predation increases their survival/reproduction; inherited variants become more frequent. Individuals do not intentionally acquire the needed inherited colour. Selection changes frequencies across generations.

Answer any 4 questions.

3 [10]
A kidney model filters blood and returns useful substances to the circulation. Its ADH concentration then rises.
(1) [1]
Name the functional unit of the kidney.
Worked solution / model answer
Nephron.
(2) [2]
Explain ultrafiltration in the glomerulus.
Worked solution / model answer
High blood pressure drives water and small dissolved substances into Bowman’s capsule; blood cells and most large proteins remain in the circulation.
(3) [2]
Why is glucose in the filtrate normally absent from final urine?
Worked solution / model answer
It is small enough to be filtered, then normally selectively reabsorbed, chiefly in the proximal convoluted tubule.
(4) [2]
Predict two changes in final urine when ADH increases.
Worked solution / model answer
Water reabsorption increases; urine volume decreases and it becomes more concentrated.
(5) [3]
Distinguish excretion, egestion and secretion with one example each.
Worked solution / model answer
Excretion removes metabolic waste, e.g. urea; egestion removes undigested food from the alimentary canal; secretion releases a useful substance, e.g. a digestive enzyme or hormone.

5 [10]
Compare control of blood glucose after a carbohydrate-rich meal with the response to sudden danger.
(1) [1]
Name the endocrine region of the pancreas.
Worked solution / model answer
Islets of Langerhans.
(2) [2]
State the source and effect of insulin.
Worked solution / model answer
Beta cells secrete insulin; it promotes glucose utilisation/storage and lowers blood glucose.
(3) [2]
Why are glucagon and insulin described as antagonistic?
Worked solution / model answer
Glucagon raises blood glucose, including by glycogen breakdown; insulin lowers it. Their effects on this regulated quantity oppose each other.
(4) [2]
Give two physiological effects of adrenaline.
Worked solution / model answer
Increased heart rate; increased blood glucose. Accept appropriate increased breathing or redirected blood supply to skeletal muscles.
(5) [3]
Distinguish endocrine and exocrine glands and give an example of each.
Worked solution / model answer
Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into blood, e.g. thyroid. Exocrine glands release products through ducts, e.g. salivary glands. The pancreas has both functions.

8 [10]

Worked solution / model answer

(3) [2]
Give genotypic and phenotypic ratios.

(4) [2]
Calculate the expected dwarf number among 68 offspring. Explain whether this number is guaranteed.
Worked solution / model answer
68/4=17. It is an expectation; random segregation can give a different actual count.
(5) [3]
Explain a test cross distinguishing TT and Tt, including what an all-tall small sample can establish.
Worked solution / model answer
Cross with tt. TT gives all Tt; Tt gives expected 1:1 tall:dwarf. A dwarf demonstrates a t allele in the tall parent; an all-tall small sample does not conclusively establish TT.
