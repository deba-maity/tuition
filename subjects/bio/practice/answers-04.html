2 [25]
(1) [5]
A root hair is placed in 11% sucrose for 24 minutes and its living contents pull away from the wall. Name the process, state which structure is selectively permeable, and predict the change on transfer to distilled water if the cell is still alive.
Worked solution / model answer
Process: plasmolysis due to exosmosis. The plasma membrane is selectively permeable; the cell wall is freely permeable. In distilled water, endosmosis can cause deplasmolysis and restore turgidity.
(2) [5]
Across 33 observations, subject A has difficulty with balance and coordination of muscle activity, while subject B has difficulty with involuntary functions such as breathing and heartbeat. Name the associated brain regions and contrast voluntary control with involuntary regulation.
Worked solution / model answer
A: cerebellum; B: medulla oblongata. Voluntary action involves conscious initiation; involuntary regulation maintains functions without repeated conscious commands. These are functional associations in an educational model.
(3) [5]
A heart beats 80 times per minute with stroke volume 82 mL. A blood marker is at left ventricle. State the next major route it follows, the oxygenation change across the relevant capillary bed, and why valves are needed. Calculate cardiac output in L/min.
Worked solution / model answer
Route: aorta → systemic arteries → body capillaries → veins → vena cava → right atrium. Pulmonary capillaries oxygenate blood; systemic capillaries supply tissues and collect CO₂. Valves maintain one-way flow by preventing backflow. Arteries are named by direction away from the heart, not oxygen content. Cardiac output=heart rate×stroke volume=80×82=6560 mL/min=6.56 L/min.
(4) [5]
A class examines 61 structural records. Compare pair A (human appendix and reduced caecal structure) with pair B (human arm and whale flipper). Identify the kind of evolutionary evidence and explain why similar function alone does not prove close ancestry.
Worked solution / model answer
A: vestigial: reduced remnant with ancestral significance. B: homologous: common basic structural origin with different functions. Functional resemblance can arise independently; structural/developmental relationships and other evidence are needed.
(5) [5]
A tall pea plant is crossed with a dwarf plant. Among 464 offspring, 232 are tall and 232 dwarf. T is dominant to t. Determine the tall parent’s genotype, show the cross and state the expected offspring if this tall parent were instead crossed with a pure tall plant.
Worked solution / model answer

Write the parental genotypes, list gametes and combine one allele from each parent. Count genotype and phenotype classes separately.

1. The dwarf parent is tt. Dwarf offspring must receive t from both parents, so the tall parent is Tt, not TT.
2. First cross: Tt×tt. Gametes are T,t and t. Offspring: ½Tt (tall), ½tt (dwarf), matching 232 of each phenotype.
3. Alternative cross: Tt×TT. Gametes are T,t and T. Offspring: ½TT and ½Tt.
4. All offspring in the alternative cross are tall; the genotype ratio is 1:1 and the phenotype is 100% tall.

Section B — 40 marks; choice

Answer any 4 questions.

3 [10]
Compare control of blood glucose after a carbohydrate-rich meal with the response to sudden danger.
(1) [1]
Name the endocrine region of the pancreas.
Worked solution / model answer
Islets of Langerhans.
(2) [2]
State the source and effect of insulin.
Worked solution / model answer
Beta cells secrete insulin; it promotes glucose utilisation/storage and lowers blood glucose.
(3) [2]
Why are glucagon and insulin described as antagonistic?
Worked solution / model answer
Glucagon raises blood glucose, including by glycogen breakdown; insulin lowers it. Their effects on this regulated quantity oppose each other.
(4) [2]
Give two physiological effects of adrenaline.
Worked solution / model answer
Increased heart rate; increased blood glucose. Accept appropriate increased breathing or redirected blood supply to skeletal muscles.
(5) [3]
Distinguish endocrine and exocrine glands and give an example of each.
Worked solution / model answer
Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into blood, e.g. thyroid. Exocrine glands release products through ducts, e.g. salivary glands. The pancreas has both functions.
4 [10]
Consider the normal human sequence from ovulation to implantation and placental exchange.
(1) [1]
Name the usual site of fertilisation.
Worked solution / model answer
Oviduct/fallopian tube.
(2) [2]
Distinguish ovulation from implantation.
Worked solution / model answer
Ovulation releases an ovum from an ovary; implantation embeds the early embryo in the uterine lining.
(3) [2]
State two functions of amniotic fluid.
Worked solution / model answer
Cushions against mechanical shock; permits fetal movement in a protected fluid environment. Accept suitable temperature/protection points.
(4) [2]
Explain the directions of oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange at the placenta.
Worked solution / model answer
Oxygen passes from maternal towards fetal blood; carbon dioxide passes from fetal towards maternal blood across placental surfaces. Direct mixing is not required.
(5) [3]
Give one function each of testes, scrotum and sperm duct.
Worked solution / model answer
Testes produce sperm/testosterone; scrotum helps maintain a lower temperature for sperm production; sperm duct carries sperm towards the urethra.
5 [10]
Fertiliser runoff enters a lake. An algal bloom is followed by falling dissolved oxygen. A nearby school generates peels, paper and used batteries.
(1) [1]
Name excessive nutrient enrichment of a water body.
Worked solution / model answer
Eutrophication.
(2) [2]
Explain why oxygen falls after the bloom.
Worked solution / model answer
Dead organic material is decomposed by microorganisms; their aerobic respiration consumes dissolved oxygen.
(3) [2]
Distinguish biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste with examples.
Worked solution / model answer
Peels are broken down biologically; plastic wrappers persist under ordinary conditions and are non-biodegradable.
(4) [2]

Worked solution / model answer
Controlled quantity/timing of fertiliser; vegetated buffers or runoff interception before it enters the lake.

Give separate management routes for peels, paper and batteries.
Worked solution / model answer
Peels: compost/appropriate biogas process. Clean paper: reuse/recycle. Batteries: authorised separate collection/recovery. Segregation avoids contamination and improves recovery.
6 [10]
Tallness T is dominant over dwarfness t in a pea-plant model. Two heterozygotes are crossed. Use Mendelian segregation and a large population.
(1) [1]
Name the alternative form of a gene.
Worked solution / model answer
Allele.
(2) [2]
Write parental genotypes and their gametes.
Worked solution / model answer
Tt×Tt; each parent produces T and t.
(3) [2]
Give genotypic and phenotypic ratios.
Worked solution / model answer
TT:Tt:tt=1:2:1; tall:dwarf=3:1.
(4) [2]
Calculate the expected dwarf number among 100 offspring. Explain whether this number is guaranteed.
Worked solution / model answer
100/4=25. It is an expectation; random segregation can give a different actual count.
(5) [3]
Explain a test cross distinguishing TT and Tt, including what an all-tall small sample can establish.
Worked solution / model answer
Cross with tt. TT gives all Tt; Tt gives expected 1:1 tall:dwarf. A dwarf demonstrates a t allele in the tall parent; an all-tall small sample does not conclusively establish TT.

Sinoatrial node.

Why is the left ventricle more muscular than the right?
Worked solution / model answer
It generates greater pressure for the systemic circuit; the right pumps through the shorter lower-pressure pulmonary circuit.

Calculate cardiac output in L/min.

69×83=5727 mL/min=5.727 L/min.
(5) [3]
Trace blood from the venae cavae to the aorta, naming the chambers in order.
Worked solution / model answer
Venae cavae → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → bicuspid valve → left ventricle → aorta. Credit chamber order, lung circuit and systemic exit.
8 [10]
One diploid cell with 24 chromosomes completes 6 successive rounds of mitosis. Every cell divides and none dies.
(1) [1]
Name the stage with chromosomes at the equator.
Worked solution / model answer
Metaphase.
(2) [2]
Calculate cell number and chromosome number in each daughter cell.
Worked solution / model answer
2^6=64 cells; each retains 24 chromosomes.
(3) [2]
Distinguish chromosome and chromatid at metaphase.
Worked solution / model answer
One replicated chromosome has two sister chromatids joined at a centromere; while joined they count as one chromosome.
(4) [2]
Give two functions of mitosis in a multicellular organism.
Worked solution / model answer
Growth by increasing cell number; repair/replacement of damaged or worn-out cells.
(5) [3]
Compare mitosis and meiosis using divisions, products and chromosome number.
Worked solution / model answer
Mitosis: one division, usually two cells, parental chromosome number maintained. Meiosis: two divisions, usually four haploid products, half the diploid chromosome number.
