(1) [5]
A simplified healthy response shows blood glucose changing from 91 to 164 mg/dL after a meal and then to 87 mg/dL after 108 minutes. Identify the hormone expected to increase after the meal, explain two of its effects and describe negative feedback.
Worked solution / model answer
Insulin secretion increases. It promotes glucose uptake and conversion of surplus glucose to glycogen, helping lower the raised concentration. As the stimulus falls, secretion reduces: negative feedback stabilises conditions. These values are supplied for a model, not individual diagnosis.
(2) [5]
A seedling root with many root hairs is supplied 194 mL water. After a gardener adds concentrated fertiliser (12% model solute concentration), its young leaves wilt although soil remains wet. Explain the root-hair adaptations, the direction of water movement and why “more water in the soil” does not by itself prove water will enter the roots.
Worked solution / model answer
Root hairs provide large absorptive surface, thin permeable walls and a selectively permeable membrane. A strongly concentrated external solution can draw water out by exosmosis. Osmotic movement depends on relative solution concentration/water potential, not total water volume alone.
(3) [5]
Across 44 observations, subject A has difficulty with involuntary functions such as breathing and heartbeat, while subject B has difficulty with balance and coordination of muscle activity. Name the associated brain regions and contrast voluntary control with involuntary regulation.
Worked solution / model answer
A: medulla oblongata; B: cerebellum. Voluntary action involves conscious initiation; involuntary regulation maintains functions without repeated conscious commands. These are functional associations in an educational model.
(4) [5]
For an X-linked recessive model of red-green colour blindness, a mother XᴺXⁿ and father XᴺY have children. Show the cross and state risks separately for sons and daughters. If 124 births followed the expected equal sex ratio, how many affected children are expected?
Worked solution / model answer

Write the parental genotypes, list gametes and combine one allele from each parent. Count genotype and phenotype classes separately.

Mother gametes=Xᴺ, Xⁿ; father gametes Xᴺ, Y. ½ sons affected; no daughters affected; ½ daughters carriers. Overall affected fraction=1/4; expected affected=31. The probability is per birth, not a fixed family outcome.
(5) [5]
In a potometer, an air bubble moves from 14 mm to 63 mm in 3 minutes. Calculate uptake distance per minute. Under more humid air it moves only 16.333 mm in the same time. Explain the difference and why a potometer measures uptake, not transpiration exactly.
Worked solution / model answer
First uptake rate=49/3=16.33 mm/min; humid-air rate=5.44 mm/min. Humidity reduces the water-vapour diffusion gradient. Some absorbed water is retained or used, so uptake is an estimate of transpiration, not an identical quantity.

3 [10]
A model heart beats 73 times/min with stroke volume 77 mL.
(1) [1]
Name the natural pacemaker.
Worked solution / model answer
Sinoatrial node.
(2) [2]
Distinguish pulmonary artery and pulmonary veins.
Worked solution / model answer
Artery: deoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs. Veins: oxygenated blood from lungs to left atrium.
(3) [2]
Why is the left ventricle more muscular than the right?
Worked solution / model answer
It generates greater pressure for the systemic circuit; the right pumps through the shorter lower-pressure pulmonary circuit.
(4) [2]
Calculate cardiac output in L/min.
Worked solution / model answer
73×77=5621 mL/min=5.621 L/min.
(5) [3]
Trace blood from the venae cavae to the aorta, naming the chambers in order.
Worked solution / model answer
Venae cavae → right atrium → tricuspid valve → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium → bicuspid valve → left ventricle → aorta. Credit chamber order, lung circuit and systemic exit.
4 [10]
One diploid cell with 24 chromosomes completes 3 successive rounds of mitosis. Every cell divides and none dies.
(1) [1]
Name the stage with chromosomes at the equator.
Worked solution / model answer
Metaphase.
(2) [2]
Calculate cell number and chromosome number in each daughter cell.
Worked solution / model answer
2^3=8 cells; each retains 24 chromosomes.
(3) [2]
Distinguish chromosome and chromatid at metaphase.
Worked solution / model answer
One replicated chromosome has two sister chromatids joined at a centromere; while joined they count as one chromosome.
(4) [2]
Give two functions of mitosis in a multicellular organism.
Worked solution / model answer
Growth by increasing cell number; repair/replacement of damaged or worn-out cells.
(5) [3]
Compare mitosis and meiosis using divisions, products and chromosome number.
Worked solution / model answer
Mitosis: one division, usually two cells, parental chromosome number maintained. Meiosis: two divisions, usually four haploid products, half the diploid chromosome number.
5 [10]
A potato strip increases from 25 g to 27 g in dilute solution. An identical strip loses mass in concentrated sugar solution. Both are blotted before weighing.
(1) [1]
Name the main process causing the changes.
Worked solution / model answer
Osmosis.
(2) [2]
Explain the first strip’s mass gain.
Worked solution / model answer
Water enters cells from the more dilute external solution across selectively permeable membranes, down the water-potential gradient.
(3) [2]
Describe water movement and the cell-state change in concentrated solution.
Worked solution / model answer
Water leaves the cells; turgor decreases. Sufficient water loss produces plasmolysis, with protoplast withdrawal from the wall.
(4) [2]
Calculate percentage mass gain and explain blotting.
Worked solution / model answer
Gain=(27−25)/25×100=8.00%. Blotting removes adhering solution that would alter measured mass.
(5) [3]
Give three controls for a fair comparison.
Worked solution / model answer
Comparable tissue and equal initial dimensions; same duration; same temperature. Accept equal solution volume and consistent blotting. Concentration is the variable changed.
6 [10]
A destarched plant has one leaf partly covered with opaque paper. After sunlight exposure, the leaf is tested for starch.
(1) [1]
Name the starch-testing reagent.
Worked solution / model answer
Iodine solution.
(2) [2]
Why must the plant be destarched first?

Previously stored starch must be removed so the result can be attributed to starch formed during the experiment.
(3) [2]
Why is alcohol heated in a water bath during the test?
Worked solution / model answer
Alcohol removes chlorophyll so the colour change can be seen; a water bath avoids direct flame near flammable alcohol.

State the results expected in exposed and covered regions.
Worked solution / model answer
Exposed: blue-black. Covered: yellow-brown, provided destarching and light exclusion were effective.

Worked solution / model answer
It supports a requirement for light in starch formation under these conditions. Carbon dioxide was not independently changed between the two regions; its role needs a separate controlled experiment.
7 [10]
A kidney model filters blood and returns useful substances to the circulation. Its ADH concentration then rises.
(1) [1]
Name the functional unit of the kidney.
Worked solution / model answer
Nephron.
(2) [2]
Explain ultrafiltration in the glomerulus.
Worked solution / model answer
High blood pressure drives water and small dissolved substances into Bowman’s capsule; blood cells and most large proteins remain in the circulation.
(3) [2]
Why is glucose in the filtrate normally absent from final urine?
Worked solution / model answer
It is small enough to be filtered, then normally selectively reabsorbed, chiefly in the proximal convoluted tubule.
(4) [2]
Predict two changes in final urine when ADH increases.
Worked solution / model answer
Water reabsorption increases; urine volume decreases and it becomes more concentrated.
(5) [3]
Distinguish excretion, egestion and secretion with one example each.
Worked solution / model answer
Excretion removes metabolic waste, e.g. urea; egestion removes undigested food from the alimentary canal; secretion releases a useful substance, e.g. a digestive enzyme or hormone.
