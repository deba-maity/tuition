(1) [5]
2 diploid body cells, each containing 20 chromosomes, undergo 5 complete rounds of mitosis. Find the number of daughter cells and chromosomes per daughter cell. Explain the role of mitosis in tissue growth.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify the stage from the chromosome behaviour. Count chromosomes by centromeres; after DNA replication a metaphase chromosome has two chromatids.

Cells=2×2^5=64. Every daughter cell has 20 chromosomes. Mitosis preserves chromosome number and produces genetically similar cells for growth/repair.
(2) [5]
Of 132 stomata examined before drought, 107 are open. After water stress, only 53 are open. Find the before/after percentage open and explain the roles of guard-cell turgor and ABA. Give one trade-off of closure.
Worked solution / model answer
Before=81.06%; after=40.15%. Guard-cell turgor controls the pore; ABA promotes closure during stress. Closure conserves water but restricts CO₂ uptake and can reduce photosynthesis.
(3) [5]
A potato strip initially has mass 7.2 g. After immersion in 9% sucrose solution it has mass 5.8 g. Calculate percentage mass change and explain the direction of water movement through the cell membrane. Assume no tissue loss.
Worked solution / model answer
Mass change=-1.4 g; percentage=-19.44%. Water moves out by exosmosis into the more concentrated external solution; cells lose turgor and may plasmolyse.
(4) [5]
A water plant releases 11 bubbles in 7 minutes under one light condition and 22 bubbles in the same time under brighter light. Calculate both rates, infer the effect of light and give one reason bubble count is only an approximate measure of photosynthesis.
Worked solution / model answer
Rates=1.57 and 3.14 bubbles/min. The higher rate is consistent with increased photosynthesis when light was limiting. Bubble sizes vary and some oxygen dissolves; keep plant size, temperature and CO₂ supply controlled.
(5) [5]
A simplified kidney model forms 197 L filtrate per day and produces 1.5 L urine. Calculate the volume and percentage reabsorbed. Name the filtration site and explain why normal urine should not contain large plasma proteins.
Worked solution / model answer
Reabsorbed=195.5 L; percentage=99.24%. Ultrafiltration occurs at the glomerulus into Bowman’s capsule. The filtration barrier normally retains blood cells and large proteins.

Answer any 4 questions.

3 [10]
One diploid cell with 8 chromosomes completes 6 successive rounds of mitosis. Every cell divides and none dies.
(1) [1]
Name the stage with chromosomes at the equator.
Worked solution / model answer
Metaphase.
(2) [2]
Calculate cell number and chromosome number in each daughter cell.
Worked solution / model answer
2^6=64 cells; each retains 8 chromosomes.
(3) [2]
Distinguish chromosome and chromatid at metaphase.
Worked solution / model answer
One replicated chromosome has two sister chromatids joined at a centromere; while joined they count as one chromosome.
(4) [2]
Give two functions of mitosis in a multicellular organism.
Worked solution / model answer
Growth by increasing cell number; repair/replacement of damaged or worn-out cells.
(5) [3]
Compare mitosis and meiosis using divisions, products and chromosome number.
Worked solution / model answer
Mitosis: one division, usually two cells, parental chromosome number maintained. Meiosis: two divisions, usually four haploid products, half the diploid chromosome number.
4 [10]
A potato strip increases from 17 g to 21 g in dilute solution. An identical strip loses mass in concentrated sugar solution. Both are blotted before weighing.
(1) [1]
Name the main process causing the changes.
Worked solution / model answer
Osmosis.
(2) [2]
Explain the first strip’s mass gain.
Worked solution / model answer
Water enters cells from the more dilute external solution across selectively permeable membranes, down the water-potential gradient.
(3) [2]
Describe water movement and the cell-state change in concentrated solution.
Worked solution / model answer
Water leaves the cells; turgor decreases. Sufficient water loss produces plasmolysis, with protoplast withdrawal from the wall.
(4) [2]
Calculate percentage mass gain and explain blotting.
Worked solution / model answer
Gain=(21−17)/17×100=23.53%. Blotting removes adhering solution that would alter measured mass.
(5) [3]
Give three controls for a fair comparison.
Worked solution / model answer
Comparable tissue and equal initial dimensions; same duration; same temperature. Accept equal solution volume and consistent blotting. Concentration is the variable changed.
5 [10]
A destarched plant has one leaf partly covered with opaque paper. After sunlight exposure, the leaf is tested for starch.
(1) [1]
Name the starch-testing reagent.
Worked solution / model answer
Iodine solution.
(2) [2]
Why must the plant be destarched first?
Worked solution / model answer
Previously stored starch must be removed so the result can be attributed to starch formed during the experiment.
(3) [2]
Why is alcohol heated in a water bath during the test?
Worked solution / model answer
Alcohol removes chlorophyll so the colour change can be seen; a water bath avoids direct flame near flammable alcohol.
(4) [2]
State the results expected in exposed and covered regions.
Worked solution / model answer
Exposed: blue-black. Covered: yellow-brown, provided destarching and light exclusion were effective.
(5) [3]
State the conclusion and explain why the experiment does not establish the need for carbon dioxide.
Worked solution / model answer
It supports a requirement for light in starch formation under these conditions. Carbon dioxide was not independently changed between the two regions; its role needs a separate controlled experiment.
6 [10]
A kidney model filters blood and returns useful substances to the circulation. Its ADH concentration then rises.
(1) [1]
Name the functional unit of the kidney.
Worked solution / model answer
Nephron.
(2) [2]
Explain ultrafiltration in the glomerulus.
Worked solution / model answer
High blood pressure drives water and small dissolved substances into Bowman’s capsule; blood cells and most large proteins remain in the circulation.
(3) [2]
Why is glucose in the filtrate normally absent from final urine?
Worked solution / model answer
It is small enough to be filtered, then normally selectively reabsorbed, chiefly in the proximal convoluted tubule.
(4) [2]
Predict two changes in final urine when ADH increases.
Worked solution / model answer
Water reabsorption increases; urine volume decreases and it becomes more concentrated.
(5) [3]
Distinguish excretion, egestion and secretion with one example each.
Worked solution / model answer
Excretion removes metabolic waste, e.g. urea; egestion removes undigested food from the alimentary canal; secretion releases a useful substance, e.g. a digestive enzyme or hormone.
7 [10]
A person withdraws a hand from a hot surface. Separately, the person walks from a dim room into bright light.
(1) [1]
Name the junction between communicating neurons.
Worked solution / model answer
Synapse.
(2) [2]
State the impulse directions in sensory and motor neurons.
Worked solution / model answer
Sensory: from receptor towards CNS. Motor: from CNS towards effector.
(3) [2]
Why can withdrawal begin before conscious awareness of pain?
Worked solution / model answer
A spinal reflex pathway can coordinate the response without waiting for conscious cerebral interpretation; impulses also travel to the brain.
(4) [2]
State two changes in the iris during pupil constriction.
Worked solution / model answer
Circular muscles contract; radial muscles relax.
(5) [3]
Trace the withdrawal reflex and distinguish receptor and effector.
Worked solution / model answer
Skin receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron in spinal cord → motor neuron → muscle. Receptor detects the stimulus; effector performs the response.
8 [10]
Compare control of blood glucose after a carbohydrate-rich meal with the response to sudden danger.
(1) [1]
Name the endocrine region of the pancreas.
Worked solution / model answer
Islets of Langerhans.
(2) [2]
State the source and effect of insulin.
Worked solution / model answer
Beta cells secrete insulin; it promotes glucose utilisation/storage and lowers blood glucose.
(3) [2]
Why are glucagon and insulin described as antagonistic?
Worked solution / model answer
Glucagon raises blood glucose, including by glycogen breakdown; insulin lowers it. Their effects on this regulated quantity oppose each other.
(4) [2]
Give two physiological effects of adrenaline.
Worked solution / model answer
Increased heart rate; increased blood glucose. Accept appropriate increased breathing or redirected blood supply to skeletal muscles.
(5) [3]
Distinguish endocrine and exocrine glands and give an example of each.
Worked solution / model answer
Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into blood, e.g. thyroid. Exocrine glands release products through ducts, e.g. salivary glands. The pancreas has both functions.
