(1) [5]
Across 3 observations, subject A has difficulty with balance and coordination of muscle activity, while subject B has difficulty with involuntary functions such as breathing and heartbeat. Name the associated brain regions and contrast voluntary control with involuntary regulation.
Worked solution / model answer
A: cerebellum; B: medulla oblongata. Voluntary action involves conscious initiation; involuntary regulation maintains functions without repeated conscious commands. These are functional associations in an educational model.
(2) [5]
In a model observed for 66 minutes, response A increases water reabsorption and response B lowers high blood glucose. Identify both hormones and source glands. Explain one difference between endocrine signalling and a nerve impulse.
Worked solution / model answer
A=ADH, released from posterior pituitary; B=insulin, pancreatic beta cells. Hormones travel in blood to target cells with receptors; nerve impulses travel along neurons and usually act more rapidly and locally.
(3) [5]
In a population of 57000, 1824 live births and 798 deaths occur in one year. Calculate birth and death rates per thousand and natural growth rate per thousand. State one limitation of comparing these rates without age-structure information.
Worked solution / model answer
Birth rate=32.0‰; death rate=14.0‰; natural increase=18.0‰. Different age structures can affect births/deaths and must be considered in interpretation.
(4) [5]
A tall pea plant is crossed with a dwarf plant. Among 7708 offspring, 3854 are tall and 3854 dwarf. T is dominant to t. Determine the tall parent’s genotype, show the cross and state the expected offspring if this tall parent were instead crossed with a pure tall plant.
Worked solution / model answer

Write the parental genotypes, list gametes and combine one allele from each parent. Count genotype and phenotype classes separately.

1. The dwarf parent is tt. Dwarf offspring must receive t from both parents, so the tall parent is Tt, not TT.
2. First cross: Tt×tt. Gametes are T,t and t. Offspring: ½Tt (tall), ½tt (dwarf), matching 3854 of each phenotype.
3. Alternative cross: Tt×TT. Gametes are T,t and T. Offspring: ½TT and ½Tt.
4. All offspring in the alternative cross are tall; the genotype ratio is 1:1 and the phenotype is 100% tall.
(5) [5]
A water plant releases 44 bubbles in 6 minutes under one light condition and 74 bubbles in the same time under brighter light. Calculate both rates, infer the effect of light and give one reason bubble count is only an approximate measure of photosynthesis.
Worked solution / model answer
Rates=7.33 and 12.33 bubbles/min. The higher rate is consistent with increased photosynthesis when light was limiting. Bubble sizes vary and some oxygen dissolves; keep plant size, temperature and CO₂ supply controlled. Temperature changed as well and can affect metabolic rate; control it independently before attributing the difference solely to light.

3 [10]
A destarched plant has one leaf partly covered with opaque paper. After sunlight exposure, the leaf is tested for starch.
(1) [1]
Name the starch-testing reagent.
Worked solution / model answer
Iodine solution.
(2) [2]
Why must the plant be destarched first?
Worked solution / model answer
Previously stored starch must be removed so the result can be attributed to starch formed during the experiment.
(3) [2]
Why is alcohol heated in a water bath during the test?
Worked solution / model answer
Alcohol removes chlorophyll so the colour change can be seen; a water bath avoids direct flame near flammable alcohol.
(4) [2]
State the results expected in exposed and covered regions.
Worked solution / model answer
Exposed: blue-black. Covered: yellow-brown, provided destarching and light exclusion were effective.
(5) [3]
State the conclusion and explain why the experiment does not establish the need for carbon dioxide.
Worked solution / model answer
It supports a requirement for light in starch formation under these conditions. Carbon dioxide was not independently changed between the two regions; its role needs a separate controlled experiment.
4 [10]
A kidney model filters blood and returns useful substances to the circulation. Its ADH concentration then rises.
(1) [1]
Name the functional unit of the kidney.
Worked solution / model answer
Nephron.
(2) [2]
Explain ultrafiltration in the glomerulus.
Worked solution / model answer
High blood pressure drives water and small dissolved substances into Bowman’s capsule; blood cells and most large proteins remain in the circulation.
(3) [2]
Why is glucose in the filtrate normally absent from final urine?
Worked solution / model answer
It is small enough to be filtered, then normally selectively reabsorbed, chiefly in the proximal convoluted tubule.
(4) [2]
Predict two changes in final urine when ADH increases.
Worked solution / model answer
Water reabsorption increases; urine volume decreases and it becomes more concentrated.
(5) [3]
Distinguish excretion, egestion and secretion with one example each.
Worked solution / model answer
Excretion removes metabolic waste, e.g. urea; egestion removes undigested food from the alimentary canal; secretion releases a useful substance, e.g. a digestive enzyme or hormone.
5 [10]

Worked solution / model answer

Why can withdrawal begin before conscious awareness of pain?

Circular muscles contract; radial muscles relax.

Trace the withdrawal reflex and distinguish receptor and effector.

Skin receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron in spinal cord → motor neuron → muscle. Receptor detects the stimulus; effector performs the response.
6 [10]
Compare control of blood glucose after a carbohydrate-rich meal with the response to sudden danger.
(1) [1]
Name the endocrine region of the pancreas.
Worked solution / model answer
Islets of Langerhans.
(2) [2]
State the source and effect of insulin.
Worked solution / model answer
Beta cells secrete insulin; it promotes glucose utilisation/storage and lowers blood glucose.
(3) [2]
Why are glucagon and insulin described as antagonistic?
Worked solution / model answer
Glucagon raises blood glucose, including by glycogen breakdown; insulin lowers it. Their effects on this regulated quantity oppose each other.
(4) [2]
Give two physiological effects of adrenaline.
Worked solution / model answer
Increased heart rate; increased blood glucose. Accept appropriate increased breathing or redirected blood supply to skeletal muscles.
(5) [3]
Distinguish endocrine and exocrine glands and give an example of each.
Worked solution / model answer
Endocrine glands are ductless and release hormones into blood, e.g. thyroid. Exocrine glands release products through ducts, e.g. salivary glands. The pancreas has both functions.
7 [10]
Consider the normal human sequence from ovulation to implantation and placental exchange.
(1) [1]
Name the usual site of fertilisation.
Worked solution / model answer
Oviduct/fallopian tube.
(2) [2]
Distinguish ovulation from implantation.
Worked solution / model answer
Ovulation releases an ovum from an ovary; implantation embeds the early embryo in the uterine lining.
(3) [2]
State two functions of amniotic fluid.
Worked solution / model answer
Cushions against mechanical shock; permits fetal movement in a protected fluid environment. Accept suitable temperature/protection points.
(4) [2]
Explain the directions of oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange at the placenta.
Worked solution / model answer
Oxygen passes from maternal towards fetal blood; carbon dioxide passes from fetal towards maternal blood across placental surfaces. Direct mixing is not required.
(5) [3]
Give one function each of testes, scrotum and sperm duct.
Worked solution / model answer
Testes produce sperm/testosterone; scrotum helps maintain a lower temperature for sperm production; sperm duct carries sperm towards the urethra.
8 [10]
Fertiliser runoff enters a lake. An algal bloom is followed by falling dissolved oxygen. A nearby school generates peels, paper and used batteries.
(1) [1]
Name excessive nutrient enrichment of a water body.
Worked solution / model answer
Eutrophication.
(2) [2]
Explain why oxygen falls after the bloom.
Worked solution / model answer
Dead organic material is decomposed by microorganisms; their aerobic respiration consumes dissolved oxygen.
(3) [2]
Distinguish biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste with examples.
Worked solution / model answer
Peels are broken down biologically; plastic wrappers persist under ordinary conditions and are non-biodegradable.
(4) [2]
Suggest two measures to reduce fertiliser runoff.
Worked solution / model answer
Controlled quantity/timing of fertiliser; vegetated buffers or runoff interception before it enters the lake.
(5) [3]
Give separate management routes for peels, paper and batteries.
Worked solution / model answer
Peels: compost/appropriate biogas process. Clean paper: reuse/recycle. Batteries: authorised separate collection/recovery. Segregation avoids contamination and improves recovery.
