(1) [1]
A perpendicular force 12 N has a moment arm 35 cm. Find its moment.
  1. 5.2 N m
  2. 3.2 N m
  3. 4.2 N m
  4. 6.2 N m
Worked solution / model answer
C. 4.2 N m
Perpendicular distance=0.35 m. Moment=force×distance=12×0.35=4.2 N m. At half the distance the moment is 2.1 N m. In the inverse calculation force=4.2/0.35=12 N; through the pivot the arm is zero.
(2) [1]
Find the work by a 10 N force over 17 m when the force and displacement enclose 90°.
  1. -1 J
  2. 1 J
  3. 2 J
  4. 0 J
Worked solution / model answer

Use work=force×distance in the force direction, GPE=mgh, KE=½mv² and power=energy/time. Efficiency=useful output/input.

D. 0 J
W=Fs cosθ=10×17×cos 90°=0 J. The force is perpendicular to displacement and does no work.

Check your work

(1) [3]
A machine has velocity ratio 2 and efficiency 80%. It is operated with effort 21 N. Find mechanical advantage and the load it can raise steadily. Why can its MA be less than its VR?
Worked solution / model answer
η=MA/VR×100, so MA=2×80/100=1.6. Load=MA×effort=33.6 N. Friction and other dissipative losses reduce useful output work.
(2) [3]
Two equal, opposite, parallel forces of 29 N act along lines 0.36 m apart. Find their resultant force and couple moment. Does choosing another moment point change the couple moment?
Worked solution / model answer
Resultant force=0. Couple moment=one force×perpendicular separation=29×0.36=10.44 N m. It is independent of the moment point.
(3) [4]
250 g of water at 65°C is mixed with 150 g of water at 26°C in an insulated container of negligible heat capacity. Find the final temperature. State the heat-balance equation.
Worked solution / model answer

Write heat lost=heat gained. Use Q=mcΔT for a temperature change and Q=mL for a change of state. Check whether all ice melts before solving for a final temperature above 0°C.

Heat lost=heat gained: 250c(65−T)=150c(T−26). T=(250×65+150×26)/400=50.38°C. The result lies between the starting temperatures.

Check your work
Use consistent mass and specific-heat units. Include the vessel’s heat capacity only when it is supplied.

(1) [3]
A source produces sound of frequency 800 Hz in air where speed is 340 m/s. Find the wavelength. When the source enters a medium in which sound travels twice as fast, what happens to frequency and wavelength?
Worked solution / model answer

(2) [3]
A nucleus has mass number 102 and atomic number 46. It emits 1 alpha particle(s) and then 2 beta-minus particle(s). Find the final mass and atomic numbers and explain whether a following gamma emission changes either.
Worked solution / model answer
Each α reduces A by 4 and Z by 2; each β⁻ leaves A unchanged and increases Z by 1. Final A=98; Z=46. Gamma emission changes neither A nor Z.
(3) [4]
A 76-turn coil gives a deflection while a bar magnet approaches it. In one trial speed rises from 1 to 9 cm/s. Predict the effect on induced emf. Explain the effect of each further change: (a) keep the magnet stationary; (b) double the turns.
Worked solution / model answer
Faster approach increases the rate of flux change and induced emf. (a) No flux change occurs, so no induced emf is produced. (b) For the same flux change per turn, the induced emf increases.
