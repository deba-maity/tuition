Distinguish kinetic and gravitational potential energy.
Worked solution / model answer
Energy due to motion, ½mv²; energy due to height in a gravitational field, mgh near Earth.
(3) [2]
Why cannot steady DC operate a transformer continuously in the ordinary way?
Worked solution / model answer
Steady DC produces no continuously changing magnetic flux, so no sustained induced secondary emf; switching transients are different.

Worked solution / model answer
Resultant force zero; resultant moment about any point zero.
(5) [2]
Why can temperature stay constant while ice melts despite heat input?
Worked solution / model answer
Energy is absorbed as latent heat to change the state rather than increase the temperature under the stated melting conditions.
(6) [2]
State both conditions for total internal reflection.
Worked solution / model answer
Light must go from optically denser to rarer medium; angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle.
(7) [3]
A source produces sound of frequency 1050 Hz in air where speed is 330 m/s. Find the wavelength. When the source enters a medium in which sound travels twice as fast, what happens to frequency and wavelength?
Worked solution / model answer
λ=v/f=330/1050=0.314 m. Frequency is fixed by the source and remains 1050 Hz. At twice the speed, wavelength doubles to 0.629 m.

(1) [3]
A straight current-carrying wire carries 1 A in a magnetic field (N to the left and S to the right). Explain the change in force direction if you (a) reverse current and interchange poles, (b) interchange the magnetic poles only. Name the hand rule for the motor effect.
Worked solution / model answer
(a) Both changes reverse their factors, so the force direction remains unchanged. (b) The force reverses. Use Fleming’s left-hand rule for force on a current-carrying conductor.
(2) [3]
A 35 kg body increases its speed from 2 m/s to 20 m/s. Find the increase in kinetic energy and the net work done. State the principle connecting the two.
Worked solution / model answer

Use work=force×distance in the force direction, GPE=mgh, KE=½mv² and power=energy/time. Efficiency=useful output/input.

ΔKE=½m(v²−u²)=½×35×(20²−2²)=6930 J. Net work equals the change in kinetic energy (work-energy theorem).
(3) [4]
A light horizontal beam is supported at a pivot. A 50 N load is 25 cm to the left. What downward force at 30 cm to the right balances it? Find the upward reaction at the pivot.
Worked solution / model answer
Clockwise moment=anticlockwise moment: F×30=50×25. F=125/3 N. Vertical balance gives reaction=50+125/3=275/3 N. The beam weight is neglected.

(1) [3]
A heater supplies 120 W entirely to 100 g material of specific heat 0.9 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹. How long does a temperature rise of 58°C take, if no phase change occurs?
Worked solution / model answer

Write heat lost=heat gained. Use Q=mcΔT for a temperature change and Q=mL for a change of state. Check whether all ice melts before solving for a final temperature above 0°C.

Q=mcΔT=5220 J. Since P=Q/t, t=Q/P=43.50 s. Watt is joule per second.
(2) [3]
A machine has velocity ratio 7 and efficiency 50%. It is operated with effort 34 N. Find mechanical advantage and the load it can raise steadily. Why can its MA be less than its VR?
Worked solution / model answer
η=MA/VR×100, so MA=7×50/100=3.5. Load=MA×effort=119 N. Friction and other dissipative losses reduce useful output work.
(3) [4]
A labelled gamma-emitting sealed source is handled remotely from 22 cm away in a school demonstration. State its ionising/penetrating character, recommend a suitable shield, and explain why increasing distance and reducing exposure time help.
Worked solution / model answer
Gamma: high penetration; needs substantial lead or concrete shielding. Use thick lead/concrete shielding. Distance reduces received intensity; shorter exposure reduces accumulated dose. Never handle or open the source directly.
