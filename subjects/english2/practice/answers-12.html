Section B — Drama

Answer at least ONE question from this section. Across Sections B–D, answer FOUR questions in total. Each is 16 marks.

2 [16]
Julius Caesar Act V Context summary: The armies meet at Philippi; mistaken reports lead to Cassius’s death, followed later by Brutus’s defeat.
(1) [3]
Why does Lucilius pretend to be Brutus, and how does Antony respond?
Worked solution / model answer
Lucilius risks capture to protect Brutus. Antony recognises the loyalty behind the deception and orders him treated well, judging him a valuable honourable prisoner rather than punishing him immediately.
(2) [3]
Why is Cassius disturbed by the birds accompanying the army?
Worked solution / model answer
He contrasts the earlier eagles with ravens, crows and kites and interprets the change as an ominous sign. His admission that he now partly credits omens shows anxiety before battle.
(3) [3]
Explain the error that leads Cassius to order Pindarus to kill him.
Worked solution / model answer
Pindarus mistakes the soldiers surrounding Titinius and their shouts for capture. Cassius concludes that his friend has been taken and his position is lost. In fact the troops are friendly and celebrate Titinius.
(4) [3]
Why does Titinius place a garland on Cassius and then kill himself?
Worked solution / model answer
The garland signifies the success and honour Cassius failed to recognise. Finding Cassius dead because of a mistake, Titinius expresses loyalty and grief and uses Cassius’s sword to take his own life.
(5) [4]
Explain Antony’s final judgement of Brutus and its qualification of the play’s political conflict.
Worked solution / model answer
Antony distinguishes Brutus’s concern for the public good from the envy motivating other conspirators. He praises Brutus’s balanced nature and honour, acknowledging moral worth in a defeated opponent while not undoing the disastrous consequences of his act.
3 [16]
Julius Caesar Act IV Context summary: The triumvirs prepare their campaign. Brutus and Cassius quarrel, reconcile, and discuss marching to Philippi.
(1) [3]
Why does Antony compare Lepidus to an animal used to carry burdens?
Worked solution / model answer
He regards Lepidus as useful for tasks rather than as an equal political judgement-maker. The comparison shows contempt and Antony’s wish to control the partnership.
(2) [3]
How is the quarrel between Brutus and Cassius resolved?
Worked solution / model answer
Cassius offers Brutus his dagger and exposes his hurt; Brutus softens, acknowledges anger and accepts reconciliation. Their personal affection temporarily overcomes accusations and political differences.
(3) [3]
What does Brutus disclose about Portia, and why is the disclosure significant here?
Worked solution / model answer
Portia has died by swallowing fire, distressed by Brutus’s absence and the political situation. Brutus’s controlled disclosure explains part of his emotional strain and shows the personal cost of the conflict.
(4) [3]
Give Cassius’s reason for waiting for the enemy and Brutus’s reason for marching to Philippi.
Worked solution / model answer
Cassius wants the enemy to tire and consume resources while their army rests. Brutus fears the enemy will gather support from the people between them and argues that they should act at a favourable moment.
(5) [4]
How does Caesar’s ghost affect the close of Act IV?
Worked solution / model answer
The apparition identifies itself as Brutus’s evil spirit and promises another meeting at Philippi. Brutus questions it but cannot control it. It connects Caesar’s continuing influence with the approaching battle and Brutus’s fate.

Section C — Prose

Answer at least ONE question from this section. Across Sections B–D, answer FOUR questions in total. Each is 16 marks.

4 [16]
The Elevator Context summary: Martin fears the small elevator in his building; encounters with a large woman intensify his anxiety.
(1) [3]
Why is the woman’s physical presence particularly disturbing inside the elevator?
Worked solution / model answer
She occupies much of the small space and seems to stare at Martin. Her proximity combines his existing fear of confinement with uncertainty about her intentions.
(2) [3]
How does using the stairs fail to solve Martin’s problem?
Worked solution / model answer
The stairs are also difficult and frightening to him; an accident injures him. Avoidance increases his vulnerability and eventually makes him more dependent on the elevator.
(3) [3]
What effect is produced by the woman’s final greeting to Martin?
Worked solution / model answer
Her knowledge of him and the private encounter intensify his fear. The ending stops without explaining her intentions or supplying a rescue, leaving the threat unresolved.
(4) [3]
How does Martin’s father respond to his fear?
Worked solution / model answer
He dismisses it as weakness and expects him to overcome it. The response makes Martin ashamed and leaves him unable to seek reassuring understanding.
(5) [4]
How do setting, parental response and the open ending work together?
Worked solution / model answer
The confined elevator creates fear; the father’s dismissal isolates Martin; injury restricts his choices. The unresolved final encounter leaves readers sharing uncertainty instead of receiving a complete explanation.
5 [16]
The Pedestrian Context summary: Leonard Mead walks through a screen-dominated city and is questioned by an automated police car.
(1) [3]
How do descriptions of the houses contribute to the atmosphere?
Worked solution / model answer
The houses seem dark, silent and tomb-like apart from screen light. The imagery suggests isolation and diminished public life rather than a lively neighbourhood.
(2) [3]
How does the automated police car represent social authority?
Worked solution / model answer
Its impersonal questioning reduces Mead to a set of expected categories. Its lack of a human occupant emphasises mechanical control without sympathetic judgement.
(3) [3]
Why does the absence of other pedestrians matter?
Worked solution / model answer
It makes Mead’s ordinary walk appear exceptional. It also shows a society whose people withdraw into houses and screen entertainment, leaving public space empty.
(4) [3]
Why does the contrast between Mead’s home and the others matter near the end?
Worked solution / model answer
His brightly lit home contrasts with the surrounding darkness and symbolises a more active individual life. He is removed from that life by the conformity-enforcing system.
(5) [4]
Explain how setting and the encounter develop the conflict between individuality and conformity.
Worked solution / model answer
Mead’s attentive walking contrasts with the empty screen-bound city. The police questions deny the legitimacy of harmless difference. His detention turns social sameness into coercion rather than a free preference.

Section D — Poetry

Answer at least ONE question from this section. Across Sections B–D, answer FOUR questions in total. Each is 16 marks.

6 [16]
The Glove and the Lions Context summary: At a royal lion-fight a lady drops her glove among the animals to test her lover publicly.
(1) [3]
What does the king’s response contribute to the ending?
Worked solution / model answer
It endorses the rejection and exposes the lady’s vanity. Public judgement reverses the admiration she hoped the stunt would win.
(2) [3]
What does the lady expect her lover to prove?
Worked solution / model answer
She expects him to retrieve the glove as evidence of devotion and to make her the admired centre of a spectacular romantic gesture.
(3) [3]
How does the final action change the meaning of the heroic rescue?
Worked solution / model answer
The danger is overcome, but the emotional reward is refused. The act affirms self-respect rather than the lady’s power to demand sacrifice.
(4) [3]
How are the lions presented before the glove is dropped?
Worked solution / model answer
Their violent movement and power establish genuine physical danger. This makes the test a serious misuse of courage rather than a harmless game.
(5) [4]
Discuss the distinction between love and vanity in the lady’s test and its outcome.
Worked solution / model answer
The test values spectacle over the beloved’s safety. The lover shows courage but refuses that condition of affection. The king’s approval makes the closing judgement favour dignity and sincere care over theatrical display.
7 [16]
A Considerable Speck Context summary: A writer notices a minute moving creature on his manuscript and observes it before deciding its fate.
(1) [3]
Why is the speck first easy to dismiss?
Worked solution / model answer
Its tiny size makes it appear insignificant and it is initially seen against the writer’s working page. Close observation challenges that first judgement.
(2) [3]
How does the writer’s control over the page create a moral choice?
Worked solution / model answer
He could destroy the creature easily while writing. Recognising signs of an independent mind makes restraint an ethical decision rather than a physical necessity.
(3) [3]
What does the decision to spare the speck reveal?
Worked solution / model answer
It reveals respect for a perceived independent mind and the deliberate restraint of power. The speaker values mental life without requiring physical grandeur.
(4) [3]
Why is the poem more than an observation of an insect?
Worked solution / model answer
The encounter prompts reflection on judgement, intelligence and the treatment of a weaker being. The creature’s size tests the speaker’s willingness to recognise value.
(5) [4]
Explain how observation changes the speaker’s response and supports the title.
Worked solution / model answer
What initially seems negligible displays purposeful behaviour. The speaker revises his judgement and chooses to spare it. The creature becomes considerable through the intelligence he perceives, giving the title its comic and moral force.
