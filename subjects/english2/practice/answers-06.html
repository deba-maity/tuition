Section B — Drama

Answer at least ONE question from this section. Across Sections B–D, answer FOUR questions in total. Each is 16 marks.

2 [16]
Julius Caesar Act V Context summary: The armies meet at Philippi; mistaken reports lead to Cassius’s death, followed later by Brutus’s defeat.
(1) [3]
What disagreement occurs between Antony and Octavius about their positions in battle?
Worked solution / model answer
Antony directs Octavius to the left; Octavius chooses the right. Octavius’s refusal to follow the instruction reveals independence within their alliance rather than complete submission to Antony.
(2) [3]
Why is Cassius disturbed by the birds accompanying the army?
Worked solution / model answer
He contrasts the earlier eagles with ravens, crows and kites and interprets the change as an ominous sign. His admission that he now partly credits omens shows anxiety before battle.
(3) [3]
Why does Cassius send Titinius towards the approaching troops?
Worked solution / model answer
Cassius needs to know whether the troops are friends or enemies. He sends Titinius to investigate and relies on Pindarus’s distant observations instead of obtaining a reliable direct report.
(4) [3]
Why does Lucilius pretend to be Brutus, and how does Antony respond?
Worked solution / model answer
Lucilius risks capture to protect Brutus. Antony recognises the loyalty behind the deception and orders him treated well, judging him a valuable honourable prisoner rather than punishing him immediately.
(5) [4]
Explain Antony’s final judgement of Brutus and its qualification of the play’s political conflict.
Worked solution / model answer
Antony distinguishes Brutus’s concern for the public good from the envy motivating other conspirators. He praises Brutus’s balanced nature and honour, acknowledging moral worth in a defeated opponent while not undoing the disastrous consequences of his act.
3 [16]
Julius Caesar Act IV Context summary: The triumvirs prepare their campaign. Brutus and Cassius quarrel, reconcile, and discuss marching to Philippi.
(1) [3]
Explain the disagreement over money sent to Brutus’s army.
Worked solution / model answer
Brutus says Cassius denied funds when Brutus would not raise them dishonourably. Cassius denies deliberately refusing him and blames the messenger. Distrust turns a logistical dispute into a personal quarrel.
(2) [3]
What does the proscription scene reveal about Antony and Octavius?
Worked solution / model answer
They bargain over people marked for death, including relatives, and discuss using Caesar’s will to meet expenses. Political calculation contrasts with the public image of loyalty surrounding Caesar’s funeral.
(3) [3]
How is the quarrel between Brutus and Cassius resolved?
Worked solution / model answer
Cassius offers Brutus his dagger and exposes his hurt; Brutus softens, acknowledges anger and accepts reconciliation. Their personal affection temporarily overcomes accusations and political differences.
(4) [3]
Why does Antony compare Lepidus to an animal used to carry burdens?
Worked solution / model answer
He regards Lepidus as useful for tasks rather than as an equal political judgement-maker. The comparison shows contempt and Antony’s wish to control the partnership.
(5) [4]
How does Caesar’s ghost affect the close of Act IV?
Worked solution / model answer
The apparition identifies itself as Brutus’s evil spirit and promises another meeting at Philippi. Brutus questions it but cannot control it. It connects Caesar’s continuing influence with the approaching battle and Brutus’s fate.

Section C — Prose

Answer at least ONE question from this section. Across Sections B–D, answer FOUR questions in total. Each is 16 marks.

4 [16]
With the Photographer Context summary: A customer asks for a photograph; the photographer treats his real features as defects to be altered.
(1) [3]
How do the photographer’s comments on the narrator’s face establish his attitude?
Worked solution / model answer
He inspects individual features as faults and treats the customer as raw material. His professional certainty diminishes the narrator’s authority over his own appearance.
(2) [3]
Why does the narrator insist on ownership of his face?
Worked solution / model answer
The insistence asserts personal identity and dignity against an expert who behaves as though the face can be redesigned. Its earnestness in an absurd situation also produces humour.
(3) [3]
Why does the finished photograph fail even if its features are considered attractive?
Worked solution / model answer
Attractiveness is not the narrator’s main criterion. It fails to preserve his individuality and therefore cannot serve the personal and emotional purpose for which he wanted it.
(4) [3]
Why is the narrator’s purpose in visiting the studio important to the conflict?
Worked solution / model answer
He wants a recognisable likeness of himself. The photographer assumes that technically improving the face is more important. Their incompatible definitions of a satisfactory photograph create the conflict.
(5) [4]
Discuss the conflict between appearance and identity using the visit, the alterations and the ending.
Worked solution / model answer
The visit begins with a wish for a personal likeness. Repeated criticism and retouching replace the individual with a supposed ideal. The narrator’s rejection asserts that identity and recognisability matter more than imposed perfection.
5 [16]
The Girl Who Can Context summary: Adjoa hears adults debate her thin legs; her success in running challenges Nana’s judgement.
(1) [3]
Why is the school setting important to Adjoa’s development?
Worked solution / model answer
It creates an opportunity for her ability to be recognised through participation and performance. The opportunity extends beyond the domestic expectations surrounding her.
(2) [3]
What does Nana’s response to Adjoa’s achievement reveal?
Worked solution / model answer
Pride and emotional involvement qualify her earlier criticism. She can respond to evidence and affection even while representing traditional assumptions.
(3) [3]
How does the first-person child narrator expose prejudice without a long direct argument?
Worked solution / model answer
Readers compare Adjoa’s puzzled observations with the adults’ certainty. Her straightforward account allows the inconsistency of judging ability by appearance to emerge.
(4) [3]
How does Adjoa’s running challenge Nana’s judgement?
Worked solution / model answer
The legs dismissed as inadequate enable exceptional performance. Actual achievement overturns an appearance-based assessment and provides a different measure of capability.
(5) [4]
Compare Nana’s initial assessment with the outcome, and explain the story’s treatment of change.
Worked solution / model answer
Initially Nana assesses Adjoa through conventional physical expectations. Running supplies counter-evidence. Nana’s pride suggests attitudes can be challenged through experience, while Adjoa gains recognition on the basis of capability.

Section D — Poetry

Answer at least ONE question from this section. Across Sections B–D, answer FOUR questions in total. Each is 16 marks.

6 [16]
Haunted Houses Context summary: The speaker imagines past inhabitants continuing to share the places occupied by living people.
(1) [3]
How does the speaker question exclusive ownership of a house?
Worked solution / model answer
The living possess legal title, but the lives and memories of earlier occupants also belong to the place. Ownership is set against historical and emotional continuity.
(2) [3]
Why are domestic spaces important to the poem?
Worked solution / model answer
Doorways, stairs and rooms connect everyday life with people who occupied them before. Familiar surroundings make the past seem close to the living.
(3) [3]
Why does the calm tone matter to the treatment of death?
Worked solution / model answer
It makes coexistence with the past contemplative and natural. The poem offers continuity rather than a sensational supernatural threat.
(4) [3]
How does the poem connect the material and spiritual worlds?
Worked solution / model answer
It presents them as overlapping rather than completely separate. Images of passage and connection allow the unseen to influence ordinary experience.
(5) [4]
Explain how two images develop the continuing relationship between past and present.
Worked solution / model answer
The quiet movement of former inhabitants through familiar rooms makes the past part of ordinary domestic life. These presences do not attack the living; they suggest that a place retains the lives once lived there. The image of unseen guests extends the same idea into a social gathering: visible people are not its only company, because memory and earlier lives also occupy the space. Both images make past and present coexist. The calm, reflective tone turns haunting into continuity rather than a conventional supernatural threat.
7 [16]
When Great Trees Fall Context summary: The poem compares the loss of influential people with the fall of great trees, then considers how life responds.
(1) [3]
How does the shift in tone develop the poem’s argument?
Worked solution / model answer
The early disturbance and diminished perception give way to a more affirmative recognition of existence and responsibility. The change enacts gradual recovery.
(2) [3]
Why does the opening describe reactions in the natural world?
Worked solution / model answer
The disturbed animals and landscape give physical scale to loss. The analogy prepares readers for the emotional and intellectual disruption caused by a great soul’s death.
(3) [3]
Why does the poem’s movement towards renewal not cancel grief?
Worked solution / model answer
Grief remains the starting reality. Renewal grows from recognising the continuing possibilities inspired by the person, rather than pretending that the loss did not occur.
(4) [3]
How does the poem represent the immediate effect of bereavement on perception?
Worked solution / model answer
The death changes the survivors’ experience of their surroundings: even breathing and seeing become strained, conveying how thoroughly grief disturbs ordinary perception. Memory then becomes painfully sharp, dwelling on kind words left unsaid and shared experiences that will never occur. This combines a diminished present with a heightened awareness of missed possibilities. The loss is therefore intellectual and emotional as well as physical; the person who once helped give the world meaning is absent.
(5) [4]
Trace the movement from the tree image to the closing affirmation.
Worked solution / model answer
A large physical loss models the disruption of an influential death. Human grief then affects perception and memory. The later recovery recognises that the person’s existence enlarges the possibilities of those who remain, allowing affirmation without erasing sorrow.
