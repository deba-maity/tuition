Section B — Drama

Answer at least ONE question from this section. Across Sections B–D, answer FOUR questions in total. Each is 16 marks.

2 [16]
Julius Caesar Act IV Context summary: The triumvirs prepare their campaign. Brutus and Cassius quarrel, reconcile, and discuss marching to Philippi.
(1) [3]
Explain the disagreement over money sent to Brutus’s army.
Worked solution / model answer
Brutus says Cassius denied funds when Brutus would not raise them dishonourably. Cassius denies deliberately refusing him and blames the messenger. Distrust turns a logistical dispute into a personal quarrel.
(2) [3]
What does Brutus disclose about Portia, and why is the disclosure significant here?
Worked solution / model answer
Portia has died by swallowing fire, distressed by Brutus’s absence and the political situation. Brutus’s controlled disclosure explains part of his emotional strain and shows the personal cost of the conflict.
(3) [3]
How is the quarrel between Brutus and Cassius resolved?
Worked solution / model answer
Cassius offers Brutus his dagger and exposes his hurt; Brutus softens, acknowledges anger and accepts reconciliation. Their personal affection temporarily overcomes accusations and political differences.
(4) [3]
What does the proscription scene reveal about Antony and Octavius?
Worked solution / model answer
They bargain over people marked for death, including relatives, and discuss using Caesar’s will to meet expenses. Political calculation contrasts with the public image of loyalty surrounding Caesar’s funeral.
(5) [4]
How does Caesar’s ghost affect the close of Act IV?
Worked solution / model answer
The apparition identifies itself as Brutus’s evil spirit and promises another meeting at Philippi. Brutus questions it but cannot control it. It connects Caesar’s continuing influence with the approaching battle and Brutus’s fate.
3 [16]
Julius Caesar Act III Context summary: Caesar is killed at the Capitol; Brutus permits Antony to address the citizens under conditions.
(1) [3]
Why does Brutus use the contrast between his love for Caesar and his love for Rome?
Worked solution / model answer
He presents assassination as public duty overcoming personal affection. The contrast asks citizens to judge him as a patriot. His appeal depends on their accepting that Caesar threatened their freedom.
(2) [3]
Why does Caesar reject Metellus Cimber’s petition, and what does his response reveal about his self-image?
Worked solution / model answer
Metellus seeks the recall of his banished brother. Caesar refuses to be moved by pleading and presents his judgement as fixed. This confidence in constancy makes his vulnerability to the conspirators ironic.
(3) [3]
Explain how Antony’s repeated praise of the conspirators changes its effect during the funeral speech.
Worked solution / model answer
The apparently respectful description becomes ironic as Antony supplies evidence against the ambition charge. Repetition lets listeners reach the accusation themselves while Antony appears to obey the restriction on blaming the conspirators.
(4) [3]
Explain why Brutus refuses to allow the conspirators to kill Antony as well. What does this reveal about his political judgement?
Worked solution / model answer
Brutus wants an act against tyranny rather than indiscriminate slaughter; he underestimates Antony’s independent power and assumes that Caesar’s death removes the danger. His ethical intentions accompany a serious strategic error.
(5) [4]
Compare Brutus’s and Antony’s methods of addressing the citizens.
Worked solution / model answer
Brutus offers a concise justification based on honour, reason and civic freedom. Antony uses irony, concrete memories, wounds, the will and controlled emotion. Both seek public judgement, but Antony adapts his speech to the crowd’s developing responses.

4 [16]
The Girl Who Can Context summary: Adjoa hears adults debate her thin legs; her success in running challenges Nana’s judgement.
(1) [3]
Why is the school setting important to Adjoa’s development?
Worked solution / model answer
It creates an opportunity for her ability to be recognised through participation and performance. The opportunity extends beyond the domestic expectations surrounding her.
(2) [3]
How does the first-person child narrator expose prejudice without a long direct argument?
Worked solution / model answer
Readers compare Adjoa’s puzzled observations with the adults’ certainty. Her straightforward account allows the inconsistency of judging ability by appearance to emerge.
(3) [3]
How does Adjoa’s running challenge Nana’s judgement?
Worked solution / model answer
The legs dismissed as inadequate enable exceptional performance. Actual achievement overturns an appearance-based assessment and provides a different measure of capability.
(4) [3]
What does Nana’s response to Adjoa’s achievement reveal?
Worked solution / model answer
Pride and emotional involvement qualify her earlier criticism. She can respond to evidence and affection even while representing traditional assumptions.
(5) [4]
Compare Nana’s initial assessment with the outcome, and explain the story’s treatment of change.
Worked solution / model answer
Initially Nana assesses Adjoa through conventional physical expectations. Running supplies counter-evidence. Nana’s pride suggests attitudes can be challenged through experience, while Adjoa gains recognition on the basis of capability.
5 [16]
The Last Lesson Context summary: Franz arrives at school to learn that an official order has ended French teaching in Alsace and Lorraine.
(1) [3]
How does M. Hamel distribute responsibility for neglected learning?
Worked solution / model answer
He criticises pupils’ postponement, parents’ preference for work and his own past lapses. The blame is shared rather than placed only on Franz.
(2) [3]
Why is M. Hamel unable to complete his final spoken farewell?
Worked solution / model answer
Emotion overwhelms him. He turns to the blackboard and writes a patriotic declaration, making the silent action carry what he cannot say aloud.
(3) [3]
Why have the elders come to the classroom?
Worked solution / model answer
They regret neglecting education and wish to honour the teacher’s long service. Their attendance expresses respect for their language and shared identity.
(4) [3]
What unusual features of the classroom signal a change?
Worked solution / model answer
The usual noise is absent, M. Hamel wears formal clothes and village elders sit at the back. These details prepare Franz for the seriousness of the final lesson.
(5) [4]
Trace Franz’s change and connect it to language and identity.
Worked solution / model answer
Franz begins afraid of a school exercise and willing to avoid it. The order makes him understand language as a threatened shared inheritance. Attentive listening and regret replace indifference; the teacher’s final act gives that recognition public emotional force.

6 [16]
When Great Trees Fall Context summary: The poem compares the loss of influential people with the fall of great trees, then considers how life responds.
(1) [3]
What responsibility do the living acquire through remembering great souls?
Worked solution / model answer
They can live more fully and recognise capacities awakened by those lives. Continuing influence becomes a reason to act rather than remain permanently diminished.
(2) [3]
What role does memory play after the death of a great person?
Worked solution / model answer
Memory recalls the person’s presence and the opportunities for appreciation that were missed. It makes absence painful while preserving the influence of the life.
(3) [3]
Why does the opening describe reactions in the natural world?
Worked solution / model answer
The disturbed animals and landscape give physical scale to loss. The analogy prepares readers for the emotional and intellectual disruption caused by a great soul’s death.
(4) [3]
What does the image of a great tree represent in the poem?
Worked solution / model answer
It represents a person whose life gives others shelter, direction or strength. The scale of the tree suggests the breadth of the loss caused by such a death.
(5) [4]
Trace the movement from the tree image to the closing affirmation.
Worked solution / model answer
A large physical loss models the disruption of an influential death. Human grief then affects perception and memory. The later recovery recognises that the person’s existence enlarges the possibilities of those who remain, allowing affirmation without erasing sorrow.
7 [16]
The Power of Music Context summary: Bhishma Lochan Sharma sings with such exaggerated effects that people and animals suffer until a goat intervenes.
(1) [3]
What is ironic about the title?
Worked solution / model answer
Music conventionally suggests pleasure or harmony. Here its power is represented as disruptive and painful, making the title praise the performance in a comic opposite sense.
(2) [3]
Explain the role of sound and rhythm in the comic narrative.
Worked solution / model answer
The lively rhythm carries the performance forward with a force out of proportion to its musical worth. Repeated distress among listeners makes the song sound relentless rather than pleasurable. Their appeals to stop contrast with the singer’s persistence, so each continuing disturbance increases the comic tension. The goat’s abrupt physical intervention supplies a decisive change: action silences a voice that ordinary requests could not restrain. Sound, narrative pace and the sudden ending together expose the singer’s self-importance.
(3) [3]
Why are the reactions of animals and people presented together?
Worked solution / model answer
They make the suffering seem universal and extend the joke beyond an individual listener’s taste. The whole environment becomes a witness against the singer.
(4) [3]
What makes the singer’s confidence comic?
Worked solution / model answer
He is absorbed in his performance while the surrounding world reacts with distress. The mismatch between self-satisfaction and actual effect undermines his view of his talent.
(5) [4]
How do hyperbole, contrast and the final reversal work together?
Worked solution / model answer
Impossible effects inflate the song into a catastrophe; the singer’s delight contrasts with widespread distress. A goat’s simple action defeats the supposedly powerful performer, completing the satire of self-important bad singing.
