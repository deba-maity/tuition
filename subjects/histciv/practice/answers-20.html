Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
Give three reasons why the Lok Sabha exercises stronger control over the Union Council of Ministers than the Rajya Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
The Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha; a no-confidence motion there can require resignation; the Lok Sabha controls grants and the Money Bill process.
(2) [3]
A Money Bill has been passed by the Lok Sabha. State three limitations on the Rajya Sabha’s role in dealing with it.
Worked solution / model answer
The Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend it as an equal chamber; it can recommend changes; it must return it within 14 days, after which the constitutional Money Bill procedure allows passage without its return. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject its recommendations.
(3) [4]
Explain why the formal executive head and the politically responsible government are different in India. Link the President, Prime Minister and Lok Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
President is constitutional head; Council headed by PM ordinarily provides aid/advice and directs policy; Council’s collective responsibility to Lok Sabha links executive government to elected-house confidence.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain collective responsibility of ministers to the Lok Sabha using a concrete consequence for a lost confidence vote.
Worked solution / model answer
Ministers publicly support Cabinet decisions as one team; the Council remains in office while it enjoys Lok Sabha confidence; loss of confidence requires the Council as a whole to resign, not merely one minister.
(2) [3]
Distinguish the Cabinet from the wider Council of Ministers in composition, frequency of meetings and policy-making.
Worked solution / model answer
Cabinet is the smaller senior group within the Council; it meets regularly as the main decision-making body; the Council includes other ranks and does not ordinarily meet collectively for every policy decision.
(3) [4]
Explain four steps/features of the constitutional impeachment process for the President.
Worked solution / model answer
A charge of violation of the Constitution may originate in either house; the prescribed notice/signature and special-majority requirements apply; the other house investigates or causes investigation with a right of defence; a resolution supported by at least two-thirds of that house’s total membership removes the President. The initiating resolution also requires the constitutional two-thirds-total-membership majority.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
State who appoints High Court judges, the retirement age, and one relevant professional qualification.
Worked solution / model answer
The President appoints them through the constitutional process; retirement age is 62; at least ten years’ judicial office in India or at least ten years as an advocate of a High Court is a relevant alternative, along with citizenship.
(2) [3]
A person is unlawfully detained. Name the relevant writ and explain its purpose. Contrast it with mandamus.
Worked solution / model answer
Habeas corpus requires production of the detained person and examination of the legality of detention; it protects personal liberty. Mandamus directs a public authority to perform a public/legal duty it has failed to perform.
(3) [4]
Explain four safeguards of judicial independence.
Worked solution / model answer
Secure tenure with removal only by the prescribed process; protected service conditions/salaries as constitutionally provided; separation from routine executive direction in adjudication; restrictions on discussion of judicial conduct except appropriate removal proceedings. Post-retirement practice restrictions for Supreme Court judges are another relevant safeguard.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three ways in which British economic policies contributed to the Revolt of 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Heavy revenue demands burdened cultivators; decline of crafts/competition from British manufactures hurt artisans; annexations and loss of patronage affected dependants of Indian courts. Link each policy to the affected group.
(2) [3]
Explain the Enfield cartridge issue as the immediate cause of the revolt, distinguishing it from long-term grievances.
Worked solution / model answer
Cartridges were believed to be greased with cow/pig fat; biting them offended Hindu/Muslim religious beliefs; this triggered existing resentment rather than creating all discontent by itself.
(3) [4]
Compare the role of armed rebellion in 1857 with the intended discipline of Gandhian mass movements. Use methods, leadership and participation.
Worked solution / model answer
1857 involved military/civil rebellion with varied regional leadership and motives; Gandhian movements sought disciplined non-violent mass mobilisation. Both challenged colonial rule, but methods, coordination and political setting differed.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain why the Salt March was an effective beginning for Civil Disobedience.
Worked solution / model answer
Salt affected ordinary people across classes; breaking the law provided a clear non-violent act against colonial authority; the march mobilised public attention and widened participation.
(2) [3]
Why was Non-Cooperation withdrawn after Chauri Chaura? State the incident’s significance and Gandhi’s reasoning.
Worked solution / model answer
Protesters killed policemen after setting a police station on fire; violence contradicted the movement’s non-violent discipline; Gandhi suspended the movement because preparation/self-restraint were inadequate for the planned struggle.
(3) [4]
Give four causes or immediate circumstances leading to Quit India in 1942.
Worked solution / model answer
Failure of the Cripps Mission; wartime hardships and discontent; threat of Japanese advance; demand for an immediate end to British rule rather than an uncertain post-war promise. Explain their combined effect.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain why the INA trials had political significance beyond the legal charges.
Worked solution / model answer
They generated widespread nationalist sympathy; united different sections in support; intensified disaffection and challenged colonial legitimacy despite the INA’s military defeat.
(2) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(3) [4]
Explain four contributions of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA to the freedom struggle.
Worked solution / model answer
Leadership and reorganisation of INA; formation of the Provisional Government of Azad Hind; mobilisation of Indians overseas and the military campaign; political impact of INA trials and patriotic response. Do not claim that INA alone secured independence militarily.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
Compare the League of Nations and the United Nations in origin and purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
League followed WWI; UN followed WWII; both aimed at peace/cooperation but differed in structure and enforcement arrangements. Their existence did not automatically guarantee collective action.
(2) [3]
State three common ideological features of Fascism and Nazism.
Worked solution / model answer
Dictatorial one-party leadership; aggressive nationalism and militarism; suppression of opposition and individual freedoms. Nazism’s racial ideology must not be treated as identical in every detail to Italian Fascism.
(3) [4]
Explain four consequences of the Second World War.
Worked solution / model answer
Defeat of Axis powers; huge human/material loss; formation of the United Nations; emergence of US/USSR rivalry and the Cold War, with decolonisation accelerated in the wider aftermath. Use four distinct consequences.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
State three purposes of the United Nations.
Worked solution / model answer
Maintain international peace/security; develop friendly relations among nations; cooperate on economic/social/humanitarian problems and human rights. Explain each distinct purpose.
(2) [3]
State the composition and principal judicial role of the International Court of Justice.
Worked solution / model answer
Fifteen judges; elected for nine-year terms through General Assembly and Security Council; settles submitted legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions when properly requested. It is not a criminal trial court for ordinary individual offences.
(3) [4]
Explain four objectives/principles associated with NAM.
Worked solution / model answer
Independent foreign policy; opposition to colonialism/racism; peaceful coexistence and settlement of disputes; disarmament and reduced bloc confrontation. Explain how these differ from passive neutrality in every conflict.
