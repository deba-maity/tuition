Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
A member wants to ask the government for an explanation of an urgent matter. Explain Question Hour, an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion as different accountability devices.
Worked solution / model answer
Question Hour permits questions to ministers; an adjournment motion interrupts normal business for a definite urgent public matter under the rules; a no-confidence motion tests the Lok Sabha’s confidence in the Council of Ministers.
(2) [3]
Distinguish an ordinary bill from a Money Bill with reference to introduction, Rajya Sabha powers and a joint sitting.
Worked solution / model answer
An ordinary bill may generally originate in either house; a Money Bill originates only in Lok Sabha on the required recommendation. Both houses have substantial ordinary-bill powers, while Rajya Sabha only recommends on a Money Bill. Joint sitting provisions address specified ordinary-bill deadlocks, not Money Bills.
(3) [4]
Explain why the formal executive head and the politically responsible government are different in India. Link the President, Prime Minister and Lok Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
President is constitutional head; Council headed by PM ordinarily provides aid/advice and directs policy; Council’s collective responsibility to Lok Sabha links executive government to elected-house confidence.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
Give three financial powers or functions associated with the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Prior recommendation for introduction of a Money Bill; the annual financial statement is laid before Parliament in the President’s name; appointment of the Finance Commission. Authorisation of advances from the Contingency Fund is also relevant.
(2) [3]
Distinguish the Cabinet from the wider Council of Ministers in composition, frequency of meetings and policy-making.
Worked solution / model answer
Cabinet is the smaller senior group within the Council; it meets regularly as the main decision-making body; the Council includes other ranks and does not ordinarily meet collectively for every policy decision.
(3) [4]
Classify these presidential functions and explain each classification: appointing ministers, assenting to a bill, granting a pardon, and appointing the Finance Commission.
Worked solution / model answer
Appointment: executive; assent: legislative; pardon: judicial/clemency; Finance Commission: financial. Give one explanatory clause linking each action to its category rather than four unexplained labels.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
State three constitutional qualifications for appointment as a Supreme Court judge.
Worked solution / model answer
Indian citizenship plus the prescribed judicial/advocacy qualification: at least five years as a High Court judge, at least ten years as a High Court advocate, or a distinguished jurist in the President’s opinion. Do not treat all alternative professional qualifications as simultaneously compulsory.
(2) [3]
State who appoints High Court judges, the retirement age, and one relevant professional qualification.
Worked solution / model answer
The President appoints them through the constitutional process; retirement age is 62; at least ten years’ judicial office in India or at least ten years as an advocate of a High Court is a relevant alternative, along with citizenship.
(3) [4]
Compare presidential clemency and a court’s judicial review. Why are these not interchangeable?
Worked solution / model answer
Clemency affects punishment through a constitutional executive power; judicial review examines legal/constitutional validity. Both have defined limits but one is not an ordinary appellate substitute for the other.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
What was the General Service Enlistment Act, and why did some sepoys resent it? Give the policy and two related concerns.
Worked solution / model answer
The 1856 measure required new recruits to accept overseas service when ordered. Crossing seas conflicted with some religious/caste beliefs; it increased anxiety about British interference and service conditions. Explain resentment without claiming every soldier shared one belief.
(2) [3]
Compare political grievances of rulers with military grievances of sepoys in the revolt.
Worked solution / model answer
Rulers feared annexation/loss of authority and honours; sepoys resented unequal pay/promotion and service changes; the cartridge issue connected military grievance with religious fear. Different groups joined for overlapping, not identical, motives.
(3) [4]
Compare the role of armed rebellion in 1857 with the intended discipline of Gandhian mass movements. Use methods, leadership and participation.
Worked solution / model answer
1857 involved military/civil rebellion with varied regional leadership and motives; Gandhian movements sought disciplined non-violent mass mobilisation. Both challenged colonial rule, but methods, coordination and political setting differed.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three contributions of the press to the growth of Indian nationalism.
Worked solution / model answer
It criticised exploitative policies; spread political ideas and information across regions; linked educated opinion and exposed common grievances, supporting a sense of shared national interest.
(2) [3]
Explain the significance of Swadeshi, boycott and national education as related methods.
Worked solution / model answer
Swadeshi promoted indigenous goods; boycott withdrew support from imported goods/institutions; national education developed self-reliant institutions and national consciousness. Link all three to constructive and oppositional work.
(3) [4]
Give four contributions of an Assertive Nationalist leader, naming the leader first.
Worked solution / model answer
For Tilak: popular festivals for political mobilisation; newspapers Kesari/Mahratta; insistence on Swaraj; support for Swadeshi/boycott/national education and national awareness. Accept another syllabus leader with four accurate matched contributions.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(2) [3]
Explain why the INA trials had political significance beyond the legal charges.
Worked solution / model answer
They generated widespread nationalist sympathy; united different sections in support; intensified disaffection and challenged colonial legitimacy despite the INA’s military defeat.
(3) [4]
State four provisions of the Indian Independence Act of 1947.
Worked solution / model answer
Creation of two independent dominions from 15 August 1947; end of British sovereignty/control; constituent assemblies gained legislative authority pending constitutions; British paramountcy over princely states lapsed. Relevant provisions must not be confused with unrelated later republican arrangements.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain the policy of appeasement and two reasons it encouraged aggression.
Worked solution / model answer
Concessions were made to aggressive powers in hope of preserving peace; failure to enforce limits encouraged further demands; it weakened collective resistance and gave aggressors time/confidence to expand.
(2) [3]
Compare the League of Nations and the United Nations in origin and purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
League followed WWI; UN followed WWII; both aimed at peace/cooperation but differed in structure and enforcement arrangements. Their existence did not automatically guarantee collective action.
(3) [4]
Explain how experience of two world wars encouraged both international organisation and a desire to avoid power-bloc domination. Distinguish the UN from NAM.
Worked solution / model answer
War destruction encouraged collective peace/security through the UN; later Cold War rivalry led NAM members to independent non-bloc judgement. The organisations arose in related international conditions but have different structures and roles.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
State three purposes of the United Nations.
Worked solution / model answer
Maintain international peace/security; develop friendly relations among nations; cooperate on economic/social/humanitarian problems and human rights. Explain each distinct purpose.
(2) [3]
State the composition and principal judicial role of the International Court of Justice.
Worked solution / model answer
Fifteen judges; elected for nine-year terms through General Assembly and Security Council; settles submitted legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions when properly requested. It is not a criminal trial court for ordinary individual offences.
(3) [4]
Explain why the UN and NAM are not interchangeable organisations using four distinctions.
Worked solution / model answer
UN is a universal Charter-based international organisation; NAM is a grouping centred on independent non-bloc policy. Their membership principles differ; UN has formal principal organs such as the Council/ICJ; NAM does not replace those Charter mechanisms or hold the Council’s enforcement powers.
