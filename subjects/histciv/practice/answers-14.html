Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
State three features explaining why the Rajya Sabha is called a permanent house.
Worked solution / model answer
It is not dissolved as a whole; members have staggered six-year terms; approximately one-third retire every two years.
(2) [3]
A Money Bill has been passed by the Lok Sabha. State three limitations on the Rajya Sabha’s role in dealing with it.
Worked solution / model answer
The Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend it as an equal chamber; it can recommend changes; it must return it within 14 days, after which the constitutional Money Bill procedure allows passage without its return. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject its recommendations.
(3) [4]
Explain why the formal executive head and the politically responsible government are different in India. Link the President, Prime Minister and Lok Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
President is constitutional head; Council headed by PM ordinarily provides aid/advice and directs policy; Council’s collective responsibility to Lok Sabha links executive government to elected-house confidence.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain how the President is the constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise effective executive authority.
Worked solution / model answer
Executive acts are formally taken in the President’s name; the President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council; the Prime Minister heads the Council and directs the government enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.
(2) [3]
State three legislative powers of the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Summoning/proroguing Parliament; addressing Parliament as prescribed and sending messages; assent to bills and promulgation of ordinances when constitutional conditions are met. Dissolution of Lok Sabha is another accepted relevant point.
(3) [4]
Give four ways in which the Prime Minister leads the Union government.
Worked solution / model answer
Selects/recommends ministers; allocates or coordinates portfolios and policy; chairs Cabinet meetings; acts as the main link between President and Council and communicates decisions. Leading the parliamentary majority is an additional relevant point.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
A person is unlawfully detained. Name the relevant writ and explain its purpose. Contrast it with mandamus.
Worked solution / model answer
Habeas corpus requires production of the detained person and examination of the legality of detention; it protects personal liberty. Mandamus directs a public authority to perform a public/legal duty it has failed to perform.
(2) [3]
Distinguish original, appellate and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
Worked solution / model answer
Original: specified cases begin there, including certain federal disputes and rights remedies; appellate: hears eligible appeals from lower courts; advisory: the President may seek an opinion under Article 143, which is advisory rather than an ordinary executable judgment.
(3) [4]
Explain judicial review and name three kinds of government action that may raise a constitutional challenge.
Worked solution / model answer
Judicial review examines conformity with the Constitution. A law allegedly violating fundamental rights, legislation outside the enacting body’s competence, or unconstitutional executive action may be reviewed. Courts do not replace policy merely because they prefer another policy.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain the Enfield cartridge issue as the immediate cause of the revolt, distinguishing it from long-term grievances.
Worked solution / model answer
Cartridges were believed to be greased with cow/pig fat; biting them offended Hindu/Muslim religious beliefs; this triggered existing resentment rather than creating all discontent by itself.
(2) [3]
Explain three socio-religious causes of resentment before 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Fear of conversion linked with missionary activity; suspicion of interference with customs and inheritance laws; racial discrimination and anxiety about unfamiliar reforms. Show how fear and distrust connected these developments.
(3) [4]
Compare the role of armed rebellion in 1857 with the intended discipline of Gandhian mass movements. Use methods, leadership and participation.
Worked solution / model answer
1857 involved military/civil rebellion with varied regional leadership and motives; Gandhian movements sought disciplined non-violent mass mobilisation. Both challenged colonial rule, but methods, coordination and political setting differed.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Why was Non-Cooperation withdrawn after Chauri Chaura? State the incident’s significance and Gandhi’s reasoning.
Worked solution / model answer
Protesters killed policemen after setting a police station on fire; violence contradicted the movement’s non-violent discipline; Gandhi suspended the movement because preparation/self-restraint were inadequate for the planned struggle.
(2) [3]
Explain why the Salt March was an effective beginning for Civil Disobedience.
Worked solution / model answer
Salt affected ordinary people across classes; breaking the law provided a clear non-violent act against colonial authority; the march mobilised public attention and widened participation.
(3) [4]
Give four causes or immediate circumstances leading to Quit India in 1942.
Worked solution / model answer
Failure of the Cripps Mission; wartime hardships and discontent; threat of Japanese advance; demand for an immediate end to British rule rather than an uncertain post-war promise. Explain their combined effect.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(2) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(3) [4]
Distinguish the Cabinet Mission’s aim of preserving unity from the final 1947 partition arrangement using four points.
Worked solution / model answer
Cabinet Mission proposed a union with limited central subjects and provincial grouping; it sought to avoid division into sovereign states. The 1947 plan accepted partition and two successor dominions; transfer of power followed with different constitutional arrangements. Explain differences rather than mixing clauses from the two plans.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain the policy of appeasement and two reasons it encouraged aggression.
Worked solution / model answer
Concessions were made to aggressive powers in hope of preserving peace; failure to enforce limits encouraged further demands; it weakened collective resistance and gave aggressors time/confidence to expand.
(2) [3]
State three common ideological features of Fascism and Nazism.
Worked solution / model answer
Dictatorial one-party leadership; aggressive nationalism and militarism; suppression of opposition and individual freedoms. Nazism’s racial ideology must not be treated as identical in every detail to Italian Fascism.
(3) [4]
Explain four consequences of the Second World War.
Worked solution / model answer
Defeat of Axis powers; huge human/material loss; formation of the United Nations; emergence of US/USSR rivalry and the Cold War, with decolonisation accelerated in the wider aftermath. Use four distinct consequences.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
A student says “non-alignment means never taking a position.” Correct the statement with three points.
Worked solution / model answer
It rejects automatic alignment with a power bloc; members judge issues on their merits; active support for peace, decolonisation and cooperation is consistent with non-alignment.
(2) [3]
State the composition and principal judicial role of the International Court of Justice.
Worked solution / model answer
Fifteen judges; elected for nine-year terms through General Assembly and Security Council; settles submitted legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions when properly requested. It is not a criminal trial court for ordinary individual offences.
(3) [4]
Explain four distinctions between the General Assembly and Security Council.
Worked solution / model answer
Membership: all UN members versus fifteen; representation/voting: one state one vote versus permanent/non-permanent categories; veto: absent in Assembly but present for permanent Council members on substantive decisions; role: deliberation/recommendations versus primary responsibility for peace/security under the Charter.
