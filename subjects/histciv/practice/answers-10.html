Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
A member wants to ask the government for an explanation of an urgent matter. Explain Question Hour, an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion as different accountability devices.
Worked solution / model answer
Question Hour permits questions to ministers; an adjournment motion interrupts normal business for a definite urgent public matter under the rules; a no-confidence motion tests the Lok Sabha’s confidence in the Council of Ministers.
(2) [3]
Distinguish an ordinary bill from a Money Bill with reference to introduction, Rajya Sabha powers and a joint sitting.
Worked solution / model answer
An ordinary bill may generally originate in either house; a Money Bill originates only in Lok Sabha on the required recommendation. Both houses have substantial ordinary-bill powers, while Rajya Sabha only recommends on a Money Bill. Joint sitting provisions address specified ordinary-bill deadlocks, not Money Bills.
(3) [4]
Distinguish the roles of the Speaker and the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha using four points.
Worked solution / model answer
The Speaker is elected by Lok Sabha members; the Vice-President is ex officio Rajya Sabha Chairman. They preside over their respective houses and enforce procedure. The Speaker certifies a Money Bill. A joint sitting is normally presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker, not by the Rajya Sabha Chairman.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain how the President is the constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise effective executive authority.
Worked solution / model answer
Executive acts are formally taken in the President’s name; the President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council; the Prime Minister heads the Council and directs the government enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.
(2) [3]
Explain collective responsibility of ministers to the Lok Sabha using a concrete consequence for a lost confidence vote.
Worked solution / model answer
Ministers publicly support Cabinet decisions as one team; the Council remains in office while it enjoys Lok Sabha confidence; loss of confidence requires the Council as a whole to resign, not merely one minister.
(3) [4]
Give four ways in which the Prime Minister leads the Union government.
Worked solution / model answer
Selects/recommends ministers; allocates or coordinates portfolios and policy; chairs Cabinet meetings; acts as the main link between President and Council and communicates decisions. Leading the parliamentary majority is an additional relevant point.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
Distinguish original, appellate and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
Worked solution / model answer
Original: specified cases begin there, including certain federal disputes and rights remedies; appellate: hears eligible appeals from lower courts; advisory: the President may seek an opinion under Article 143, which is advisory rather than an ordinary executable judgment.
(2) [3]
State who appoints High Court judges, the retirement age, and one relevant professional qualification.
Worked solution / model answer
The President appoints them through the constitutional process; retirement age is 62; at least ten years’ judicial office in India or at least ten years as an advocate of a High Court is a relevant alternative, along with citizenship.
(3) [4]
Compare presidential clemency and a court’s judicial review. Why are these not interchangeable?
Worked solution / model answer
Clemency affects punishment through a constitutional executive power; judicial review examines legal/constitutional validity. Both have defined limits but one is not an ordinary appellate substitute for the other.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain the Enfield cartridge issue as the immediate cause of the revolt, distinguishing it from long-term grievances.
Worked solution / model answer
Cartridges were believed to be greased with cow/pig fat; biting them offended Hindu/Muslim religious beliefs; this triggered existing resentment rather than creating all discontent by itself.
(2) [3]
What was the General Service Enlistment Act, and why did some sepoys resent it? Give the policy and two related concerns.
Worked solution / model answer
The 1856 measure required new recruits to accept overseas service when ordered. Crossing seas conflicted with some religious/caste beliefs; it increased anxiety about British interference and service conditions. Explain resentment without claiming every soldier shared one belief.
(3) [4]
Explain four reasons for the revolt’s failure.
Worked solution / model answer
Limited territorial spread; lack of unified leadership and common political programme; superior British organisation/resources/communications; lack of sustained support from several princes and groups. Support each reason with its practical effect.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Compare Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience in their central method and one major starting event each.
Worked solution / model answer
Non-Cooperation withdrew support from institutions and began amid Rowlatt/Punjab/Khilafat grievances; Civil Disobedience deliberately broke selected unjust laws, notably salt law through the Dandi March in 1930. Both were intended to be non-violent.
(2) [3]
Explain why the Salt March was an effective beginning for Civil Disobedience.
Worked solution / model answer
Salt affected ordinary people across classes; breaking the law provided a clear non-violent act against colonial authority; the march mobilised public attention and widened participation.
(3) [4]
State four clauses of the Gandhi-Irwin Pact, including at least one Congress undertaking.
Worked solution / model answer
Government undertook release of political prisoners not convicted of violence, withdrawal of ordinances/prosecutions, restoration of eligible confiscated property and permission for peaceful salt collection/picketing. Congress agreed to suspend Civil Disobedience and attend the Second Round Table Conference. Any four accurate clauses, with both sides represented.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain why the INA trials had political significance beyond the legal charges.
Worked solution / model answer
They generated widespread nationalist sympathy; united different sections in support; intensified disaffection and challenged colonial legitimacy despite the INA’s military defeat.
(2) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(3) [4]
Explain four contributions of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA to the freedom struggle.
Worked solution / model answer
Leadership and reorganisation of INA; formation of the Provisional Government of Azad Hind; mobilisation of Indians overseas and the military campaign; political impact of INA trials and patriotic response. Do not claim that INA alone secured independence militarily.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain the policy of appeasement and two reasons it encouraged aggression.
Worked solution / model answer
Concessions were made to aggressive powers in hope of preserving peace; failure to enforce limits encouraged further demands; it weakened collective resistance and gave aggressors time/confidence to expand.
(2) [3]
Compare the League of Nations and the United Nations in origin and purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
League followed WWI; UN followed WWII; both aimed at peace/cooperation but differed in structure and enforcement arrangements. Their existence did not automatically guarantee collective action.
(3) [4]
Explain four consequences of the Second World War.
Worked solution / model answer
Defeat of Axis powers; huge human/material loss; formation of the United Nations; emergence of US/USSR rivalry and the Cold War, with decolonisation accelerated in the wider aftermath. Use four distinct consequences.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
State three purposes of the United Nations.
Worked solution / model answer
Maintain international peace/security; develop friendly relations among nations; cooperate on economic/social/humanitarian problems and human rights. Explain each distinct purpose.
(2) [3]
State three functions of the General Assembly.
Worked solution / model answer
Discuss issues within the Charter and make recommendations; approve the budget/apportion expenses; elect or participate in elections of members of UN organs under the Charter. It does not possess the same binding enforcement powers as the Security Council.
(3) [4]
Explain why the UN and NAM are not interchangeable organisations using four distinctions.
Worked solution / model answer
UN is a universal Charter-based international organisation; NAM is a grouping centred on independent non-bloc policy. Their membership principles differ; UN has formal principal organs such as the Council/ICJ; NAM does not replace those Charter mechanisms or hold the Council’s enforcement powers.
