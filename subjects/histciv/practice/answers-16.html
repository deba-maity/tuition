Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
State three features explaining why the Rajya Sabha is called a permanent house.
Worked solution / model answer
It is not dissolved as a whole; members have staggered six-year terms; approximately one-third retire every two years.
(2) [3]
Give three reasons why the Lok Sabha exercises stronger control over the Union Council of Ministers than the Rajya Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
The Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha; a no-confidence motion there can require resignation; the Lok Sabha controls grants and the Money Bill process.
(3) [4]
Explain why the formal executive head and the politically responsible government are different in India. Link the President, Prime Minister and Lok Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
President is constitutional head; Council headed by PM ordinarily provides aid/advice and directs policy; Council’s collective responsibility to Lok Sabha links executive government to elected-house confidence.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
State three legislative powers of the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Summoning/proroguing Parliament; addressing Parliament as prescribed and sending messages; assent to bills and promulgation of ordinances when constitutional conditions are met. Dissolution of Lok Sabha is another accepted relevant point.
(2) [3]
Explain how the President is the constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise effective executive authority.
Worked solution / model answer
Executive acts are formally taken in the President’s name; the President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council; the Prime Minister heads the Council and directs the government enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.
(3) [4]
Give four ways in which the Prime Minister leads the Union government.
Worked solution / model answer
Selects/recommends ministers; allocates or coordinates portfolios and policy; chairs Cabinet meetings; acts as the main link between President and Council and communicates decisions. Leading the parliamentary majority is an additional relevant point.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
State three constitutional qualifications for appointment as a Supreme Court judge.
Worked solution / model answer
Indian citizenship plus the prescribed judicial/advocacy qualification: at least five years as a High Court judge, at least ten years as a High Court advocate, or a distinguished jurist in the President’s opinion. Do not treat all alternative professional qualifications as simultaneously compulsory.
(2) [3]
A person is unlawfully detained. Name the relevant writ and explain its purpose. Contrast it with mandamus.
Worked solution / model answer
Habeas corpus requires production of the detained person and examination of the legality of detention; it protects personal liberty. Mandamus directs a public authority to perform a public/legal duty it has failed to perform.
(3) [4]
Compare presidential clemency and a court’s judicial review. Why are these not interchangeable?
Worked solution / model answer
Clemency affects punishment through a constitutional executive power; judicial review examines legal/constitutional validity. Both have defined limits but one is not an ordinary appellate substitute for the other.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
What was the General Service Enlistment Act, and why did some sepoys resent it? Give the policy and two related concerns.
Worked solution / model answer
The 1856 measure required new recruits to accept overseas service when ordered. Crossing seas conflicted with some religious/caste beliefs; it increased anxiety about British interference and service conditions. Explain resentment without claiming every soldier shared one belief.
(2) [3]
Explain the Enfield cartridge issue as the immediate cause of the revolt, distinguishing it from long-term grievances.
Worked solution / model answer
Cartridges were believed to be greased with cow/pig fat; biting them offended Hindu/Muslim religious beliefs; this triggered existing resentment rather than creating all discontent by itself.
(3) [4]
Compare the role of armed rebellion in 1857 with the intended discipline of Gandhian mass movements. Use methods, leadership and participation.
Worked solution / model answer
1857 involved military/civil rebellion with varied regional leadership and motives; Gandhian movements sought disciplined non-violent mass mobilisation. Both challenged colonial rule, but methods, coordination and political setting differed.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Give three causes of the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Worked solution / model answer
Rowlatt Act repression; Jallianwala Bagh and the Punjab wrongs; the Khilafat grievance and disillusionment with post-war policy. Explain the connection to withdrawal of cooperation.
(2) [3]
Compare Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience in their central method and one major starting event each.
Worked solution / model answer
Non-Cooperation withdrew support from institutions and began amid Rowlatt/Punjab/Khilafat grievances; Civil Disobedience deliberately broke selected unjust laws, notably salt law through the Dandi March in 1930. Both were intended to be non-violent.
(3) [4]
Give four causes or immediate circumstances leading to Quit India in 1942.
Worked solution / model answer
Failure of the Cripps Mission; wartime hardships and discontent; threat of Japanese advance; demand for an immediate end to British rule rather than an uncertain post-war promise. Explain their combined effect.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain why the INA trials had political significance beyond the legal charges.
Worked solution / model answer
They generated widespread nationalist sympathy; united different sections in support; intensified disaffection and challenged colonial legitimacy despite the INA’s military defeat.
(2) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(3) [4]
State four provisions of the Indian Independence Act of 1947.
Worked solution / model answer
Creation of two independent dominions from 15 August 1947; end of British sovereignty/control; constituent assemblies gained legislative authority pending constitutions; British paramountcy over princely states lapsed. Relevant provisions must not be confused with unrelated later republican arrangements.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
State three common ideological features of Fascism and Nazism.
Worked solution / model answer
Dictatorial one-party leadership; aggressive nationalism and militarism; suppression of opposition and individual freedoms. Nazism’s racial ideology must not be treated as identical in every detail to Italian Fascism.
(2) [3]
Compare the League of Nations and the United Nations in origin and purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
League followed WWI; UN followed WWII; both aimed at peace/cooperation but differed in structure and enforcement arrangements. Their existence did not automatically guarantee collective action.
(3) [4]
Explain how experience of two world wars encouraged both international organisation and a desire to avoid power-bloc domination. Distinguish the UN from NAM.
Worked solution / model answer
War destruction encouraged collective peace/security through the UN; later Cold War rivalry led NAM members to independent non-bloc judgement. The organisations arose in related international conditions but have different structures and roles.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
A student says “non-alignment means never taking a position.” Correct the statement with three points.
Worked solution / model answer
It rejects automatic alignment with a power bloc; members judge issues on their merits; active support for peace, decolonisation and cooperation is consistent with non-alignment.
(2) [3]
State three purposes of the United Nations.
Worked solution / model answer
Maintain international peace/security; develop friendly relations among nations; cooperate on economic/social/humanitarian problems and human rights. Explain each distinct purpose.
(3) [4]
Describe the composition and voting of the Security Council using four points.
Worked solution / model answer
Fifteen members; five permanent members; ten elected non-permanent members with two-year terms; substantive decisions require the prescribed majority without a negative vote/veto by a permanent member. Do not say every member has veto power.
