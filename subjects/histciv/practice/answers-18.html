Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
Distinguish an ordinary bill from a Money Bill with reference to introduction, Rajya Sabha powers and a joint sitting.
Worked solution / model answer
An ordinary bill may generally originate in either house; a Money Bill originates only in Lok Sabha on the required recommendation. Both houses have substantial ordinary-bill powers, while Rajya Sabha only recommends on a Money Bill. Joint sitting provisions address specified ordinary-bill deadlocks, not Money Bills.
(2) [3]
A Money Bill has been passed by the Lok Sabha. State three limitations on the Rajya Sabha’s role in dealing with it.
Worked solution / model answer
The Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend it as an equal chamber; it can recommend changes; it must return it within 14 days, after which the constitutional Money Bill procedure allows passage without its return. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject its recommendations.
(3) [4]
Explain why the formal executive head and the politically responsible government are different in India. Link the President, Prime Minister and Lok Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
President is constitutional head; Council headed by PM ordinarily provides aid/advice and directs policy; Council’s collective responsibility to Lok Sabha links executive government to elected-house confidence.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain how the President is the constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise effective executive authority.
Worked solution / model answer
Executive acts are formally taken in the President’s name; the President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council; the Prime Minister heads the Council and directs the government enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.
(2) [3]
Give three financial powers or functions associated with the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Prior recommendation for introduction of a Money Bill; the annual financial statement is laid before Parliament in the President’s name; appointment of the Finance Commission. Authorisation of advances from the Contingency Fund is also relevant.
(3) [4]
Give four ways in which the Prime Minister leads the Union government.
Worked solution / model answer
Selects/recommends ministers; allocates or coordinates portfolios and policy; chairs Cabinet meetings; acts as the main link between President and Council and communicates decisions. Leading the parliamentary majority is an additional relevant point.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
Distinguish original, appellate and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
Worked solution / model answer
Original: specified cases begin there, including certain federal disputes and rights remedies; appellate: hears eligible appeals from lower courts; advisory: the President may seek an opinion under Article 143, which is advisory rather than an ordinary executable judgment.
(2) [3]
State who appoints High Court judges, the retirement age, and one relevant professional qualification.
Worked solution / model answer
The President appoints them through the constitutional process; retirement age is 62; at least ten years’ judicial office in India or at least ten years as an advocate of a High Court is a relevant alternative, along with citizenship.
(3) [4]
Distinguish the Supreme Court and High Courts with four carefully matched points.
Worked solution / model answer
Supreme Court is the national apex; High Courts head state/territorial judicial systems. Retirement ages are 65 and 62. Supreme Court rights writ power is under Article 32, High Courts under Article 226 with a wider purpose scope. Their territorial and appellate positions differ within the integrated system.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
Compare political grievances of rulers with military grievances of sepoys in the revolt.
Worked solution / model answer
Rulers feared annexation/loss of authority and honours; sepoys resented unequal pay/promotion and service changes; the cartridge issue connected military grievance with religious fear. Different groups joined for overlapping, not identical, motives.
(2) [3]
Explain three socio-religious causes of resentment before 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Fear of conversion linked with missionary activity; suspicion of interference with customs and inheritance laws; racial discrimination and anxiety about unfamiliar reforms. Show how fear and distrust connected these developments.
(3) [4]
Explain four reasons for the revolt’s failure.
Worked solution / model answer
Limited territorial spread; lack of unified leadership and common political programme; superior British organisation/resources/communications; lack of sustained support from several princes and groups. Support each reason with its practical effect.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Why was Non-Cooperation withdrawn after Chauri Chaura? State the incident’s significance and Gandhi’s reasoning.
Worked solution / model answer
Protesters killed policemen after setting a police station on fire; violence contradicted the movement’s non-violent discipline; Gandhi suspended the movement because preparation/self-restraint were inadequate for the planned struggle.
(2) [3]
Give three causes of the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Worked solution / model answer
Rowlatt Act repression; Jallianwala Bagh and the Punjab wrongs; the Khilafat grievance and disillusionment with post-war policy. Explain the connection to withdrawal of cooperation.
(3) [4]
Give four causes or immediate circumstances leading to Quit India in 1942.
Worked solution / model answer
Failure of the Cripps Mission; wartime hardships and discontent; threat of Japanese advance; demand for an immediate end to British rule rather than an uncertain post-war promise. Explain their combined effect.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(2) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(3) [4]
State four provisions of the Indian Independence Act of 1947.
Worked solution / model answer
Creation of two independent dominions from 15 August 1947; end of British sovereignty/control; constituent assemblies gained legislative authority pending constitutions; British paramountcy over princely states lapsed. Relevant provisions must not be confused with unrelated later republican arrangements.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
State three ways the Treaty of Versailles weakened Germany.
Worked solution / model answer
Territorial losses; military restrictions; reparations and war-guilt provisions. Explain the burden or humiliation of each rather than writing “harsh treaty” three times.
(2) [3]
Compare the League of Nations and the United Nations in origin and purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
League followed WWI; UN followed WWII; both aimed at peace/cooperation but differed in structure and enforcement arrangements. Their existence did not automatically guarantee collective action.
(3) [4]
Explain four factors helping the rise of dictatorships in inter-war Europe.
Worked solution / model answer
Economic distress/unemployment; resentment over peace settlements or disappointed national aims; weak parliamentary governments; fear of communism and propaganda/paramilitary methods. Relate factors accurately to Germany/Italy as appropriate.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
State three functions of the General Assembly.
Worked solution / model answer
Discuss issues within the Charter and make recommendations; approve the budget/apportion expenses; elect or participate in elections of members of UN organs under the Charter. It does not possess the same binding enforcement powers as the Security Council.
(2) [3]
A student says “non-alignment means never taking a position.” Correct the statement with three points.
Worked solution / model answer
It rejects automatic alignment with a power bloc; members judge issues on their merits; active support for peace, decolonisation and cooperation is consistent with non-alignment.
(3) [4]
Give one principal field of work and one concrete type of contribution each for WHO and UNESCO.
Worked solution / model answer
WHO: international public health, such as disease-control coordination/health standards; UNESCO: education, science and culture, such as heritage protection/literacy and educational cooperation. Two accurate field-example pairs earn four points.
