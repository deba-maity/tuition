Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
Distinguish an ordinary bill from a Money Bill with reference to introduction, Rajya Sabha powers and a joint sitting.
Worked solution / model answer
An ordinary bill may generally originate in either house; a Money Bill originates only in Lok Sabha on the required recommendation. Both houses have substantial ordinary-bill powers, while Rajya Sabha only recommends on a Money Bill. Joint sitting provisions address specified ordinary-bill deadlocks, not Money Bills.
(2) [3]
A member wants to ask the government for an explanation of an urgent matter. Explain Question Hour, an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion as different accountability devices.
Worked solution / model answer
Question Hour permits questions to ministers; an adjournment motion interrupts normal business for a definite urgent public matter under the rules; a no-confidence motion tests the Lok Sabha’s confidence in the Council of Ministers.
(3) [4]
Distinguish the roles of the Speaker and the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha using four points.
Worked solution / model answer
The Speaker is elected by Lok Sabha members; the Vice-President is ex officio Rajya Sabha Chairman. They preside over their respective houses and enforce procedure. The Speaker certifies a Money Bill. A joint sitting is normally presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker, not by the Rajya Sabha Chairman.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
State three legislative powers of the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Summoning/proroguing Parliament; addressing Parliament as prescribed and sending messages; assent to bills and promulgation of ordinances when constitutional conditions are met. Dissolution of Lok Sabha is another accepted relevant point.
(2) [3]
Explain how the President is the constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise effective executive authority.
Worked solution / model answer
Executive acts are formally taken in the President’s name; the President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council; the Prime Minister heads the Council and directs the government enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.
(3) [4]
Explain four steps/features of the constitutional impeachment process for the President.
Worked solution / model answer
A charge of violation of the Constitution may originate in either house; the prescribed notice/signature and special-majority requirements apply; the other house investigates or causes investigation with a right of defence; a resolution supported by at least two-thirds of that house’s total membership removes the President. The initiating resolution also requires the constitutional two-thirds-total-membership majority.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
Distinguish original, appellate and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
Worked solution / model answer
Original: specified cases begin there, including certain federal disputes and rights remedies; appellate: hears eligible appeals from lower courts; advisory: the President may seek an opinion under Article 143, which is advisory rather than an ordinary executable judgment.
(2) [3]
State who appoints High Court judges, the retirement age, and one relevant professional qualification.
Worked solution / model answer
The President appoints them through the constitutional process; retirement age is 62; at least ten years’ judicial office in India or at least ten years as an advocate of a High Court is a relevant alternative, along with citizenship.
(3) [4]
Give four advantages of Lok Adalats in suitable disputes.
Worked solution / model answer
They promote settlement by conciliation; reduce delay; reduce expense; ease the ordinary courts’ burden and improve access. Their role should not be described as a universal replacement for trials in every kind of case.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three ways in which British economic policies contributed to the Revolt of 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Heavy revenue demands burdened cultivators; decline of crafts/competition from British manufactures hurt artisans; annexations and loss of patronage affected dependants of Indian courts. Link each policy to the affected group.
(2) [3]
Explain the Enfield cartridge issue as the immediate cause of the revolt, distinguishing it from long-term grievances.
Worked solution / model answer
Cartridges were believed to be greased with cow/pig fat; biting them offended Hindu/Muslim religious beliefs; this triggered existing resentment rather than creating all discontent by itself.
(3) [4]
Give four army changes introduced after the revolt and explain their purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
Higher European troop proportion; artillery largely retained under European control; recruitment reorganised to prevent broad unity; different communities/regions balanced within forces. These changes aimed to reduce the risk of another united military rebellion.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three contributions of the press to the growth of Indian nationalism.
Worked solution / model answer
It criticised exploitative policies; spread political ideas and information across regions; linked educated opinion and exposed common grievances, supporting a sense of shared national interest.
(2) [3]
Explain the official reason and nationalist interpretation of the 1905 Partition of Bengal, and identify the Viceroy associated with it.
Worked solution / model answer
Officially administrative convenience; nationalists saw a divide-and-rule attempt to weaken unity, including religious division; Lord Curzon was the Viceroy.
(3) [4]
Compare Early and Assertive Nationalists using aims, methods, attitude to British rule and mass participation.
Worked solution / model answer
Early leaders pursued constitutional reform through petitions, resolutions and persuasion, often trusting liberal British opinion; Assertive leaders stressed self-reliance and Swaraj, boycott/Swadeshi/national education and broader mobilisation. Avoid describing all Early leaders as loyal to every colonial policy.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(2) [3]
Explain why the INA trials had political significance beyond the legal charges.
Worked solution / model answer
They generated widespread nationalist sympathy; united different sections in support; intensified disaffection and challenged colonial legitimacy despite the INA’s military defeat.
(3) [4]
Explain four contributions of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA to the freedom struggle.
Worked solution / model answer
Leadership and reorganisation of INA; formation of the Provisional Government of Azad Hind; mobilisation of Indians overseas and the military campaign; political impact of INA trials and patriotic response. Do not claim that INA alone secured independence militarily.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
State three common ideological features of Fascism and Nazism.
Worked solution / model answer
Dictatorial one-party leadership; aggressive nationalism and militarism; suppression of opposition and individual freedoms. Nazism’s racial ideology must not be treated as identical in every detail to Italian Fascism.
(2) [3]
Explain the policy of appeasement and two reasons it encouraged aggression.
Worked solution / model answer
Concessions were made to aggressive powers in hope of preserving peace; failure to enforce limits encouraged further demands; it weakened collective resistance and gave aggressors time/confidence to expand.
(3) [4]
Explain four long-term causes of the First World War.
Worked solution / model answer
Aggressive nationalism; imperial/colonial rivalry; militarism and arms race; rival alliance systems. Link each to growing distrust and the spread of conflict.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
Name three architects of the Non-Aligned Movement and explain its basic position.
Worked solution / model answer
Nehru, Tito and Nasser are accepted architects (others include Sukarno and Nkrumah). NAM sought independent judgement rather than membership of Cold War power blocs; it did not require indifference to world affairs.
(2) [3]
State the composition and principal judicial role of the International Court of Justice.
Worked solution / model answer
Fifteen judges; elected for nine-year terms through General Assembly and Security Council; settles submitted legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions when properly requested. It is not a criminal trial court for ordinary individual offences.
(3) [4]
Give one principal field of work and one concrete type of contribution each for WHO and UNESCO.
Worked solution / model answer
WHO: international public health, such as disease-control coordination/health standards; UNESCO: education, science and culture, such as heritage protection/literacy and educational cooperation. Two accurate field-example pairs earn four points.
