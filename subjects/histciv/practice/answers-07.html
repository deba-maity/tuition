Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
A member wants to ask the government for an explanation of an urgent matter. Explain Question Hour, an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion as different accountability devices.
Worked solution / model answer
Question Hour permits questions to ministers; an adjournment motion interrupts normal business for a definite urgent public matter under the rules; a no-confidence motion tests the Lok Sabha’s confidence in the Council of Ministers.
(2) [3]
A Money Bill has been passed by the Lok Sabha. State three limitations on the Rajya Sabha’s role in dealing with it.
Worked solution / model answer
The Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend it as an equal chamber; it can recommend changes; it must return it within 14 days, after which the constitutional Money Bill procedure allows passage without its return. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject its recommendations.
(3) [4]
Explain two exclusive powers of the Rajya Sabha and distinguish them from two powers shared by both houses.
Worked solution / model answer
It may authorise Parliament to legislate on a State List matter in the national interest and authorise creation of an All India Service by the required special resolution. Both houses participate in constitutional amendment and impeachment of the President; those are not exclusive Rajya Sabha powers.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
State three legislative powers of the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Summoning/proroguing Parliament; addressing Parliament as prescribed and sending messages; assent to bills and promulgation of ordinances when constitutional conditions are met. Dissolution of Lok Sabha is another accepted relevant point.
(2) [3]
Give three financial powers or functions associated with the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Prior recommendation for introduction of a Money Bill; the annual financial statement is laid before Parliament in the President’s name; appointment of the Finance Commission. Authorisation of advances from the Contingency Fund is also relevant.
(3) [4]
Classify these presidential functions and explain each classification: appointing ministers, assenting to a bill, granting a pardon, and appointing the Finance Commission.
Worked solution / model answer
Appointment: executive; assent: legislative; pardon: judicial/clemency; Finance Commission: financial. Give one explanatory clause linking each action to its category rather than four unexplained labels.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
A person is unlawfully detained. Name the relevant writ and explain its purpose. Contrast it with mandamus.
Worked solution / model answer
Habeas corpus requires production of the detained person and examination of the legality of detention; it protects personal liberty. Mandamus directs a public authority to perform a public/legal duty it has failed to perform.
(2) [3]
State who appoints High Court judges, the retirement age, and one relevant professional qualification.
Worked solution / model answer
The President appoints them through the constitutional process; retirement age is 62; at least ten years’ judicial office in India or at least ten years as an advocate of a High Court is a relevant alternative, along with citizenship.
(3) [4]
Explain judicial review and name three kinds of government action that may raise a constitutional challenge.
Worked solution / model answer
Judicial review examines conformity with the Constitution. A law allegedly violating fundamental rights, legislation outside the enacting body’s competence, or unconstitutional executive action may be reviewed. Courts do not replace policy merely because they prefer another policy.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
What was the General Service Enlistment Act, and why did some sepoys resent it? Give the policy and two related concerns.
Worked solution / model answer
The 1856 measure required new recruits to accept overseas service when ordered. Crossing seas conflicted with some religious/caste beliefs; it increased anxiety about British interference and service conditions. Explain resentment without claiming every soldier shared one belief.
(2) [3]
Explain three socio-religious causes of resentment before 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Fear of conversion linked with missionary activity; suspicion of interference with customs and inheritance laws; racial discrimination and anxiety about unfamiliar reforms. Show how fear and distrust connected these developments.
(3) [4]
Compare the role of armed rebellion in 1857 with the intended discipline of Gandhian mass movements. Use methods, leadership and participation.
Worked solution / model answer
1857 involved military/civil rebellion with varied regional leadership and motives; Gandhian movements sought disciplined non-violent mass mobilisation. Both challenged colonial rule, but methods, coordination and political setting differed.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three contributions of the press to the growth of Indian nationalism.
Worked solution / model answer
It criticised exploitative policies; spread political ideas and information across regions; linked educated opinion and exposed common grievances, supporting a sense of shared national interest.
(2) [3]
Explain the official reason and nationalist interpretation of the 1905 Partition of Bengal, and identify the Viceroy associated with it.
Worked solution / model answer
Officially administrative convenience; nationalists saw a divide-and-rule attempt to weaken unity, including religious division; Lord Curzon was the Viceroy.
(3) [4]
Give four contributions of an Assertive Nationalist leader, naming the leader first.
Worked solution / model answer
For Tilak: popular festivals for political mobilisation; newspapers Kesari/Mahratta; insistence on Swaraj; support for Swadeshi/boycott/national education and national awareness. Accept another syllabus leader with four accurate matched contributions.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(2) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(3) [4]
Explain four contributions of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA to the freedom struggle.
Worked solution / model answer
Leadership and reorganisation of INA; formation of the Provisional Government of Azad Hind; mobilisation of Indians overseas and the military campaign; political impact of INA trials and patriotic response. Do not claim that INA alone secured independence militarily.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
State three ways the Treaty of Versailles weakened Germany.
Worked solution / model answer
Territorial losses; military restrictions; reparations and war-guilt provisions. Explain the burden or humiliation of each rather than writing “harsh treaty” three times.
(2) [3]
Compare the League of Nations and the United Nations in origin and purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
League followed WWI; UN followed WWII; both aimed at peace/cooperation but differed in structure and enforcement arrangements. Their existence did not automatically guarantee collective action.
(3) [4]
Explain four long-term causes of the First World War.
Worked solution / model answer
Aggressive nationalism; imperial/colonial rivalry; militarism and arms race; rival alliance systems. Link each to growing distrust and the spread of conflict.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
A student says “non-alignment means never taking a position.” Correct the statement with three points.
Worked solution / model answer
It rejects automatic alignment with a power bloc; members judge issues on their merits; active support for peace, decolonisation and cooperation is consistent with non-alignment.
(2) [3]
State three functions of the General Assembly.
Worked solution / model answer
Discuss issues within the Charter and make recommendations; approve the budget/apportion expenses; elect or participate in elections of members of UN organs under the Charter. It does not possess the same binding enforcement powers as the Security Council.
(3) [4]
Explain why the UN and NAM are not interchangeable organisations using four distinctions.
Worked solution / model answer
UN is a universal Charter-based international organisation; NAM is a grouping centred on independent non-bloc policy. Their membership principles differ; UN has formal principal organs such as the Council/ICJ; NAM does not replace those Charter mechanisms or hold the Council’s enforcement powers.
