Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
A member wants to ask the government for an explanation of an urgent matter. Explain Question Hour, an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion as different accountability devices.
Worked solution / model answer
Question Hour permits questions to ministers; an adjournment motion interrupts normal business for a definite urgent public matter under the rules; a no-confidence motion tests the Lok Sabha’s confidence in the Council of Ministers.
(2) [3]
State three features explaining why the Rajya Sabha is called a permanent house.
Worked solution / model answer
It is not dissolved as a whole; members have staggered six-year terms; approximately one-third retire every two years.
(3) [4]
Distinguish the roles of the Speaker and the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha using four points.
Worked solution / model answer
The Speaker is elected by Lok Sabha members; the Vice-President is ex officio Rajya Sabha Chairman. They preside over their respective houses and enforce procedure. The Speaker certifies a Money Bill. A joint sitting is normally presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker, not by the Rajya Sabha Chairman.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
State three legislative powers of the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Summoning/proroguing Parliament; addressing Parliament as prescribed and sending messages; assent to bills and promulgation of ordinances when constitutional conditions are met. Dissolution of Lok Sabha is another accepted relevant point.
(2) [3]
Explain collective responsibility of ministers to the Lok Sabha using a concrete consequence for a lost confidence vote.
Worked solution / model answer
Ministers publicly support Cabinet decisions as one team; the Council remains in office while it enjoys Lok Sabha confidence; loss of confidence requires the Council as a whole to resign, not merely one minister.
(3) [4]
Classify these presidential functions and explain each classification: appointing ministers, assenting to a bill, granting a pardon, and appointing the Finance Commission.
Worked solution / model answer
Appointment: executive; assent: legislative; pardon: judicial/clemency; Finance Commission: financial. Give one explanatory clause linking each action to its category rather than four unexplained labels.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
A person is unlawfully detained. Name the relevant writ and explain its purpose. Contrast it with mandamus.
Worked solution / model answer
Habeas corpus requires production of the detained person and examination of the legality of detention; it protects personal liberty. Mandamus directs a public authority to perform a public/legal duty it has failed to perform.
(2) [3]
State who appoints High Court judges, the retirement age, and one relevant professional qualification.
Worked solution / model answer
The President appoints them through the constitutional process; retirement age is 62; at least ten years’ judicial office in India or at least ten years as an advocate of a High Court is a relevant alternative, along with citizenship.
(3) [4]
Give four advantages of Lok Adalats in suitable disputes.
Worked solution / model answer
They promote settlement by conciliation; reduce delay; reduce expense; ease the ordinary courts’ burden and improve access. Their role should not be described as a universal replacement for trials in every kind of case.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three ways in which British economic policies contributed to the Revolt of 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Heavy revenue demands burdened cultivators; decline of crafts/competition from British manufactures hurt artisans; annexations and loss of patronage affected dependants of Indian courts. Link each policy to the affected group.
(2) [3]
Explain three socio-religious causes of resentment before 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Fear of conversion linked with missionary activity; suspicion of interference with customs and inheritance laws; racial discrimination and anxiety about unfamiliar reforms. Show how fear and distrust connected these developments.
(3) [4]
Give four army changes introduced after the revolt and explain their purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
Higher European troop proportion; artillery largely retained under European control; recruitment reorganised to prevent broad unity; different communities/regions balanced within forces. These changes aimed to reduce the risk of another united military rebellion.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
State three early objectives of the Indian National Congress.
Worked solution / model answer
Promote contacts and unity among political workers; develop national feeling beyond regional/religious divisions; formulate and present common demands and train public opinion.
(2) [3]
Explain the official reason and nationalist interpretation of the 1905 Partition of Bengal, and identify the Viceroy associated with it.
Worked solution / model answer
Officially administrative convenience; nationalists saw a divide-and-rule attempt to weaken unity, including religious division; Lord Curzon was the Viceroy.
(3) [4]
Explain four ways the Partition of Bengal stimulated the national movement.
Worked solution / model answer
It was opposed as division of a politically active region; boycott of foreign goods expanded; Swadeshi industries/education received support; new public participation and assertive methods developed. Explain events and responses rather than merely listing dates.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(2) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(3) [4]
Explain four contributions of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA to the freedom struggle.
Worked solution / model answer
Leadership and reorganisation of INA; formation of the Provisional Government of Azad Hind; mobilisation of Indians overseas and the military campaign; political impact of INA trials and patriotic response. Do not claim that INA alone secured independence militarily.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
State three ways the Treaty of Versailles weakened Germany.
Worked solution / model answer
Territorial losses; military restrictions; reparations and war-guilt provisions. Explain the burden or humiliation of each rather than writing “harsh treaty” three times.
(2) [3]
State three common ideological features of Fascism and Nazism.
Worked solution / model answer
Dictatorial one-party leadership; aggressive nationalism and militarism; suppression of opposition and individual freedoms. Nazism’s racial ideology must not be treated as identical in every detail to Italian Fascism.
(3) [4]
Explain four causes of the Second World War.
Worked solution / model answer
Aggressive expansion by dictatorships; weaknesses/failure of collective security and the League; appeasement; grievances from Versailles and the immediate invasion of Poland. Distinguish long-term conditions from the immediate trigger.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
Name three architects of the Non-Aligned Movement and explain its basic position.
Worked solution / model answer
Nehru, Tito and Nasser are accepted architects (others include Sukarno and Nkrumah). NAM sought independent judgement rather than membership of Cold War power blocs; it did not require indifference to world affairs.
(2) [3]
State three functions of the General Assembly.
Worked solution / model answer
Discuss issues within the Charter and make recommendations; approve the budget/apportion expenses; elect or participate in elections of members of UN organs under the Charter. It does not possess the same binding enforcement powers as the Security Council.
(3) [4]
Describe the composition and voting of the Security Council using four points.
Worked solution / model answer
Fifteen members; five permanent members; ten elected non-permanent members with two-year terms; substantive decisions require the prescribed majority without a negative vote/veto by a permanent member. Do not say every member has veto power.
