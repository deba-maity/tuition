Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
A member wants to ask the government for an explanation of an urgent matter. Explain Question Hour, an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion as different accountability devices.
Worked solution / model answer
Question Hour permits questions to ministers; an adjournment motion interrupts normal business for a definite urgent public matter under the rules; a no-confidence motion tests the Lok Sabha’s confidence in the Council of Ministers.
(2) [3]
State three features explaining why the Rajya Sabha is called a permanent house.
Worked solution / model answer
It is not dissolved as a whole; members have staggered six-year terms; approximately one-third retire every two years.
(3) [4]
Explain why the formal executive head and the politically responsible government are different in India. Link the President, Prime Minister and Lok Sabha.
Worked solution / model answer
President is constitutional head; Council headed by PM ordinarily provides aid/advice and directs policy; Council’s collective responsibility to Lok Sabha links executive government to elected-house confidence.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain how the President is the constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise effective executive authority.
Worked solution / model answer
Executive acts are formally taken in the President’s name; the President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council; the Prime Minister heads the Council and directs the government enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.
(2) [3]
Give three financial powers or functions associated with the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Prior recommendation for introduction of a Money Bill; the annual financial statement is laid before Parliament in the President’s name; appointment of the Finance Commission. Authorisation of advances from the Contingency Fund is also relevant.
(3) [4]
Explain four steps/features of the constitutional impeachment process for the President.
Worked solution / model answer
A charge of violation of the Constitution may originate in either house; the prescribed notice/signature and special-majority requirements apply; the other house investigates or causes investigation with a right of defence; a resolution supported by at least two-thirds of that house’s total membership removes the President. The initiating resolution also requires the constitutional two-thirds-total-membership majority.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
Distinguish original, appellate and advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court.
Worked solution / model answer
Original: specified cases begin there, including certain federal disputes and rights remedies; appellate: hears eligible appeals from lower courts; advisory: the President may seek an opinion under Article 143, which is advisory rather than an ordinary executable judgment.
(2) [3]
A person is unlawfully detained. Name the relevant writ and explain its purpose. Contrast it with mandamus.
Worked solution / model answer
Habeas corpus requires production of the detained person and examination of the legality of detention; it protects personal liberty. Mandamus directs a public authority to perform a public/legal duty it has failed to perform.
(3) [4]
Compare presidential clemency and a court’s judicial review. Why are these not interchangeable?
Worked solution / model answer
Clemency affects punishment through a constitutional executive power; judicial review examines legal/constitutional validity. Both have defined limits but one is not an ordinary appellate substitute for the other.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
What was the General Service Enlistment Act, and why did some sepoys resent it? Give the policy and two related concerns.
Worked solution / model answer
The 1856 measure required new recruits to accept overseas service when ordered. Crossing seas conflicted with some religious/caste beliefs; it increased anxiety about British interference and service conditions. Explain resentment without claiming every soldier shared one belief.
(2) [3]
Explain three ways in which British economic policies contributed to the Revolt of 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Heavy revenue demands burdened cultivators; decline of crafts/competition from British manufactures hurt artisans; annexations and loss of patronage affected dependants of Indian courts. Link each policy to the affected group.
(3) [4]
Compare the role of armed rebellion in 1857 with the intended discipline of Gandhian mass movements. Use methods, leadership and participation.
Worked solution / model answer
1857 involved military/civil rebellion with varied regional leadership and motives; Gandhian movements sought disciplined non-violent mass mobilisation. Both challenged colonial rule, but methods, coordination and political setting differed.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
State three early objectives of the Indian National Congress.
Worked solution / model answer
Promote contacts and unity among political workers; develop national feeling beyond regional/religious divisions; formulate and present common demands and train public opinion.
(2) [3]
Explain the significance of Swadeshi, boycott and national education as related methods.
Worked solution / model answer
Swadeshi promoted indigenous goods; boycott withdrew support from imported goods/institutions; national education developed self-reliant institutions and national consciousness. Link all three to constructive and oppositional work.
(3) [4]
Compare Early and Assertive Nationalists using aims, methods, attitude to British rule and mass participation.
Worked solution / model answer
Early leaders pursued constitutional reform through petitions, resolutions and persuasion, often trusting liberal British opinion; Assertive leaders stressed self-reliance and Swaraj, boycott/Swadeshi/national education and broader mobilisation. Avoid describing all Early leaders as loyal to every colonial policy.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(2) [3]
Give three reasons Congress accepted partition under the 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Experience of obstruction/conflict in the interim government; fear of civil war and further bloodshed; belief that a smaller strong centre was preferable to an unworkable weak united arrangement; desire to avoid prolonged British rule. Any three explained reasons.
(3) [4]
State four provisions of the Indian Independence Act of 1947.
Worked solution / model answer
Creation of two independent dominions from 15 August 1947; end of British sovereignty/control; constituent assemblies gained legislative authority pending constitutions; British paramountcy over princely states lapsed. Relevant provisions must not be confused with unrelated later republican arrangements.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
State three ways the Treaty of Versailles weakened Germany.
Worked solution / model answer
Territorial losses; military restrictions; reparations and war-guilt provisions. Explain the burden or humiliation of each rather than writing “harsh treaty” three times.
(2) [3]
State three common ideological features of Fascism and Nazism.
Worked solution / model answer
Dictatorial one-party leadership; aggressive nationalism and militarism; suppression of opposition and individual freedoms. Nazism’s racial ideology must not be treated as identical in every detail to Italian Fascism.
(3) [4]
Explain how experience of two world wars encouraged both international organisation and a desire to avoid power-bloc domination. Distinguish the UN from NAM.
Worked solution / model answer
War destruction encouraged collective peace/security through the UN; later Cold War rivalry led NAM members to independent non-bloc judgement. The organisations arose in related international conditions but have different structures and roles.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
State three purposes of the United Nations.
Worked solution / model answer
Maintain international peace/security; develop friendly relations among nations; cooperate on economic/social/humanitarian problems and human rights. Explain each distinct purpose.
(2) [3]
State three functions of the General Assembly.
Worked solution / model answer
Discuss issues within the Charter and make recommendations; approve the budget/apportion expenses; elect or participate in elections of members of UN organs under the Charter. It does not possess the same binding enforcement powers as the Security Council.
(3) [4]
Explain four objectives/principles associated with NAM.
Worked solution / model answer
Independent foreign policy; opposition to colonialism/racism; peaceful coexistence and settlement of disputes; disarmament and reduced bloc confrontation. Explain how these differ from passive neutrality in every conflict.
