Part II A — Civics; 20 marks

Answer any 2 questions.

3 [10]
(1) [3]
A member wants to ask the government for an explanation of an urgent matter. Explain Question Hour, an adjournment motion and a no-confidence motion as different accountability devices.
Worked solution / model answer
Question Hour permits questions to ministers; an adjournment motion interrupts normal business for a definite urgent public matter under the rules; a no-confidence motion tests the Lok Sabha’s confidence in the Council of Ministers.
(2) [3]
A Money Bill has been passed by the Lok Sabha. State three limitations on the Rajya Sabha’s role in dealing with it.
Worked solution / model answer
The Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend it as an equal chamber; it can recommend changes; it must return it within 14 days, after which the constitutional Money Bill procedure allows passage without its return. The Lok Sabha may accept or reject its recommendations.
(3) [4]
Distinguish the roles of the Speaker and the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha using four points.
Worked solution / model answer
The Speaker is elected by Lok Sabha members; the Vice-President is ex officio Rajya Sabha Chairman. They preside over their respective houses and enforce procedure. The Speaker certifies a Money Bill. A joint sitting is normally presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker, not by the Rajya Sabha Chairman.
4 [10]
(1) [3]
Give three financial powers or functions associated with the President.
Worked solution / model answer
Prior recommendation for introduction of a Money Bill; the annual financial statement is laid before Parliament in the President’s name; appointment of the Finance Commission. Authorisation of advances from the Contingency Fund is also relevant.
(2) [3]
Explain how the President is the constitutional head while the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers exercise effective executive authority.
Worked solution / model answer
Executive acts are formally taken in the President’s name; the President ordinarily acts on the aid and advice of the Council; the Prime Minister heads the Council and directs the government enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.
(3) [4]
Explain four steps/features of the constitutional impeachment process for the President.
Worked solution / model answer
A charge of violation of the Constitution may originate in either house; the prescribed notice/signature and special-majority requirements apply; the other house investigates or causes investigation with a right of defence; a resolution supported by at least two-thirds of that house’s total membership removes the President. The initiating resolution also requires the constitutional two-thirds-total-membership majority.
5 [10]
(1) [3]
State three constitutional qualifications for appointment as a Supreme Court judge.
Worked solution / model answer
Indian citizenship plus the prescribed judicial/advocacy qualification: at least five years as a High Court judge, at least ten years as a High Court advocate, or a distinguished jurist in the President’s opinion. Do not treat all alternative professional qualifications as simultaneously compulsory.
(2) [3]
A person is unlawfully detained. Name the relevant writ and explain its purpose. Contrast it with mandamus.
Worked solution / model answer
Habeas corpus requires production of the detained person and examination of the legality of detention; it protects personal liberty. Mandamus directs a public authority to perform a public/legal duty it has failed to perform.
(3) [4]
Explain four safeguards of judicial independence.
Worked solution / model answer
Secure tenure with removal only by the prescribed process; protected service conditions/salaries as constitutionally provided; separation from routine executive direction in adjudication; restrictions on discussion of judicial conduct except appropriate removal proceedings. Post-retirement practice restrictions for Supreme Court judges are another relevant safeguard.

Part II B — History; 30 marks

Answer any 3 questions.

6 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three ways in which British economic policies contributed to the Revolt of 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Heavy revenue demands burdened cultivators; decline of crafts/competition from British manufactures hurt artisans; annexations and loss of patronage affected dependants of Indian courts. Link each policy to the affected group.
(2) [3]
Explain three socio-religious causes of resentment before 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Fear of conversion linked with missionary activity; suspicion of interference with customs and inheritance laws; racial discrimination and anxiety about unfamiliar reforms. Show how fear and distrust connected these developments.
(3) [4]
Explain four changes in British policy or administration after the Revolt of 1857.
Worked solution / model answer
Company rule ended and authority passed to the Crown; policy reassured princes and renounced indiscriminate annexation; assurances were given about religious non-interference; the army was reorganised with a higher European proportion and changed recruitment/command arrangements.
7 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain three contributions of the press to the growth of Indian nationalism.
Worked solution / model answer
It criticised exploitative policies; spread political ideas and information across regions; linked educated opinion and exposed common grievances, supporting a sense of shared national interest.
(2) [3]
Explain the significance of Swadeshi, boycott and national education as related methods.
Worked solution / model answer
Swadeshi promoted indigenous goods; boycott withdrew support from imported goods/institutions; national education developed self-reliant institutions and national consciousness. Link all three to constructive and oppositional work.
(3) [4]
Explain four factors contributing to the formation of the Muslim League.
Worked solution / model answer
British encouragement of separate political identities; elite concerns about representation; the Simla Deputation/separate-electorate demand; perceived need to protect Muslim political interests in the contemporary setting. Avoid presenting the community as having one uniform view.
8 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain why the INA trials had political significance beyond the legal charges.
Worked solution / model answer
They generated widespread nationalist sympathy; united different sections in support; intensified disaffection and challenged colonial legitimacy despite the INA’s military defeat.
(2) [3]
Name the last Viceroy and explain two terms of his June 1947 plan.
Worked solution / model answer
Lord Mountbatten; partition arrangements for British India with relevant provincial decisions; transfer of power to successor dominions and lapse of paramountcy/princely-state arrangements. State two specific accurate terms.
(3) [4]
Explain four contributions of Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA to the freedom struggle.
Worked solution / model answer
Leadership and reorganisation of INA; formation of the Provisional Government of Azad Hind; mobilisation of Indians overseas and the military campaign; political impact of INA trials and patriotic response. Do not claim that INA alone secured independence militarily.
9 [10]
(1) [3]
Explain the Sarajevo crisis as the immediate cause of the First World War.
Worked solution / model answer
Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand at Sarajevo; Austro-Hungarian action/ultimatum against Serbia; alliance commitments and mobilisation widened the crisis into war. Distinguish trigger from underlying tensions.
(2) [3]
Compare the League of Nations and the United Nations in origin and purpose.
Worked solution / model answer
League followed WWI; UN followed WWII; both aimed at peace/cooperation but differed in structure and enforcement arrangements. Their existence did not automatically guarantee collective action.
(3) [4]
Explain how experience of two world wars encouraged both international organisation and a desire to avoid power-bloc domination. Distinguish the UN from NAM.
Worked solution / model answer
War destruction encouraged collective peace/security through the UN; later Cold War rivalry led NAM members to independent non-bloc judgement. The organisations arose in related international conditions but have different structures and roles.
10 [10]
(1) [3]
State three functions of the General Assembly.
Worked solution / model answer
Discuss issues within the Charter and make recommendations; approve the budget/apportion expenses; elect or participate in elections of members of UN organs under the Charter. It does not possess the same binding enforcement powers as the Security Council.
(2) [3]
State the composition and principal judicial role of the International Court of Justice.
Worked solution / model answer
Fifteen judges; elected for nine-year terms through General Assembly and Security Council; settles submitted legal disputes between states and gives advisory opinions when properly requested. It is not a criminal trial court for ordinary individual offences.
(3) [4]
Give one principal field of work and one concrete type of contribution each for WHO and UNESCO.
Worked solution / model answer
WHO: international public health, such as disease-control coordination/health standards; UNESCO: education, science and culture, such as heritage protection/literacy and educational cooperation. Two accurate field-example pairs earn four points.
