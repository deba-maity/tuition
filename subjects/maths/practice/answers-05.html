(2) [4]
For A=[[5,-2],[1,-5]] and B=[[3,7],[1,6]], calculate AB and BA. Decide whether these particular matrices commute.
Worked solution / model answer

For AB, multiply each row of A by each column of B. The entry (i,j) is the sum of the corresponding products.

AB[1,1]=5×(3)+(-2)×(1)=13.
AB[1,2]=5×(7)+(-2)×(6)=23.
AB[2,1]=1×(3)+(-5)×(1)=-2.
AB[2,2]=1×(7)+(-5)×(6)=-23.
AB=[[13, 23], [-2, -23]].
BA[1,1]=3×(5)+(7)×(1)=22.
BA[1,2]=3×(-2)+(7)×(-5)=-41.
BA[2,1]=1×(5)+(6)×(1)=11.
BA[2,2]=1×(-2)+(6)×(-5)=-32.
BA=[[22, -41], [11, -32]].
They do not commute because AB≠BA.

Check your work
Order matters: AB need not equal BA. For unknown entries, equate corresponding positions.

(2) [4]
The values 15, 45, 65 have frequencies 7, k, 11, respectively. Their mean is 545/12. Find k and the total frequency.
Worked solution / model answer
1. Mean=Σfx/Σf=(820+45k)/(18+k).
2. Put this equal to 545/12 and cross-multiply: 545/12(18+k)=820+45k.
3. Collect k terms: (545/12−45)k=820−545/12×18.
4. k=6; total frequency=7+6+11=24.
5. Substitution gives (820+45×6)/(18+6)=545/12, confirming the stated mean.

An observer’s eye is 1.8 m above level ground. The elevation of a tower top is 30°. After moving 64 m directly towards its base, the elevation is 60°. Find the tower’s height, correct to two decimal places.
Worked solution / model answer
1. Let the nearer horizontal distance be x m and height above eye level be H m.
2. From the nearer position, tan60°=H/x, so H=x√3.
3. From the farther position, tan30°=H/(x+64), so H=(x+64)/√3.
4. Equate: 3x=x+64; x=32 m. H=32√3 m.
5. Tower height=H+eye height=32√3+1.8=57.23 m.
