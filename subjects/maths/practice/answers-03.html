(2) [4]
For A=[[3,3],[-5,-1]] and B=[[3,0],[-1,4]], calculate AB and BA. Decide whether these particular matrices commute.
Worked solution / model answer

For AB, multiply each row of A by each column of B. The entry (i,j) is the sum of the corresponding products.

AB[1,1]=3×(3)+(3)×(-1)=6.
AB[1,2]=3×(0)+(3)×(4)=12.
AB[2,1]=-5×(3)+(-1)×(-1)=-14.
AB[2,2]=-5×(0)+(-1)×(4)=-4.
AB=[[6, 12], [-14, -4]].
BA[1,1]=3×(3)+(0)×(-5)=9.
BA[1,2]=3×(3)+(0)×(-1)=9.
BA[2,1]=-1×(3)+(4)×(-5)=-23.
BA[2,2]=-1×(3)+(4)×(-1)=-7.
BA=[[9, 9], [-23, -7]].
They do not commute because AB≠BA.

Check your work
Order matters: AB need not equal BA. For unknown entries, equate corresponding positions.

(3) [4]
The values 20, 45, 75 have frequencies 4, k, 14, respectively. Their mean is 1535/27. Find k and the total frequency.
Worked solution / model answer

For a frequency distribution calculate Σf and Σfx separately, then mean=Σfx/Σf. For grouped data use class marks or the stated interpolation rule.

1. Mean=Σfx/Σf=(1130+45k)/(18+k).
2. Put this equal to 1535/27 and cross-multiply: 1535/27(18+k)=1130+45k.
3. Collect k terms: (1535/27−45)k=1130−1535/27×18.
4. k=9; total frequency=4+9+14=27.
5. Substitution gives (1130+45×9)/(18+9)=1535/27, confirming the stated mean.

Check your work
Do not take an unweighted average of values with unequal frequencies.
