(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 60 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=30 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.
(2) [5]
Equal volumes (12.88 L each) of hydrogen chloride and nitrogen are measured at the same STP conditions. Compare their numbers of molecules and calculate their masses. Molar masses: 36.5 and 28 g/mol.
Worked solution / model answer

n=m/M; gas volume at the stipulated STP=22.4n litres; particles=n×6.022×10²³. Use the balanced-equation ratio when a reaction is involved.

By Avogadro’s law equal volumes at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. Each contains 0.575 mol. Masses=20.988 g and 16.1 g. Equal volume does not imply equal mass.
(3) [5]
Analysis of 154 g of a C/O compound gives 42 g carbon and 112 g oxygen. Its relative molecular mass is 44. Find empirical and molecular formulae. C=12, O=16.
Worked solution / model answer

Balance the equation, convert the known amount into moles, apply the coefficient ratio and convert the required moles into mass or gas volume.

Moles C=3.5, O=7. Divide by 3.5: C:O=1:2. Empirical formula CO2, mass 44. Molecular multiplier=1; formula C1O2. The empirical ratio can be multiplied by an integer; molecular mass is needed to select the multiplier.
(4) [5]

Worked solution / model answer
NaCl+AgNO₃→AgCl↓+NaNO₃; Ag⁺+Cl⁻→AgCl↓. White curdy precipitate of silver chloride. n(NaCl)=1; AgCl mass=143.5 g.
(5) [5]
A 12 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 44 cm³ of dilute HCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It conducts: ionisation gives mobile ions. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions. Ionic dissociation separates pre-existing ions; ionisation forms ions from molecules. Mere dissolution can leave neutral molecules.
