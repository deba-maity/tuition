(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 250 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=125 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.
(2) [5]
7.8 g zinc reacts completely with excess dilute HCl. Calculate the mass of ZnCl₂ and the hydrogen volume at STP. Include the equation and a gas test. Zn=65, Cl=35.5, H=1.
Worked solution / model answer
Zn+2HCl→ZnCl₂+H₂. Zn moles=0.12; ZnCl₂ mass=16.32 g; H₂ volume=2.688 L. A lighted splint produces a pop.
(3) [5]
117.3 g pure Al₂O₃ is completely electrolysed in the Hall-Héroult process. Calculate the theoretical aluminium mass. State the purpose of cryolite and why carbon anodes need replacement. Al=27, O=16.
Worked solution / model answer
One mole Al₂O₃ (102 g) supplies two mol Al (54 g). Aluminium mass=62.1 g. Cryolite lowers the working melting temperature and improves conductivity of the bath. Oxygen formed at the anode reacts with carbon, consuming the anode.

(5) [5]
A 15 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 53 cm³ of solid NaCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It does not conduct: its ions are fixed in the lattice. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.
