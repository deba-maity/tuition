(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 394 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=197 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.
(2) [5]
Consider 5.9 mol of H₂O. State the number of shared electron pairs in one molecule and lone pairs on its O/N/C/Cl atom as appropriate. Explain the covalent bond in terms of electron sharing. How many moles of shared pairs are represented?
Worked solution / model answer
One molecule has 2 shared pairs and 2 lone pairs on the stated non-hydrogen central/bonded atom. A shared pair is attracted to both bonded nuclei. Shared-pair amount=11.8 mol. This is sharing, not complete electron transfer.
(3) [5]
A 4 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 154 cm³ of aqueous NaCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It conducts: dissolved ions are mobile. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.
(4) [5]
A hydrocarbon is an acyclic alkene with one double bond and has 5 carbon atoms. Write its formula, calculate the H₂ moles needed to fully hydrogenate 5.4 mol, and name a suitable catalyst.
Worked solution / model answer
Formula C5H10. Each double bond takes 1 mol H₂ per mol, so H₂ needed=5.4 mol. Use nickel under suitable heating conditions.
(5) [5]
An atom X has atomic number 4 and mass number 8. Write its shell configuration, identify its period, give valence electrons and predict its usual valency. Does changing its mass number alone change its group?
Worked solution / model answer
Shells=[2, 2]. Period=2; valence electrons=2; usual valency=2. Mass number alone does not change electron configuration or group; isotopes have the same atomic number.
