(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 250 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=125 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.

Check your work
Copper anodes replenish Cu²⁺ in copper refining; inert anodes do not supply replacement copper ions.

(1) [3]
A 9 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 79 cm³ of solid NaCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It does not conduct: its ions are fixed in the lattice. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.

(3) [4]
A 2.6 cm³ sample containing Ca²⁺ ions is tested by adding NaOH dropwise, then in excess. Describe the observations, name/formulate the precipitate, and explain why “white precipitate” alone is not a unique identification.
Worked solution / model answer
Precipitate: white precipitate with sufficient NaOH; formula Ca(OH)₂. In excess: insoluble in excess NaOH. More than one cation can form a white precipitate; excess-reagent behaviour and a second test are required.
