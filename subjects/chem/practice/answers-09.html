(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 348 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=174 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.
(2) [5]
193.2 g ethanol is dehydrated with concentrated H₂SO₄ at about 170°C. Write the product equation, find the gas volume at STP and state one chemical test that distinguishes the gas from ethane. Ethanol=46 g/mol.
Worked solution / model answer
C₂H₅OH→C₂H₄+H₂O. n=4.2; ethene volume=94.08 L. Ethene decolourises bromine water by addition under ordinary test conditions; ethane does not without a substitution condition such as UV.
(3) [5]
25.145 g NH₄Cl is warmed with excess NaOH. Identify the gas by two observations, give the equation, and calculate its volume at STP. NH₄Cl=53.5 g/mol.
Worked solution / model answer
NH₄Cl+NaOH→NaCl+NH₃+H₂O. Ammonia has a pungent smell and turns damp red litmus blue; with HCl it gives dense white fumes. n=0.47 mol; V=10.528 L at STP. Do not deliberately inhale the gas.
(4) [5]
Atoms X and Y are in period 3, with 1 and 7 valence electrons respectively. X has mass number 23. Compare their metallic character, atomic size and electronegativity. Justify each trend across a period.
Worked solution / model answer
X is to the left of Y. Across a period effective nuclear attraction generally rises: atomic size decreases, electronegativity increases and metallic character decreases. Thus X is generally larger and more metallic, Y more electronegative. The supplied mass number does not reverse periodic position.
(5) [5]
NaCl is treated with concentrated H₂SO₄ below 200°C to prepare HCl. For 175.5 g NaCl, write the laboratory equation and find the theoretical HCl mass. Explain why the gas is not collected over water. NaCl=58.5, HCl=36.5 g/mol.
Worked solution / model answer
NaCl+H₂SO₄→NaHSO₄+HCl. n(NaCl)=3; HCl mass=109.5 g. HCl is extremely soluble in water, so water collection would absorb it.

A 8 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 61 cm³ of solid NaCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It does not conduct: its ions are fixed in the lattice. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.
