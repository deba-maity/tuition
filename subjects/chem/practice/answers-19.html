(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 208 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=104 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.
(2) [5]
An atom X has atomic number 8 and mass number 22. Write its shell configuration, identify its period, give valence electrons and predict its usual valency. Does changing its mass number alone change its group?
Worked solution / model answer
Shells=[2, 6]. Period=2; valence electrons=6; usual valency=2. Mass number alone does not change electron configuration or group; isotopes have the same atomic number.

(2) [3]
A 5 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 167 cm³ of dilute HCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It conducts: ionisation gives mobile ions. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.
