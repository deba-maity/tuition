Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 376 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=188 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.

(2) [3]
A 5 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 85 cm³ of dilute HCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It conducts: ionisation gives mobile ions. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.
(3) [4]
An atom X has atomic number 20 and mass number 32. Write its shell configuration, identify its period, give valence electrons and predict its usual valency. Does changing its mass number alone change its group?
Worked solution / model answer
Shells=[2, 8, 8, 2]. Period=4; valence electrons=2; usual valency=2. Mass number alone does not change electron configuration or group; isotopes have the same atomic number.
