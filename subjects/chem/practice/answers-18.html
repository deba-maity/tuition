(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 302 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=151 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.
(2) [5]
NaCl is treated with concentrated H₂SO₄ below 200°C to prepare HCl. For 134.55 g NaCl, write the laboratory equation and find the theoretical HCl mass. Explain why the gas is not collected over water. NaCl=58.5, HCl=36.5 g/mol.
Worked solution / model answer
NaCl+H₂SO₄→NaHSO₄+HCl. n(NaCl)=2.3; HCl mass=83.95 g. HCl is extremely soluble in water, so water collection would absorb it.
(3) [5]
219.3 g pure Al₂O₃ is completely electrolysed in the Hall-Héroult process. Calculate the theoretical aluminium mass. State the purpose of cryolite and why carbon anodes need replacement. Al=27, O=16.
Worked solution / model answer
One mole Al₂O₃ (102 g) supplies two mol Al (54 g). Aluminium mass=116.1 g. Cryolite lowers the working melting temperature and improves conductivity of the bath. Oxygen formed at the anode reacts with carbon, consuming the anode.
(4) [5]
252 g CuO reacts completely with excess dilute H₂SO₄. Write the equation, describe the visible change and find the mass of anhydrous CuSO₄ formed. CuO=80 and CuSO₄=160 g/mol.
Worked solution / model answer
CuO+H₂SO₄→CuSO₄+H₂O. Black copper oxide dissolves to give a blue copper sulphate solution. n=3.15; salt mass=504 g. CuO behaves as a basic oxide.
(5) [5]
A hydrocarbon is an acyclic alkyne with one triple bond and has 3 carbon atoms. Write its formula, calculate the H₂ moles needed to fully hydrogenate 6.2 mol, and name a suitable catalyst.
Worked solution / model answer
Formula C3H4. Each triple bond takes 2 mol H₂ per mol, so H₂ needed=12.4 mol. Use nickel under suitable heating conditions. A specific addition reaction such as bromine decolourisation tests bonding; colourless appearance alone reveals no bond multiplicity.

(1) [3]
Atoms X and Y are in period 3, with 1 and 7 valence electrons respectively. X has mass number 44. Compare their metallic character, atomic size and electronegativity. Justify each trend across a period.
Worked solution / model answer
X is to the left of Y. Across a period effective nuclear attraction generally rises: atomic size decreases, electronegativity increases and metallic character decreases. Thus X is generally larger and more metallic, Y more electronegative. The supplied mass number does not reverse periodic position.
