(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 302 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=151 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.

Check your work
Copper anodes replenish Cu²⁺ in copper refining; inert anodes do not supply replacement copper ions.

(2) [3]
A 2 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 58 cm³ of solid NaCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It does not conduct: its ions are fixed in the lattice. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.
(3) [4]
Two labelled 15 cm³ solutions contain Fe³⁺ and Cu²⁺ respectively. Give a stepwise NaOH/NH₄OH test to distinguish them. State observations separately for both and include the excess-reagent result where useful.
Worked solution / model answer
Fe³⁺: reddish-brown precipitate, insoluble in excess NaOH, insoluble in excess NH₄OH. Cu²⁺: pale blue precipitate, insoluble in excess NaOH, deep-blue solution in excess NH₄OH. Test separate small portions and compare the full pattern, not just the presence of a precipitate.
