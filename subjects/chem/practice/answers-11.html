(1) [5]
Acidulated water is electrolysed with inert electrodes. 334 cm³ hydrogen is collected at one electrode. Name both electrode products, calculate oxygen volume at the same conditions, write a cathode equation and explain why acid is added.
Worked solution / model answer

Identify electrolyte and electrodes. At the cathode reduction consumes electrons; at the anode oxidation releases electrons. Write charge-balanced half-equations.

Cathode H₂; anode O₂. H₂:O₂=2:1, so oxygen=167 cm³. Cathode: 2H⁺+2e⁻→H₂. Dilute sulphuric acid supplies ions and improves conductivity; it is not the intended decomposition product.
(2) [5]
A 13 V test circuit is connected to a cell containing 164 cm³ of aqueous NaCl. Predict whether it conducts and explain the charge carriers. Why is merely dissolving a substance insufficient to guarantee conduction?
Worked solution / model answer
It conducts: dissolved ions are mobile. Conduction requires mobile charged particles; a molecular solute can dissolve without producing ions.
(3) [5]
326.4 g copper is heated with excess concentrated H₂SO₄. Give the balanced equation, state the acid’s role, identify the gas by a chemical test and calculate its mass. Cu=64, SO₂=64 g/mol.
Worked solution / model answer
Cu+2H₂SO₄→CuSO₄+SO₂+2H₂O. Concentrated hot sulphuric acid acts as an oxidising agent. SO₂ changes acidified dichromate orange to green. Gas moles=5.1; mass=326.4 g.
(4) [5]
A sample of hydrated copper sulphate loses 225 g water and leaves 400 g anhydrous CuSO₄ on heating. Find x in CuSO₄·xH₂O and describe the colour change. Cu=64, S=32, O=16, H=1.
Worked solution / model answer
Moles CuSO₄=2.5; water=12.5 mol. Ratio=1:5, so x=5. Blue hydrated crystals become white anhydrous copper sulphate; adding water restores blue.
(5) [5]
An atom X has atomic number 3 and mass number 25. Write its shell configuration, identify its period, give valence electrons and predict its usual valency. Does changing its mass number alone change its group?
Worked solution / model answer
Shells=[2, 1]. Period=2; valence electrons=1; usual valency=1. Mass number alone does not change electron configuration or group; isotopes have the same atomic number.
