# ICSE 2027 Evidence and Revision Guide

Updated 2026-10-11. For the ten subjects confirmed by the teacher.


## How to use this guide

Read the Focus section first, then practise the original drills and inspect the answer checkpoints. Extra-practice candidates get additional attention only after core coverage. “Skip” means a defensible syllabus boundary or unnecessary extension, never a ban on last year’s topic.


## Evidence and limits

- 40 main examination papers: ten project subjects × 2023–2026. Improvement papers and the exceptional 2022 semester exams are not pooled.

- 372 MAIN question slots are inventoried and their start pages located by OCR or scan checks. Family tags remain provisional where indicated; every subpart, diagram and answer has not been manually verified.

- Evidence confidence is different from prediction confidence. No exact-question percentages, fixed rotations or “last year means skip” rules are used.

- English Literature title comparisons use only 2025–2026 because the prescribed texts changed. Hindi 2027 scope is filtered through the revised Class X list.

- Generated sets are revision worksheets with actual totals, not complete official board mocks. For full timed mocks, open a source paper and follow its printed choices/maps.

- Eight internally labelled CISCE 2027 specimens were read through mirrored text. Hindi and Computer Applications 2027 specimens remain unverified. Diagrams and entire draft marking schemes were not independently checked.


Official reference: [2027 syllabus hub](https://cisce.org/icse-regulations-and-syllabuses-2027/) and [revised prescribed books, April 2026](https://cisce.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/34.-Appendix-I-List-of-Prescribed-Books.pdf). Past-paper files are CISCE papers hosted by an independent mirror; that mirror is not the Council. Syllabus document text was read through mirrored CISCE documents when official downloads were blocked. Read the document’s CLASS X section, not the mirror’s marketing summary.


The manifest records links, page counts, PDF hashes, extraction status and unresolved headings. See [source manifest](research/source_manifest.json) and [question inventory](research/question_inventory.csv). Missing OCR headings and ambiguous diagrams have not been converted into precise frequency statistics.


## How priorities were decided

1. Filter to the 2027 Class X scope and the school’s prescribed books.

2. Separate compulsory breadth from choice questions: appearing as an offered question does not mean every candidate answered it.

3. Record question family, year and question reference; separate the old and new literature cohorts.

4. Treat repeated assessed skills as core practice. A gap can suggest an extra drill but supplies no calibrated probability.

5. Allocate more time to the student’s mistakes; rotate form/data/diagrams without fabricating question variants.

6. Do not infer a rotation from three unrelated algorithm examples. Check the whole programming section first.


## Test of the proposed “skip last year” rule

Computer matrix programs appeared in 2024 Q6 (diagonals), 2025 Q4 (norm), and 2026 Q5 (sparse matrix). Omitting matrices after either of the first two years would leave a blind spot in the next paper. Class applications and method overloading likewise occur in every reviewed programming section. This is a concrete counterexample, not a claimed statistical backtest of all subjects.


English 2026 MCQs include texts that were not long-question selections that year. Geography offers the same theory families. Recent appearance is insufficient evidence for exclusion.

## Planning revision time

A workable teacher starting plan is 60% core skills and error correction, 25% mixed timed practice using real papers, and 15% extra variations/candidates. These are time allocations, not probabilities. Adjust using each student’s errors.

For choice sections, prepare enough families for spare choices while retaining short-question coverage of the entire scope. In Geography, prepare at least six or seven theory units well; in Computer, practise at least five program families well and trace all listed theory skills. Neither strategy guarantees an easy set of choices.

## Why the Hindi title gaps need extra caution

The 2023–2026 papers drew literature selections from the earlier whole-book scope. The revised 2027 Class X list changes which texts are eligible. A title’s frequency in those four old papers therefore cannot be converted into its 2027 probability. The useful output is an eligible-text checklist, recent examples of task style, and extra practice for less recently selected eligible texts.


## English Language

**Observed paper format:** Q1 composition 20; Q2 letter 10; Q3 notice 5 + email 5; Q4 reading/summary 20; Q5 grammar 20. All five questions compulsory, with internal writing choices.

**Format basis:** 2027 specimen instructions read on a mirror and cross-checked against 2026 main paper.


**Focus:** Practise writing forms, linked notice/email, inference and concise summary, then verb forms, prepositions, joining and transformations. These are recurring skills across the four main papers.


**Extra practice / confidence:** Rotate narrative, descriptive, argumentative and picture tasks. No evidence supports predicting the next essay subject from last year’s theme.


**What to omit or reduce:** Do not memorise model essays or omit a grammar construction because it appeared in 2026. A topical AI/environment essay is a practice context, not a board prediction.

**Original practice questions and checkpoints**


### english1-001 — Narrative composition [20 practice marks]

Write 300–350 words about a time when admitting a mistake changed the outcome of an event. Develop a clear turning point.


Answer checkpoints: Original plot; consistent viewpoint/tenses; credible consequence; specific detail; concise reflective ending. Topic is a writing drill, not a predicted title.


### english1-002 — Descriptive composition [20 practice marks]

Describe a railway platform just before a delayed train arrives. Use 300–350 words and organise movement, sound and atmosphere.


Answer checkpoints: Select vivid details; coherent spatial/time sequence; avoid a list or a story with no description.


### english1-003 — Argumentative composition [20 practice marks]

“Schools should reserve one period each week for independent reading.” Argue for or against in 300–350 words.


Answer checkpoints: Clear stance, developed reasons/examples, acknowledge objection, coherent conclusion. Assess expression and reasoning.


### english1-004 — Picture composition [20 practice marks]

Choose a panel from the supplied local picture sheet and write a story or description directly connected with it in 300–350 words.


Answer checkpoints: State chosen panel; maintain a clear link to visible details; do not memorise a generic unrelated story.


### english1-005 — Formal letter [10 practice marks]

Write to the librarian requesting a weekly student reading club. Include purpose, proposed schedule and two practical arrangements.


Answer checkpoints: Sender address, date, recipient details, salutation, focused paragraphs, suitable closing. Use a consistent accepted format.


### english1-006 — Informal letter [10 practice marks]

Write to a friend who is disappointed after losing a debate, describing one helpful experience and practical advice.


Answer checkpoints: Friendly tone, address/date, salutation and close; specific support, not empty praise.


### english1-007 — Notice and email [10 practice marks]

Your school will hold an inter-house science quiz on 18 January at 10 a.m. in the hall for Classes IX–X. Write a notice inviting teams of three and an email inviting a neighbouring school’s science teacher as judge.


Answer checkpoints: Notice5 + email5; consistent event title/date/time/venue; eligibility/registration deadline; email subject and formal invitation. Set a sensible deadline before event.


### english1-008 — Verb forms [4 practice marks]

Supply verb forms: Yesterday Maya (visit) the library. She (look) for a book when the librarian (offer) help. By closing time she (choose) two books.


Answer checkpoints: visited; was looking; offered; had chosen. Accept contextually justified alternatives where appropriate.


### english1-009 — Prepositions [4 practice marks]

Fill the blanks: interested __ music; responsible __ the keys; arrived __ the station; divided __ two groups.


Answer checkpoints: in; for; at; into. Fixed contexts supply unambiguous meanings.


### english1-010 — Sentence synthesis [2 practice marks]

Join without using and, but or so: The road was flooded. The bus continued its journey.


Answer checkpoints: Although the road was flooded, the bus continued its journey. Preserve contrast and meaning.


### english1-011 — Sentence transformation [2 practice marks]

Rewrite beginning “No sooner”: As soon as the bell rang, the students entered the classroom.


Answer checkpoints: No sooner had the bell rung than the students entered the classroom.


### english1-012 — Voice and speech [2 practice marks]

Change to passive: The volunteers have cleaned the park. Change to reported speech: Ravi said, “I am ready.”


Answer checkpoints: The park has been cleaned by the volunteers. Ravi said that he was ready. Preserve tense/agreement.


### english1-013 — Reading and summary [10 practice marks]

The school library had a locked cabinet filled with old maps. Most pupils passed it without a glance. One afternoon, Tara asked whether the maps could be used rather than merely preserved. The librarian agreed, provided that the pupils handled them carefully. Tara and her classmates compared a map made fifty years earlier with a recent one. A patch of woodland had become a housing estate; a narrow stream had been drawn as a drain. At first, the pupils treated the changes as facts to copy. Then they interviewed residents who remembered the woodland. One described summer walks there; another recalled flooding after the stream was covered. The maps no longer seemed like silent sheets. They offered questions that a new map alone could not answer. The class created a display containing the two maps, short interview notes and a list of questions still unresolved. The librarian moved the cabinet key to the ordinary lending desk. She did not want careless handling, but she now believed that careful use could preserve the maps' value better than permanent isolation.

(1) Why did the librarian initially permit access only on a condition? [2]
(2) Give two changes revealed by comparing the maps. [2]
(3) What does “silent sheets” suggest about the pupils’ initial view? [2]
(4) What did residents add that the maps alone did not supply? [2]
(5) In no more than 50 words, summarise how the class made the old maps useful. [2]


Answer checkpoints: (1) She wanted careful handling to protect old maps.
(2) Woodland became housing; a stream was shown as a drain.
(3) They initially saw maps as static information, not prompts for questions about lived change.
(4) Personal memories and possible consequences, e.g. walks and flooding; accounts need further checking.
(5) Assemble comparison, interviews, display and unresolved questions; paraphrase without unnecessary background.


## English Literature

**Observed paper format:** Section A: 16 compulsory MCQs. Section B Drama: two 16-mark questions; C Prose: two; D Poetry: two. Answer one from each of B, C and D, plus one additional question from any of those sections: 16 + 4 × 16 = 80.

**Format basis:** 2027 specimen instructions read on a mirror and cross-checked against 2026 main paper.


**Focus:** Study Julius Caesar Acts III, IV and V and all ten prescribed Treasure Chest texts. For each, learn context, motive, change, theme and textual evidence. MCQs make selective chapter skipping unsafe.


**Extra practice / confidence:** Extra long-answer practice: With the Photographer, Haunted Houses and Act IV. They were not the long-question selections in 2025 or 2026. This is a weak coverage-gap inference from only two comparable papers, not a forecast. The 2027 specimen also demonstrates the six 2025 long selections; retain those texts alongside the gap candidates.


**What to omit or reduce:** Merchant of Venice and Treasure Trove are from the old cohort. Do not use their 2023–24 title frequencies to predict 2027. Keep all 2026 texts: With the Photographer and Haunted Houses already appeared in 2026 MCQs.

**Original practice questions and checkpoints**


### english2-001 — Julius Caesar Act III [8 practice marks]

How do Antony’s restraint before the conspirators and his later funeral speech reveal different strategies? Support with two moments.


Answer checkpoints: Context of assassination, permission/conditions, irony, crowd response; distinguish scenes III.1 and III.2.


### english2-002 — Julius Caesar Act IV [8 practice marks]

What causes the quarrel between Brutus and Cassius, how is it resolved, and what later choices show their differing judgments?


Answer checkpoints: Charges about corruption, emotional reconciliation, Portia’s death, military strategy; use Act IV evidence.


### english2-003 — Julius Caesar Act V [8 practice marks]

Compare Cassius’s and Brutus’s responses to defeat. How do misunderstanding and honour shape their final actions?


Answer checkpoints: Cassius’s mistaken inference about Titinius; Brutus’s choice and assistance; Antony’s final assessment. Keep chronology correct.


### english2-004 — With the Photographer [8 practice marks]

Explain how the photographer’s changes to the portrait develop the narrator’s conflict over identity.


Answer checkpoints: Contrast narrator’s request with alterations; tone, irony and ending; support with specific changes from the text.


### english2-005 — The Elevator [8 practice marks]

How do Martin’s fear and his father’s responses build suspense? Explain the effect of the ending.


Answer checkpoints: Claustrophobia, recurring encounters and dismissal of fear; open/suspenseful ending; avoid inventing a rescue.


### english2-006 — The Girl Who Can [8 practice marks]

Compare Nana’s view of Adjoa’s legs with what her achievement reveals. How does the child’s viewpoint affect the story?


Answer checkpoints: Contrast expectations and demonstrated ability; narrator’s limited understanding; change in Nana; textual evidence.


### english2-007 — The Pedestrian [8 practice marks]

How do the city’s emptiness and the automated police response develop the conflict between individuality and conformity?


Answer checkpoints: Leonard Mead’s walking, screens/absence of people, machine questioning and destination; link setting to theme.


### english2-008 — The Last Lesson [8 practice marks]

Trace Franz’s change in attitude to language and education during the lesson. Explain M. Hamel’s final actions.


Answer checkpoints: Initial reluctance, order banning French, community attendance, regret and final writing; language/identity.


### english2-009 — Haunted Houses [8 practice marks]

How does the speaker present the relationship between living people and those who have died? Explain two images.


Answer checkpoints: Presence of past occupants, continuity/memory, tone and imagery; use poem evidence rather than a generic ghost story.


### english2-010 — The Glove and the Lions [8 practice marks]

Explain the lady’s test, the lover’s response and the poem’s view of public display in love.


Answer checkpoints: Danger/public court, retrieval then rejection, king’s response; motive, dignity and irony.


### english2-011 — When Great Trees Fall [8 practice marks]

Explain how the poem moves from immediate loss to a changed sense of life after the death of a great soul.


Answer checkpoints: Natural images, emotional/intellectual disturbance and eventual renewal; connect title metaphor to human significance.


### english2-012 — A Considerable Speck [8 practice marks]

What does the speaker infer from the speck’s movements, and how does this change his response to it?


Answer checkpoints: Observed movement as thought/agency, choice to spare it, respect for intelligence; avoid asserting unobserved facts.


### english2-013 — The Power of Music [8 practice marks]

How do exaggeration and the final intervention make the poem comic? Explain two effects of the singer’s performance.


Answer checkpoints: Specific extravagant disruptions and goat’s intervention; comic irony/title; evidence from prescribed translation.

### maths-001 — GST [3 practice marks]

An intra-state article costs ₹2,000 before GST at 18%. Find CGST, SGST and the invoice total.


Answer checkpoints: CGST ₹180; SGST ₹180; total ₹2,360.


### maths-002 — GST [3 practice marks]

A GST-inclusive bill is ₹5,900 at 18%. Find taxable value and each intra-state tax component.


Answer checkpoints: Taxable value 5900/1.18 = ₹5,000; CGST = SGST = ₹450.


### maths-003 — Recurring deposit [3 practice marks]

₹1,000 is deposited monthly for 12 months at 6% p.a. Find interest and maturity.


Answer checkpoints: I=P·n(n+1)·r/(2·12·100)=₹390; maturity ₹12,390.


### maths-004 — Recurring deposit [3 practice marks]

A 12-month recurring deposit at 6% matures at ₹24,780. Find the monthly deposit.


Answer checkpoints: M=P[12+12·13·6/(2·12·100)]=12.39P; P=₹2,000.


### maths-005 — Shares and dividend [3 practice marks]

A person buys 50 shares of face value ₹100 at ₹125 each. Dividend is 12%. Find investment, income and yield.


Answer checkpoints: ₹6,250; ₹600; yield 9.6%. Dividend uses face value.


### maths-006 — Shares and dividend [3 practice marks]

Annual dividend is ₹960 on ₹100 shares paying 12%. Market price is ₹150. Find number of shares and investment.


Answer checkpoints: Dividend per share ₹12; 80 shares; investment ₹12,000.


### maths-007 — Linear inequations [3 practice marks]

Solve 3x−5<7 for integer x≥0 and list the solution set.


Answer checkpoints: x<4; {0,1,2,3}. Show the open boundary at 4 if using a number line.


### maths-008 — Quadratic equations [3 practice marks]

Solve 2x²−7x+3=0 by factorisation.


Answer checkpoints: (2x−1)(x−3)=0; x=1/2 or 3.


### maths-009 — Quadratic equations [3 practice marks]

A rectangle has area 48 cm² and length 2 cm more than breadth. Find its dimensions.


Answer checkpoints: b(b+2)=48; b=6 cm, l=8 cm; reject negative root.


### maths-010 — Ratio and proportion [3 practice marks]

If (x+2)/(x−2)=3/2, find x and state the excluded value.


Answer checkpoints: 2x+4=3x−6; x=10; x≠2.


### maths-011 — Remainder and factor theorem [3 practice marks]

Find k if x−2 is a factor of x³−3x²+kx+2, then factorise the polynomial.


Answer checkpoints: f(2)=0 gives k=1; (x−2)(x²−x−1).


### maths-012 — Matrices [3 practice marks]

For A=[[1,2],[0,3]] and B=[[2,0],[1,4]], find AB and BA.


Answer checkpoints: AB=[[4,8],[3,12]]; BA=[[2,4],[1,14]]; multiplication is not commutative.


### maths-013 — Matrices [3 practice marks]

If [[x,2],[3,y]]+[[1,0],[−1,4]]=[[5,2],[2,9]], find x and y.


Answer checkpoints: Corresponding entries: x=4, y=5.


### maths-014 — Arithmetic progression [3 practice marks]

An AP has first term 5 and common difference 3. Find its 20th term and sum of 20 terms.


Answer checkpoints: a20=62; S20=670.


### maths-015 — Geometric progression [3 practice marks]

Find the sixth term and sum of six terms of 3,6,12,...


Answer checkpoints: a6=96; S6=189.


### maths-016 — Reflection [3 practice marks]

Reflect A(−3,4) in the x-axis, y-axis and origin.


Answer checkpoints: (−3,−4); (3,4); (3,−4), respectively.


### maths-017 — Section formula [3 practice marks]

P divides A(2,1), B(8,7) internally in ratio AP:PB=1:2. Find P.


Answer checkpoints: P=((2·2+1·8)/3,(2·1+1·7)/3)=(4,3).


### maths-018 — Equation of a line [3 practice marks]

Find the line through (2,3) perpendicular to 2x+3y=6.


Answer checkpoints: Given slope −2/3; required slope 3/2; y−3=3(x−2)/2, so 3x−2y=0.


### maths-019 — Similarity [3 practice marks]

Corresponding sides of similar triangles are in ratio 2:3. Their areas differ by 45 cm². Find both areas.


Answer checkpoints: Areas 4k:9k; 5k=45; areas 36 and 81 cm².


### maths-020 — Loci [3 practice marks]

Construct the locus of points 3 cm from A and equidistant from intersecting lines l and m. State how to find points satisfying both conditions.


Answer checkpoints: Circle centre A, radius 3 cm; both angle bisectors of l,m; intersections satisfy both. Number depends on placement.


### maths-021 — Circles [3 practice marks]

ABCD is cyclic. ∠A=(2x+10)° and ∠C=(3x−5)°. Find x and both angles.


Answer checkpoints: Opposite angles sum 180°; x=35; A=80°, C=100°.


### maths-022 — Tangents and chords [3 practice marks]

From P, PA is tangent of length 12 cm. A secant meets the circle at B then C, with PB=8 cm. Find BC.


Answer checkpoints: PA²=PB·PC; PC=18 cm; BC=10 cm.


### maths-023 — Constructions [4 practice marks]

Construct triangle ABC with AB=6 cm, BC=5 cm and AC=4 cm, then its incircle. State how the centre and radius are obtained.


Answer checkpoints: SSS triangle; internal angle-bisector intersection I; perpendicular from I to a side gives radius. Retain construction arcs.


### maths-024 — Mensuration [3 practice marks]

A solid cone of radius 3 cm and height 8 cm is melted into spheres of radius 1 cm. Find the number of spheres, assuming no loss.


Answer checkpoints: Cone volume 24π; sphere volume 4π/3; 18 spheres.


### maths-025 — Trigonometric identities [3 practice marks]

For angles where both sides are defined, prove (1−cos θ)/sin θ = sin θ/(1+cos θ).


Answer checkpoints: Multiply left numerator/denominator by 1+cosθ; use 1−cos²θ=sin²θ. Do not silently divide by zero.


### maths-026 — Heights and distances [3 practice marks]

From a point 20 m from a vertical tower, the angle of elevation of its top is 60°. Find the height; neglect observer height.


Answer checkpoints: h=20tan60°=20√3 m≈34.64 m. Draw the right triangle.


### maths-027 — Statistics: graphs [4 practice marks]

Draw a histogram for intervals 0–10,10–20,20–40 with frequencies 4,8,12. State correct bar heights using a 10-unit standard width.


Answer checkpoints: Widths 10,10,20; adjusted frequencies 4,8,6. Heights must reflect frequency density.


### maths-028 — Statistics: ogive [4 practice marks]

For classes 0–10,10–20,20–30,30–40 and frequencies 4,8,12,6, form a less-than table and draw an ogive to estimate the median.


Answer checkpoints: Cumulative 4,12,24,30 at upper boundaries 10,20,30,40; include (0,0). N/2=15; interpolation estimate 22.5.


### maths-029 — Central tendency [3 practice marks]

Values 10,20,30 have frequencies 2,3,5. Find the weighted mean.


Answer checkpoints: Σfx=230, Σf=10; mean=23.


### maths-030 — Probability [3 practice marks]

One card is drawn at random from a standard 52-card deck. Find P(a king or a queen).


Answer checkpoints: 8 favourable cards; probability 8/52=2/13. Single draw.

A 2 kg body falls 5 m from rest. Neglect air resistance and take g=10 m/s². Find its kinetic energy just before impact and speed.


Answer checkpoints: KE=mgh=100 J; v=√(2gh)=10 m/s.


### phy-004 — Work, energy and power [3 practice marks]

A motor lifts 50 kg through 4 m in 10 s, g=10 m/s². If efficiency is 80%, find useful and input powers.


Answer checkpoints: Useful power=200 W; input power=250 W.


### phy-005 — Machines [3 practice marks]

A machine lifts a 300 N load using 100 N effort and has VR=4. Find MA and efficiency.


Answer checkpoints: MA=3; efficiency=(3/4)·100=75%.


### phy-006 — Machines [3 practice marks]

Draw a single movable pulley with one end of the rope fixed to the ceiling. Mark load, effort and tension. State ideal VR.


Answer checkpoints: Two supporting rope segments; ideal VR=2; neglect pulley weight/friction; effort half the load.


### phy-007 — Plane refraction [3 practice marks]

Light passes from air into glass of refractive index 1.5. If sin i=0.6, find sin r and state the direction of bending.


Answer checkpoints: sin r=0.4; bends towards normal.


### phy-008 — Total internal reflection [3 practice marks]

State both conditions for total internal reflection and explain why a ray going from air into glass cannot show it.


Answer checkpoints: Denser to rarer medium and incidence greater than critical angle; air-to-glass fails the first condition.


### phy-009 — Lenses [3 practice marks]

An object is 30 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 15 cm. Find image distance and magnification using Cartesian signs.


Answer checkpoints: u=−30, f=+15; 1/v−1/u=1/f gives v=+30 cm; m=v/u=−1, real inverted same size.


### phy-010 — Lenses [3 practice marks]

Draw rays for an object between F and 2F of a convex lens. Give position, size and nature of image.


Answer checkpoints: Beyond 2F; real, inverted, enlarged. Parallel ray through far focus; central ray undeviated.


### phy-011 — Spectrum [3 practice marks]

Which visible colour deviates most through a glass prism? Name radiation beyond violet and one use of it.


Answer checkpoints: Violet; ultraviolet; sterilisation or suitable fluorescence application.


### phy-012 — Sound [3 practice marks]

An echo returns after 0.8 s. Speed of sound is 340 m/s. Find the reflector’s distance and explain the factor 2.


Answer checkpoints: d=vt/2=136 m; sound travels outward and back.


### phy-013 — Sound [3 practice marks]

Distinguish forced vibrations from resonance. What happens when the driving frequency matches the natural frequency?


Answer checkpoints: Forced motion driven externally; resonance is large amplitude at matched frequency, with damping limiting amplitude.


### phy-014 — Current electricity [3 practice marks]

Resistors 6 Ω and 3 Ω are in parallel across 12 V. Find equivalent resistance, total current and branch currents.


Answer checkpoints: R=2 Ω; I=6 A; branches 2 A and 4 A.


### phy-015 — Electrical energy [3 practice marks]

A 1 kW heater runs for 2 h daily for 5 days. Find energy in kWh and cost at ₹8 per unit.


Answer checkpoints: 10 kWh; ₹80. One unit is 1 kWh.


### phy-016 — Household circuits [3 practice marks]

Explain why a fuse is connected in the live wire and why metal appliance bodies are earthed.


Answer checkpoints: Fuse disconnects live supply on excess current; earth provides low-resistance fault path and promotes protective disconnection.


### phy-017 — Electromagnetism [3 practice marks]

Describe how to induce current in a coil using a bar magnet. State two ways to increase the induced emf and how to reverse its direction.


Answer checkpoints: Change magnetic flux: faster motion/stronger field/more turns; reverse motion or pole. Stationary flux induces no current.


### phy-018 — Calorimetry [3 practice marks]

Mix 100 g water at 80°C with 200 g at 20°C in a vessel of negligible heat capacity. Find final temperature, with no heat loss.


Answer checkpoints: 100c(80−T)=200c(T−20); T=40°C.


### phy-019 — Calorimetry [3 practice marks]

How much heat melts 50 g ice at 0°C without temperature rise? Latent heat of fusion=336 J/g.


Answer checkpoints: Q=mL=16,800 J. Heat changes state rather than temperature.


### phy-020 — Radioactivity [3 practice marks]

A nucleus with mass number 238 and atomic number 92 emits an alpha particle. State the daughter’s two numbers, then the effect of beta-minus emission on them.


Answer checkpoints: After alpha: A=234,Z=90; beta-minus leaves A unchanged and raises Z by 1. Distinguish alpha/beta/gamma penetration.


### phy-021 — Electromagnetism [3 practice marks]

An ideal transformer has 500 primary turns and 100 secondary turns. Input is 230 V AC. Find output voltage and say whether DC can sustain this output.


Answer checkpoints: Vs/Vp=Ns/Np; Vs=46 V, step-down. Steady DC does not maintain changing flux; distinguish a switching transient.


### phy-022 — Lenses [3 practice marks]

Find the power of a convex lens with focal length 20 cm. State the sign for a concave lens with the same focal-length magnitude.


Answer checkpoints: P=1/f in metres; convex +5 D, concave −5 D.


### phy-023 — Application: lenses [3 practice marks]

A convex lens forms a sharp real image on a screen. The object is kept fixed, and the lens is replaced by another convex lens of shorter focal length at the same position. Explain whether the screen must move and in which direction, assuming the object is beyond both focal points.


Answer checkpoints: For fixed object distance U, v=fU/(U−f). A smaller f produces a smaller positive v, so move the screen towards the lens. Redraw a ray diagram; the old screen position is no longer in focus.


### phy-024 — Assertion/reason [1 practice marks]

A: Increasing the effective perpendicular distance from a pivot reduces the force needed for a given turning moment. R: Moment equals force multiplied by perpendicular distance. Choose (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.


Answer checkpoints: (a). For fixed moment, F=M/d; specify perpendicular distance.


### phy-025 — Application: specific heat [3 practice marks]

Equal masses of X and Y receive equal heat with no phase change or loss. X warms by 5°C and Y by 20°C. Find cX/cY and choose the material preferable for a low-heat-capacity calorimeter of equal mass.


Answer checkpoints: mcX·5=mcY·20; cX/cY=4. Choose Y, the lower specific heat capacity, so the vessel absorbs less heat for a given temperature rise.

**Original practice questions and checkpoints**


### chem-001 — Periodic trends [3 practice marks]

Compare atomic size and metallic character of sodium and chlorine in the same period, giving a reason.


Answer checkpoints: Na larger and more metallic; increasing effective nuclear attraction across a period reduces size and electron-loss tendency.


### chem-002 — Chemical bonding [3 practice marks]

Draw electron-dot representations of NaCl and CH₄. Explain why molten NaCl conducts but methane does not.


Answer checkpoints: Na transfers one electron to Cl; methane shares four pairs; molten NaCl has mobile ions, methane lacks them.


### chem-003 — Acids, bases and salts [3 practice marks]

Write equations to prepare copper(II) sulphate from copper(II) oxide and dilute sulphuric acid. Explain how excess solid is removed.


Answer checkpoints: CuO+H₂SO₄→CuSO₄+H₂O; add excess CuO to warm acid, filter; concentrate and crystallise filtrate.


### chem-004 — Acids, bases and salts [3 practice marks]

Dry HCl gas does not change dry blue litmus but moist blue litmus turns red. Explain and identify the ion responsible.


Answer checkpoints: Water enables ionisation; hydronium/H⁺ causes acidity. Dry gas alone does not provide aqueous ions.


### chem-005 — Analytical chemistry [3 practice marks]

Give the observations when NaOH is added dropwise then in excess to solutions containing Cu²⁺, Fe³⁺ and Zn²⁺.


Answer checkpoints: Cu: light-blue precipitate, insoluble excess; Fe³⁺: reddish-brown, insoluble; Zn: white, soluble excess. Name hydroxides.


### chem-006 — Analytical chemistry [3 practice marks]

A white hydroxide precipitate dissolves in excess NaOH and excess aqueous ammonia. Which of Zn²⁺, Pb²⁺ or Ca²⁺ fits? Explain.


Answer checkpoints: Zn²⁺; zinc hydroxide dissolves in both. Lead hydroxide remains insoluble in excess aqueous ammonia; calcium behaviour differs.


### chem-007 — Moles and stoichiometry [3 practice marks]

Find the mass and number of moles of CO₂ occupying 4.48 L at STP. Use molar volume 22.4 L/mol, C=12,O=16.


Answer checkpoints: n=0.2 mol; M=44 g/mol; mass=8.8 g.


### chem-008 — Moles and stoichiometry [3 practice marks]

A compound contains 40% C, 6.67% H and 53.33% O. Find empirical formula using C=12,H=1,O=16.


Answer checkpoints: Mole ratio 40/12:6.67:53.33/16≈1:2:1; CH₂O.


### chem-009 — Electrolysis [3 practice marks]

During copper electroplating of an iron spoon, state anode, cathode, electrolyte and electrode reactions.


Answer checkpoints: Cu anode; spoon cathode; aqueous CuSO₄; anode Cu→Cu²⁺+2e⁻; cathode Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu.


### chem-010 — Electrolysis [3 practice marks]

Explain why solid lead bromide does not conduct but molten lead bromide does. Give both electrode products and equations.


Answer checkpoints: Molten ions mobile; cathode Pb²⁺+2e⁻→Pb; anode 2Br⁻→Br₂+2e⁻.


### chem-011 — Metallurgy [3 practice marks]

Why are cryolite and carbon anodes used in aluminium extraction? State why the anodes require replacement.


Answer checkpoints: Cryolite lowers working temperature and aids conductivity/dissolution; oxygen reacts with carbon, consuming anodes.


### chem-012 — Metallurgy [3 practice marks]

In electrolytic refining of copper identify electrodes, electrolyte and what happens to insoluble impurities.


Answer checkpoints: Impure Cu anode, pure Cu cathode, acidified CuSO₄; insoluble impurities collect as anode mud.


### chem-013 — Hydrogen chloride [3 practice marks]

Describe a confirmatory test for chloride ions using dilute nitric acid and silver nitrate. Give observation and equation.


Answer checkpoints: White AgCl precipitate, soluble in aqueous ammonia; Ag⁺+Cl⁻→AgCl. Nitric acid avoids introducing chloride.


### chem-014 — Ammonia [3 practice marks]

Give the balanced Haber equation, catalyst and approximate operating conditions. Explain why ammonia is not collected over water.


Answer checkpoints: N₂+3H₂⇌2NH₃; iron catalyst, about 450°C and 200 atm; very soluble in water.


### chem-015 — Nitric acid [3 practice marks]

Write the equation for copper with concentrated nitric acid. State gas observation and why dilute acid gives a different major product.


Answer checkpoints: Cu+4HNO₃→Cu(NO₃)₂+2NO₂+2H₂O; brown NO₂; dilute commonly gives NO, which oxidises in air.


### chem-016 — Sulphuric acid [3 practice marks]

Give the Contact-process equation converting SO₂ to SO₃, catalyst and approximate temperature. Why absorb SO₃ in acid before dilution?


Answer checkpoints: 2SO₂+O₂⇌2SO₃; V₂O₅, about 450°C; direct water absorption makes difficult-to-condense acid mist; oleum is diluted.


### chem-017 — Organic chemistry [3 practice marks]

Write balanced equations for dehydration of ethanol to ethene and hydrogenation of ethene to ethane, with conditions.


Answer checkpoints: C₂H₅OH→C₂H₄+H₂O (conc H₂SO₄, about 170°C); C₂H₄+H₂→C₂H₆ (Ni catalyst, heat).


### chem-018 — Organic chemistry [3 practice marks]

An organic acid reacts with ethanol to form a sweet-smelling ester. Name the reaction and write it for ethanoic acid.


Answer checkpoints: Esterification; CH₃COOH+C₂H₅OH⇌CH₃COOC₂H₅+H₂O; warm with concentrated H₂SO₄.


### chem-019 — Organic chemistry [3 practice marks]

Draw and name the two structural isomers of C₄H₁₀. Distinguish structural isomerism from members of a homologous series.


Answer checkpoints: Butane CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃; 2-methylpropane (CH₃)₃CH. Isomers same molecular formula, different structure; successive homologues differ by CH₂.


### chem-020 — Organic chemistry [3 practice marks]

Write the equation for preparing ethyne from calcium carbide and state how bromine solution helps distinguish an unsaturated hydrocarbon.


Answer checkpoints: CaC₂+2H₂O→C₂H₂+Ca(OH)₂; unsaturated compound decolourises bromine through addition under suitable conditions.


### chem-021 — Assertion/reason [1 practice marks]

A: Aqueous sodium chloride conducts electricity. R: Its ions can move through the solution and carry charge. Choose (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.


Answer checkpoints: (a). Mobile ions carry current; this differs from electron conduction in a metal wire.


### chem-022 — Application: moles [3 practice marks]

Two pure gases have molar masses 28 g/mol and 44 g/mol. Equal 22 g samples are taken. Which contains more molecules, and what is the ratio of their numbers of molecules in the stated order?


Answer checkpoints: Numbers proportional to m/M; ratio (22/28):(22/44)=11:7. The 28 g/mol gas contains more molecules; equal masses need not give equal moles.


### chem-023 — Application: electrolysis [3 practice marks]

A student replaces inert electrodes in aqueous CuSO₄ electrolysis with copper electrodes. Describe the change in anode behaviour and explain why the blue colour is approximately maintained during normal operation.


Answer checkpoints: Copper anode dissolves: Cu→Cu²⁺+2e⁻; copper deposits at cathode: Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu. Ions removed at cathode are replenished at anode, approximately maintaining concentration. Inert anode instead evolves oxygen under the usual school conditions.

**Original practice questions and checkpoints**


### bio-001 — Cell cycle and division [3 practice marks]

Distinguish mitosis from meiosis by daughter-cell number, chromosome number and one function.


Answer checkpoints: Mitosis: 2 cells, same chromosome number, growth/repair; meiosis: 4 haploid cells, gametes. Do not confuse DNA replication with division.


### bio-002 — Cell cycle and division [3 practice marks]

Draw a labelled anaphase of mitosis in an animal cell with four chromosomes before division. Show the movement correctly.


Answer checkpoints: Sister chromatids separate at centromeres; four daughter chromosomes move to each pole. Label spindle, poles, chromosomes; avoid homologous-pair separation.


### bio-003 — Genetics [3 practice marks]

Cross Tt with Tt where T is dominant tall and t dwarf. Show gametes, Punnett square, genotype and phenotype ratios.


Answer checkpoints: Gametes T,t each; TT:Tt:tt=1:2:1; tall:dwarf=3:1.


### bio-004 — Genetics [3 practice marks]

Explain why the father’s gamete determines a human child’s chromosomal sex in the usual XX/XY model.


Answer checkpoints: Egg carries X; sperm X or Y; XX or XY. Do not attribute the outcome to the mother.


### bio-005 — Absorption by roots [3 practice marks]

Explain why a root-hair cell takes in water from moist soil and what could happen in concentrated salt solution.


Answer checkpoints: Water moves by osmosis across selectively permeable membrane; concentrated external solution can cause water loss/plasmolysis.


### bio-006 — Absorption by roots [3 practice marks]

Distinguish plasmolysis from deplasmolysis and state which experimental change reverses plasmolysis in a living cell.


Answer checkpoints: Protoplast shrinks away from wall in hypertonic solution; water entry restores it in hypotonic solution if cell remains viable.


### bio-007 — Transpiration [3 practice marks]

What does a potometer measure? State one limitation and predict the effect of increased wind speed under otherwise suitable conditions.


Answer checkpoints: Water uptake, a proxy for transpiration; some water used/stored; wind usually increases loss by removing humid air.


### bio-008 — Photosynthesis [3 practice marks]

Design a starch-test experiment showing light is needed. Include destarching, control comparison and reason for each leaf-treatment step.


Answer checkpoints: Destarch plant, mask part of leaf, expose to light; boil water then alcohol in water bath, rinse, iodine; exposed part blue-black, covered part not. No direct flame for alcohol.


### bio-009 — Photosynthesis [3 practice marks]

Why does a variegated leaf help test whether chlorophyll is necessary for photosynthesis? State the expected iodine result after suitable preparation.


Answer checkpoints: Same leaf shares conditions; only green parts produce starch and turn blue-black after destarching/light exposure.


### bio-010 — Plant chemical coordination [3 practice marks]

Name the hormone associated with cell elongation and explain positive phototropism of a shoot.


Answer checkpoints: Auxin; unequal distribution promotes greater elongation on shaded side, bending toward light.


### bio-011 — Plant chemical coordination [3 practice marks]

Match roles to hormones: fruit ripening, cell division, dormancy/stress response. Explain thigmotropism with an example.


Answer checkpoints: Ethylene; cytokinin; abscisic acid. Tendrils grow in response to contact. Distinguish growth response from simple movement.


### bio-012 — Circulation [3 practice marks]

Trace blood from the right ventricle back to the left atrium. State vessels and oxygenation changes.


Answer checkpoints: Right ventricle→pulmonary artery→lung capillaries→pulmonary veins→left atrium; becomes oxygenated in lungs.


### bio-013 — Circulation [3 practice marks]

Why is the left ventricular wall thicker than the right? Distinguish artery and vein by direction rather than oxygen content.


Answer checkpoints: Systemic circulation requires higher pressure; arteries carry away from heart, veins toward heart; pulmonary vessels are oxygenation exceptions.


### bio-014 — Excretion [3 practice marks]

Draw a nephron and mark Bowman’s capsule, glomerulus, convoluted tubules, loop of Henle and collecting duct. Distinguish ultrafiltration from selective reabsorption.


Answer checkpoints: Filtration from glomerulus into capsule under pressure; useful solutes/water returned to blood along tubules. Labels connected to the correct structures.


### bio-015 — Nervous system [3 practice marks]

Trace a simple withdrawal reflex from touching a hot object. Distinguish receptor, sensory neuron, relay, motor neuron and effector.


Answer checkpoints: Skin receptor→sensory neuron→spinal cord relay→motor neuron→muscle; immediate protective response, later conscious perception.


### bio-016 — Sense organs [3 practice marks]

Why does a myopic person have trouble seeing distant objects? Give image position and correction with a ray sketch.


Answer checkpoints: Image in front of retina; concave lens diverges rays so final image is on retina. Do not swap with hypermetropia.


### bio-017 — Endocrine system [3 practice marks]

Name the pancreatic hormone that lowers blood glucose and explain a consequence of its deficiency.


Answer checkpoints: Insulin; impaired glucose regulation/diabetes mellitus. Distinguish from diabetes insipidus.


### bio-018 — Reproduction [3 practice marks]

State the usual site of fertilisation and implantation. Explain two functions of the placenta and why maternal/fetal blood normally stays separate.


Answer checkpoints: Fertilisation in oviduct, implantation in uterine endometrium; exchange of nutrients/gases/waste across a barrier, not direct blood mixing.


### bio-019 — Human evolution [3 practice marks]

Distinguish Lamarck’s explanation of acquired traits from Darwin’s natural selection. Use a simple example without claiming individual need creates inherited variation.


Answer checkpoints: Lamarck: use/disuse and inheritance of acquired traits; Darwin: heritable variation and differential survival/reproduction across generations.


### bio-020 — Population [3 practice marks]

Define birth rate and death rate and explain how improved healthcare can initially increase population growth.


Answer checkpoints: Births/deaths per thousand population per year; death rate may fall while births remain high.


### bio-021 — Pollution [3 practice marks]

Explain how untreated sewage can reduce dissolved oxygen in a river and affect fish. Give two control measures.


Answer checkpoints: Microbial decomposition uses oxygen; aquatic life suffers; treat sewage and prevent discharge/monitor effluent. Do not confuse with direct photosynthesis oxygen use.


### bio-022 — Plant chemical coordination [3 practice marks]

State one effect each of gibberellins, cytokinins and abscisic acid.


Answer checkpoints: Gibberellins promote stem elongation/seed germination; cytokinins cell division; ABA inhibits growth/promotes dormancy or stomatal closure under stress. Match hormone to effect precisely.


### bio-023 — Human evolution [3 practice marks]

Arrange Australopithecus, Homo habilis, Homo erectus and modern humans in the usual school sequence. State two broad trends used in comparing human ancestors.


Answer checkpoints: Australopithecus→Homo habilis→Homo erectus→Homo sapiens sapiens; increasing cranial capacity and changes in posture/dentition/chin. This simplified textbook sequence is not a complete evolutionary family tree.


### bio-024 — Genetics [3 practice marks]

In the standard Mendelian dihybrid cross AaBb×AaBb with independent assortment and complete dominance, give the four phenotype classes and ratio. State which scope limitation applies.


Answer checkpoints: A_B_:A_bb:aaB_:aabb = 9:3:3:1. The listed Class X syllabus asks dihybrid phenotype, not the full dihybrid genotype analysis.


### bio-025 — Application: osmosis [3 practice marks]

Equal peeled potato strips are placed in pure water and concentrated sucrose solution. Predict each change in firmness, name the water movement, and state one variable to control.


Answer checkpoints: Pure water: endosmosis, increased turgidity/firmness; concentrated sucrose: exosmosis, flaccidity. Control initial strip size, time or temperature; water crosses a selectively permeable membrane down its water-potential gradient.


### bio-026 — Application: transpiration [3 practice marks]

Two similar watered plants are kept for one hour, one under a fast fan and one in still air, with equal light and temperature. Predict which usually loses more mass and give the mechanism and one experimental control.


Answer checkpoints: Fan plant usually loses more through transpiration because moving air removes moist air near leaves. Cover soil/pot with a water-impermeable cover to limit evaporation; use comparable leaf area.


### bio-027 — Assertion/reason [1 practice marks]

A: New cells produced during normal wound repair have the same chromosome number as the parent body cell. R: Mitosis separates duplicated chromosomes into two daughter nuclei. Choose (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.


Answer checkpoints: (a). Accurate distribution in mitosis maintains chromosome number; do not substitute meiosis.

For a normally functioning kidney, which blood vessel carries blood away with a lower urea concentration than blood entering it? (a) Renal artery (b) Renal vein (c) Pulmonary artery (d) Hepatic portal vein.
